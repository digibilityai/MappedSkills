# Source and Evidence Policy

## Objective
All strategic research should be reproducible and traceable.

## Evidence Priority
Prefer, where applicable:
1. official platform documentation;
2. first-party product/company material;
3. primary research;
4. reliable industry datasets;
5. credible specialist publications;
6. secondary commentary.

Use forums/social sources for sentiment and practitioner experience, not as sole evidence for high-stakes factual claims.

## Every Research Source Should Record
- URL
- publisher
- title
- publication date
- last updated date if available
- retrieval date
- claim(s) supported
- source type
- confidence
- notes
- whether the source is primary or secondary

## Numerical Claims
Never invent or estimate:
- search volume;
- keyword difficulty;
- rankings;
- traffic;
- conversions;
- revenue;
- market size;
- AI citation frequency;
- competitor performance.

If precise data cannot be obtained, label it as unavailable rather than fabricating a proxy.

## Search Research
For keyword/SERP research, record:
- query;
- geography;
- language;
- date checked;
- intent;
- observed SERP composition;
- leading domains;
- relevant SERP features;
- source/tool used;
- any uncertainty.

## AI Visibility Research
For each AI system, record:
- model/product;
- date;
- location/account context if relevant;
- exact prompt;
- response observation;
- MappedSkills mention;
- competitor mention;
- citations/links;
- source domains;
- methodology limitations.

Do not claim “rank #1 in ChatGPT” from anecdotal testing.

## Citation Integrity
Never fabricate a citation.
Do not cite a source for a claim it does not support.
Prefer direct evidence over paraphrased aggregators where possible.

## Fast-Changing Guidance
SEO, AI-search, crawler, structured-data, and platform guidance must be re-verified near implementation time because policies can change.
