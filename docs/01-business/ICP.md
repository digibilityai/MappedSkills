# Ideal Customer Profile — APPROVED AND FROZEN (Operational)

**Status:** APPROVED DECISION — frozen in Session 06 (2026-08-31).
**Authority:** `DEC-005` in `docs/00-project/DECISION_LOG.md`.
**Supersedes:** ICP A / ICP B / ICP C (the service-shaped hypothesis ICPs previously in this file). Those were segmented by which service a prospect would buy. This document segments by **whether MappedSkills can create economic value for them at all.**
**Governed by:** `BUSINESS_STRATEGY.md`.

**Purpose of this document:** a future team — sales, content, IA, copy, design — must be able to read this and determine whether a given prospect fits, without reopening strategy.

---

## 1. The one-line test

> **Does this business have buyers already looking for what it sells, is each qualified enquiry worth meaningfully more than the fee, can it act on and answer the enquiries it gets, and will it let us measure the whole path?**

Four yeses is a strong fit. Any hard no in §4 is a disqualifier regardless of the rest.

---

## 2. Strong-fit characteristics

All seven conditions are load-bearing. They are listed in priority order.

| # | Condition | What "yes" looks like | Why it is load-bearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Considered purchase** | Multi-step evaluation with a quote, specification, proposal, site visit or demo stage; a human closes the sale | Without it the enquiry is not the unit of value and the whole proposition collapses |
| 2 | **Meaningful value per customer / per opportunity** | A handful of additional qualified enquiries per month materially exceeds the fee | This is the arithmetic that supports a premium retainer. The single most important qualifier |
| 3 | **Identifiable existing buyer demand** | Some organic traffic, some acquisition spend, or a demonstrable search-demand family for what they sell | **MappedSkills improves capture and discovery; it does not invent a market.** Businesses needing demand created from zero are not the primary buyer |
| 4 | **A website or digital acquisition presence** | A live site that can be instrumented and improved; or existing paid activity | There must be something to capture demand *into* |
| 5 | **Ability to handle enquiries** | Someone follows up, in a reasonable time, and can tell a good enquiry from a bad one | Enquiry *quality* cannot be measured against a business that does not qualify or track. Without this the outcome is unverifiable |
| 6 | **Willingness to provide measurement access** | Analytics, site, search-console and enquiry-data access granted | **Non-negotiable.** Every outcome claim depends on it |
| 7 | **Sufficient commercial maturity** | Past founder-only survival mode; a marketing budget that already exists and is being spent somewhere; someone who can approve site changes and supply product knowledge | Recommendations that are never implemented produce no result and no case study. The sub-premium band is served by packaged low-cost competitors MappedSkills should not undercut |

---

## 3. Weak-fit characteristics

Not automatic rejections. Each is a signal that the engagement will be harder, slower, or lower-value — and that at least one condition in §2 needs explicit testing before proceeding.

- **Low value per customer**, where dozens of extra enquiries would be needed to justify the fee.
- **Very short, impulse or price-only purchase decisions** with no evaluation stage.
- **Demand that must be created rather than captured** — a genuinely new category, or a product nobody is searching for yet.
- **No functioning follow-up process** — enquiries arrive and sit.
- **Reluctance to share data**, "we'll send you a monthly summary instead of access".
- **No internal decision-maker** who can approve site or measurement changes.
- **Marketing decisions made by committee** with no single accountable owner.
- **Heavy existing in-house capability**, where MappedSkills would be a redundant layer.
- **An expectation of guaranteed rankings, guaranteed leads or guaranteed revenue.**
- **Price-first buyers** comparing against packaged low-cost SEO.
- **Ecommerce** — a different discipline with a different measurement model and a different unit of value.
- **Local B2C services** where the value per customer is too low for the fee.
- **Professional services** — treated as **weak-fit, not excluded**. *(Evidence note: Session 02 recorded professional services as the weakest reported AI-citation category, and it is a segment where MappedSkills' own weak-proof problem is mirrored in the client. That is a caution, not a disqualification. Professional-services firms with high per-client value and a genuine considered purchase — specialist consultancies, technical services, B2B advisory with large engagement values — can satisfy every condition in §2 and must be assessed on §2, not excluded by category.)*

---

## 4. Disqualifiers

A hard no. Do not pursue, regardless of budget.

1. **No demand to capture and none accessible** — the market is not searching and cannot be reached through capture-shaped channels.
2. **Refusal to grant measurement access.** The outcome cannot be measured, therefore it cannot be sold honestly.
3. **Demand for guaranteed outcomes** — guaranteed rankings, guaranteed AI citations, guaranteed lead counts, guaranteed revenue or ROI.
4. **No capacity or intention to respond to enquiries.** The engagement would produce enquiries that die.
5. **Value per opportunity too low** for a premium retainer to ever be rational.
6. **Requires claims MappedSkills cannot make honestly** — fabricated proof, borrowed benchmarks, invented figures. Project Rule 15.
7. **Illegal, prohibited or misrepresented offerings**, or businesses requiring deceptive acquisition practices.
8. **Pre-revenue with no budget and no data.**

## 5. Buyer roles

Who is spoken to, what they are accountable for, and what they need to hear. **These are the roles MappedSkills sells to; final page-level audience mapping belongs to the IA and content phases.**

| Role | Typically found in | What they are accountable for | What convinces them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Founder / Owner** | SMEs, manufacturers, owner-led firms | The whole number. Cash in, cash out | Plain arithmetic: enquiries, cost, and whether it paid. Low tolerance for jargon |
| **CEO / Managing Director** | Larger considered-purchase businesses | Growth targets and commercial risk | A defensible plan with stated assumptions and stated limits |
| **Marketing Head / Marketing Manager** | Businesses with a marketing function | Pipeline contribution and defending the budget | Measurement they can present internally, and not being made to look wrong |
| **Growth Leader / Digital Lead** | Businesses with digital maturity | Channel performance and efficiency | Method, instrumentation and honesty about attribution |
| **Commercial / Sales Leader** | Where sales owns the enquiry, common in industrial B2B | Enquiry quality and conversion to order | Enquiry *quality*, not enquiry volume. The qualification and routing story |

**Practical note:** in the beachhead segment the founder/owner and the commercial/sales leader are frequently the same person or sit one desk apart, and enquiry **quality** is the language that lands — not traffic, not rankings.

## 6. Business models

**In scope, in priority order:**
1. **B2B considered purchase** — the core. Traceable enquiries, high customer value, long cycles that make retainers rational.
2. **Manufacturing and industrial** — components, engineering services, industrial equipment, contract manufacturing, technical suppliers with dealer or export channels. *(Beachhead concentration; see §7.)*
3. **B2B services with high engagement value** — including professional services that pass §2 (see the note in §3).
4. **Considered-purchase B2C** where the ticket is high and the decision has an evaluation stage — a narrower case, assessed on §2 rather than assumed.

**Out of scope, and why:**
- **Ecommerce** — a different discipline, different measurement model, different unit of value.
- **Low-value local B2C services** — the arithmetic in §2 condition 2 does not close.
- **Pre-revenue startups** — no budget, no data, and demand often needs creating rather than capturing.
- **Enterprises with mature in-house teams and formal procurement** — MappedSkills currently has no credential that clears that gate. *(A proof-deficit constraint, not a permanent exclusion.)*

**Rule:** these are model-level defaults. **A prospect is assessed on §2, §3 and §4 — not on its industry label.** No industry is excluded by name except where a §4 disqualifier applies.

## 7. Vertical strategy

**APPROVED DECISION.** Horizontal proposition, vertical beachheads.

**Manufacturing / industrial B2B is candidate vertical beachhead #1. It is NOT the approved exclusive ICP, and MappedSkills does not become a manufacturing agency.**

**What "beachhead" means operationally:** it is where content, listings, outbound effort and case-study pursuit are **concentrated**. The proposition itself stays industry-neutral, so a good non-industrial client that passes §2 is not turned away.

**Supporting evidence (Sessions 03–04):** the cleanest commercial autocomplete family observed anywhere in the programme — eight variants, six explicitly provider-seeking, **zero contamination**; an open SERP held by small local agencies; a 3-year-old, 17-review specialist holding a Local Pack slot beside 486- and 507-review generalists; Pune's standing as a major Indian automotive and engineering centre; **all three tested AI systems using "RFQ" unprompted**; and a natural export bridge to international work.

**Adverse evidence, recorded so it is not lost:** **absolute search volume is UNKNOWN** — no keyword tool has ever been available in this programme, and this is the largest single risk to the direction; **no manufacturing case study exists**; manufacturing was the **least stable** AI prompt tested (roughly 2 of ~5 names recurring across runs); every observed entry route was a directory or a single listicle, **not vertical content**; and **healthcare has never been SERP-tested** despite an equally clean autocomplete profile, so the beachhead cannot yet be ranked against its nearest alternative.

**Three gates before any company-level commitment to manufacturing:**
1. absolute volume verification with a real keyword tool;
2. a healthcare SERP test on the same basis, so the choice is a comparison rather than a default;
3. one completed industrial engagement with a publishable result — **OWNER REQUIREMENT**; no Claude session can produce it.

**Second-beachhead rule:** do not open a second vertical until the first has three published results.

## 8. Geography

**APPROVED DECISION. India-first.**

- **Primary:** India, with Pune and the Maharashtra industrial corridor as the credibility base — observable demand, physical and geographic credibility, and genuinely open vertical and problem-led SERPs.
- **Bridge:** international-facing work **for Indian clients**. Indian manufacturers are themselves seeking export enquiries. This is a lower-risk route to international competence than direct international acquisition, and it strengthens the beachhead.
- **Later, gated, not scheduled:** direct international acquisition. **VERIFIED FACT: no international SERP and no international AI answer has ever been observed in this programme** — egress was Pune-locked in both research sessions. No claim about international winnability is supportable today, and **no international SEO or location strategy is to be created yet.**

## 9. What is deliberately NOT fixed

**No revenue band, headcount threshold or turnover figure is set.** There is no evidence to set one, and inventing one would violate the source policy.

**Problem sophistication and per-opportunity value segment better than turnover.** A ₹5 crore manufacturer with ₹15 lakh orders is a better fit than a ₹50 crore business selling ₹2,000 items.

Also not fixed here: final client-facing qualification scripts, lead-scoring thresholds, and sales-process design. These follow from this document; they are not approved by it.
