## 1. Executive conclusion

The market evidence supports the **problem** MappedSkills has identified and largely rejects the **category** it proposed to own.

The buyer's pain is real and independently documented: Pew Research measured Google users clicking a traditional result in 8% of searches carrying an AI summary versus 15% without, with only 1% clicking a link inside the summary. AI surfaces are genuinely live in India — Google's AI Mode since June 2025, now in Hindi and seven further Indian languages; OpenAI's CEO publicly claiming 100 million weekly active ChatGPT users in India by February 2026. Businesses are looking for help with this.

But "AI + Search Visibility" fails as a **defensible spearhead category** on five independent counts. Google's own documentation states that optimising for generative AI "is thus still SEO" and explicitly rejects the need for new files, markup, chunking or schema — the exact premises most GEO marketing sells. The measurement layer is a $29–$399/month commodity, with **free** AI-visibility checkers from both Ahrefs and Semrush. Google is nationalising the measurement through Search Console generative-AI reporting. Independent research shows AI brand recommendations are so unstable that identical result lists recur under 1 time in 100 — which invalidates the rank-style reporting the category sells. And most decisively, **two Pune agencies already occupy the position**: upGrowth with a published GEO offer ladder from a ₹5K–₹35K audit to ₹1.5L+/month retainers plus a named diagnostic tool, and SEOTonic — a twenty-year-old SEO firm — selling AI SEO, GEO, AEO, LLM visibility *and* CRO in packages from $199/month. In Bengaluru, DigiMark leads with the literal phrase "SEO, CRO and AI Search Growth."

The deeper finding is that **MappedSkills' constraint is not category selection at all.** Its production website publishes zero articles and zero case studies, the owner confirms no Contentful assets exist and that the contact form has not been generating enquiries, no analytics runs in production, and neither documented conversion path functions. Every competitor examined at a premium position leads with named clients, credentials, original research or a proprietary system. MappedSkills would enter *any* category with none of those. Choosing a better category changes the shape of the proof required; it does not remove the requirement.

The commercial sequence GET FOUND → CONVERT → AUTOMATE remains sound as a **customer journey**. It is unsound as a **three-pillar positioning**, because none of the credible agencies examined — in search, in CRO, or in automation — sells more than one of the three.

## 2. What the market evidence says

**Verified, and supportive of the underlying problem:**
- **Click loss is measurable.** Pew Research Center, 900 US adults, 68,879 searches in March 2025: 8% vs 15% click rate; 1% in-summary clicks; 26% vs 16% session abandonment; ~18% of searches produced an AI summary.
- **The surfaces are live in India.** AI Mode launched 2025-06-24, Hindi from 2025-09-08, seven more Indian languages by October 2025.
- **Usage scale is real.** Similarweb reports the generative-AI category growing ~70% year over year to 9.5 billion monthly visits, and ChatGPT's citation rate rising from 1.6% to 6.8% between June 2025 and May 2026. Sam Altman publicly claimed 100M weekly Indian ChatGPT users (a vendor claim, reported accurately).

**Verified, and adverse to the proposed positioning:**
- **Google disputes the premise on its own surfaces.** Guide last updated 2026-07-10: "optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO"; no new files, markup, chunking, writing style or schema required. Earlier appearance docs: "no additional requirements… nor other special optimizations necessary."
- **Measurement is commoditised.** Otterly $29–$489/mo (verified at source); Profound $99/$399/mo (verified at source); free checkers from Ahrefs and Semrush.
- **Google is absorbing the measurement.** Search Console generative-AI performance reports, June 2026.
- **The outcome resists the reporting the category sells.** SparkToro/Gumshoe, reported 2026-01-28: ~600 volunteers, 12 prompts, ~3,000 runs across ChatGPT, Claude and Google AI — same list twice under 1-in-100; same order ~1-in-1,000; yet leading names appeared in 60%–90% of responses for a given intent.
- **The position is occupied locally.** upGrowth (Pune), SEOTonic (Pune), DigiMark (Bengaluru), Qoulomb (Bengaluru/Indore).
- **The automation moat is structural.** HubSpot Elite requires 2,750 sourced points, 11,000 total points, 80% GRR, 100+ certified employees, and an invitation.
- **MappedSkills' own category is a weak AI-citation surface.** Similarweb reports Professional Services under 4% citation rate, against Travel ~23% and Automotive ~20%.

**Not established, and material:** the actual magnitude of AI referral traffic versus organic. Figures circulating in search results (a "0.13% of referral traffic" claim, an "11.4% vs 5.3% conversion" pair) were **not present on the Similarweb page retrieved** and are excluded from this analysis. Until resolved, **no MappedSkills claim that AI visibility drives revenue today is supportable.**

## 3. Current hypothesis

> **AI + Search Visibility → Conversion Rate Optimisation → Marketing Automation**
> Journey: **GET FOUND → CONVERT → AUTOMATE → GROW**
> Spearhead: **AI + Search Visibility**
> Line: *"Get Found. Convert More. Automate Growth."*

Recorded as `DEC-004` (Proposed / Not Yet Final).

### Evidence supporting it
1. **The problem is real and verifiable** (Pew; Google AI Mode in India; Similarweb category growth). MappedSkills would be selling against a documented change in buyer behaviour, not a fashion.
2. **Recurring work genuinely exists** across all three pillars — content and technical maintenance, ongoing measurement, conversion iteration, lifecycle workflows.
3. **The cross-sell logic is sound and felt by clients.** A business with more enquiries has a real follow-up problem. The sequence matches how the need actually arises.
4. **The journey is comprehensible.** "Get found, convert more, automate growth" needs no jargon — a genuine strength against a category full of acronyms.
5. **Some market validation of the shape exists.** DigiMark sells "SEO + CRO + GEO" as a headline with an 11-year track record; upGrowth sells a productised GEO ladder. The combination is sellable in India.
6. **The website can demonstrate the capability**, which is rare and valuable — and unusually credible given what the audit found wrong on MappedSkills' own site.
7. **AI-visibility work does lead naturally to conversion work**, because visibility gains are worthless to a client whose site cannot convert.

### Evidence against it
1. **Google contradicts the category's core sales narrative** on Google's own surfaces (guide updated 2026-07-10). A prospect can read Google's documentation and use it against the pitch.
2. **The words are free and already taken in Pune.** A twenty-year-old Pune SEO firm sells the whole stack from $199/month.
3. **The productisation is not novel either.** upGrowth's published ₹5K–₹35K audit → ₹4L sprint → ₹1.5L+/mo retainer ladder is the same offer architecture `OFFER_ARCHITECTURE.md` proposes.
4. **The measurement is a cheap commodity, and the free tier is owned by Ahrefs and Semrush.** An "AI Visibility Score" lead magnet competes with free.
5. **The headline outcome is hard to measure honestly.** Under 1-in-100 odds of a repeated identical brand list. Any score MappedSkills publishes must be variance-aware or it is indefensible.
6. **No credible agency examined sells all three pillars.** iPullRank and Rise at Seven: search, no CRO, no automation. Conversion and Speero: CRO, no search. Six & Flow: automation, no search. Breadth is how this market's commodity tier is organised.
7. **Marketing Automation cannot be defended as a pillar.** The moat is a platform tier out of reach; the entry tier is under the heaviest AI-commoditisation pressure of any option assessed.
8. **"Search Visibility" is used by no competitor and no buyer** — the absence is a cost of education, not an opening.
9. **The proof deficit is total**, and three pillars means three sets of proof to build simultaneously.
10. **Professional services is the weakest AI-citation category reported**, making self-demonstration slow.
11. **Organic cannot be the launch acquisition channel.** Category SERPs are owned by competitor-written listicles and directories; MappedSkills has no content and no authority. Any plan assuming the new site generates demand on publication is wrong.

## 5. Recommended spearhead category

**Recommendation (WORKING HYPOTHESIS pending owner decision and Session 03 evidence): keep discovery as the spearhead, but reframe it from a technique category to an outcome, and stop naming the acronyms in the primary proposition.**

Spearhead territory:
> **Getting found — in Google and in AI assistants — and turning that into measurable enquiries.**

Four properties make this defensible where "AI + Search Visibility" is not:

1. **It survives terminology churn.** If GEO is renamed or absorbed — which Google's own guidance already implies — the proposition is unaffected.
2. **It aligns with the platform owner instead of contradicting it.** Google says this is still SEO. Agreeing with Google, while being specific about which non-Google surfaces genuinely behave differently, is a position competitors would have to contradict their own marketing to take.
3. **It ends in a measurable business outcome**, not an unstable score. This matters directly given the 1-in-100 repeat-list finding.
4. **It carries conversion inside the promise**, so CRO is a component of one outcome rather than a second thing to sell.

**Naming decision deferred.** Session 02 recommends the *territory*, not the words. Category-term selection needs Session 03's buyer-language evidence — MappedSkills should use the term buyers search for, which is currently **UNKNOWN** and may well be "SEO" rather than anything newer.

## 7. What should NOT be part of the primary positioning

1. **GEO, AEO, LLMO or "generative engine optimisation" as headline language.** Google calls this "still SEO"; Pune competitors already sell the acronyms cheaply; `POSITIONING.md` already warns against jargon-led propositions, and the evidence supports that warning.
2. **"Marketing Automation" as a named third pillar.** Platform-tier moat unreachable; entry tier under the heaviest commoditisation pressure; bespoke delivery. Keep it as expansion.
3. **An "AI Visibility Score" presented as proprietary IP.** Build it for acquisition if useful; never claim it as the differentiator. It competes with free tools from Ahrefs and Semrush and with a free diagnostic already shipping in Pune.
4. **Any claim of guaranteed AI citation, AI ranking, or "rank #1 in ChatGPT."** Independent research puts identical repeat lists under 1-in-100. Also barred by Project Rule 8.
5. **"Performance marketing", "growth agency", "revenue growth agency", "RevOps", or "full-service digital marketing".** Red-ocean or uninformative language; three unrelated business models in the sample share the word "growth".
6. **Unsubstantiated numeric claims**, including the existing "300%+ ROI", "₹100Cr+" and "₹10Cr+" copy of unknown provenance now on the site (Rule 15).

## 8. Defensibility / moat requirements

Assume every competitor copies the words this week. On the evidence, they already have.

**Challenged WORKING HYPOTHESES — do not treat these as the moat:**
- **"AI Visibility Score"** — free equivalents from Ahrefs and Semrush; a free 4-minute diagnostic already exists in Pune. **Parity lead magnet, not IP.**
- **"Search Visibility Audit"** — every competitor offers an audit; upGrowth prices discovery at ₹5K–₹35K. **A sales mechanism whose only differentiator is its method.**
- **A named framework** — Liftmap, XOS, Relevance Engineering, FLAIR, The Growth Formula and Grove all exist. **Table stakes.**

**What MappedSkills would actually need to build**, in order of durability:

1. **A compounding benchmark dataset.** Repeated-run visibility measurement across defined Indian categories, accumulated quarter over quarter. A later entrant cannot buy the history, and history is what makes trend claims credible. **The only asset here that strengthens with time.**
2. **A published, variance-aware measurement methodology** — frequency across repeated runs, disclosed confidence, and a plain statement that "rank in AI" is not a real number. Factually correct in a market that is largely not; copyable only by competitors willing to devalue the dashboards they currently sell.
3. **Client evidence with method attached** — what changed, how it was measured, what cannot be attributed. Uncopyable because earned. **Currently MappedSkills' largest deficit.**
4. **Reusable instrumentation and attribution architecture** as a delivery asset. Unglamorous; hard to copy quickly; the precondition for every claim above.
5. **Vertical pattern libraries** — available only if the narrowing decision in §12 is taken.

**Not a moat:** offices, service breadth, years in business, tool subscriptions, or being early to a phrase.

## 9. Trust and proof requirements

**Starting position (VERIFIED FACT):** zero published articles, zero case studies, no Contentful assets to preserve (owner-confirmed), no enquiries from the contact form (owner-confirmed), no analytics in production, and neither documented conversion path working.

**How credible competitors establish trust (observed):** named clients with figures; third-party ratings and directory presence; institutional credentials (ISO 9001, ISO 27001, Google Premier Partner, HubSpot Elite); original research; a named proprietary system; a free diagnostic; transparent methodology.

**Minimum viable proof architecture**, ordered by speed to obtain, containing nothing MappedSkills must invent:

1. **The website as case study one.** Fix instrumentation, forms, conversion paths, structured data and Core Web Vitals — then publish the before/after with the method stated. Fully within MappedSkills' control, and unusually credible given the audit trail.
2. **A published methodology page**, including what cannot be attributed.
3. **Two to three real client case studies** with permission, method and honest limits. **OWNER REQUIREMENT — must be supplied. Nothing may be invented (Rule 15).** AnybodyCanBake and Digibility are flagged as candidates; publishability **UNKNOWN**.
4. **One original research asset** — the benchmark of §8.1, scoped small enough to ship and repeat.
5. **A working diagnostic tool** — parity, not IP; useful for acquisition and for feeding the dataset.
6. **Named authorship and visible expertise.** `/about` is currently not even linked from the header nav.
7. **Third-party directory profiles and ratings**, since Indian buyers demonstrably discover agencies through Clutch, GoodFirms and Semrush Agency Partners.

**Sequencing consequence:** items 1, 2 and 6 are achievable within the website transformation itself. Items 3 and 4 gate any premium positioning and are **not** solvable by Claude — they require owner decisions and client permissions.

## 10. Business-model implications

**Acquisition — the most commercially important finding.** MappedSkills cannot acquire clients through organic search at launch. No content, no authority, competitor-written listicles and directories owning the category SERPs, and professional services the weakest AI-citation category reported. **Organic and AI visibility are a 12-month-plus compounding engine, not a launch channel.** The launch period needs owner network, outbound, partnerships, directory presence and community — and the plan must say so instead of assuming the new website generates demand on publication.

**Entry offer.** A **paid** diagnostic, not a free audit. Every competitor offers a free audit, which prices the analysis at zero; paid entry also qualifies buyers, which matters given the owner-confirmed absence of enquiries from the current free-consultation model. Public anchor: upGrowth's ₹5K–₹35K discovery. **No price is recommended — out of scope.**

**Core engagement.** Recurring discovery-plus-conversion work on a shared measurement layer.

**Expansion.** Automation and lifecycle, sold once the enquiry volume makes the need felt rather than argued.

**Productisation.** Productisable: diagnostics, measurement methodology, reporting, technical playbooks, content systems, benchmark research. Resistant: bespoke CRM integration, enterprise experimentation programmes, anything needing deep client-system access.

**Delivery risk.** Senior-talent dependence on conversion research, technical SEO and analytics architecture. Client-side prerequisites: CRO needs traffic volume; automation needs CRM access. Concentration risk: a small agency selling three disciplines needs three competencies, and the credible players in this sample each chose one.

**Observed pricing anchors (recorded, not recommended):** upGrowth ₹5K–₹35K audit / ₹4L sprint / ₹1.5L+/mo retainer / ₹3L+/mo fractional CMO; DigiMark ₹25,000–₹2,00,000+/mo with plans from ₹39,999/mo; SEOTonic $199–$1,899/mo. **For contrast (VERIFIED FACT):** MappedSkills' current `/pricing` page displays ₹2,500 and ₹5,000 per month plans. **INFERENCE:** the repositioning implies an order-of-magnitude change in price point and therefore in ICP — a business-model change, not a messaging refresh. This deserves explicit owner attention.

## 11. Major risks

1. **Terminology churn** — GEO/AEO renamed or absorbed within 18 months; Google already calls it "still SEO".
2. **Google absorbing the measurement** — native generative-AI reporting in Search Console reduces proprietary-measurement value on Google surfaces.
3. **Tooling commoditisation** — $29–$399/month platforms plus free checkers from Ahrefs and Semrush compress what can be charged.
4. **Measurement credibility self-harm** — publishing an unstable score would damage the exact trust being built.
5. **Local competitors ahead** — upGrowth and SEOTonic in Pune with published pricing, tools and proof.
6. **The proof deficit** — every strategy here depends on closing it, and part of it is outside Claude's control.
7. **Weak AI-citation category** — professional services under 4%, making self-demonstration slow.
8. **Price and ICP discontinuity** — from displayed ₹2,500/mo plans to premium retainers.
9. **Owner capacity** — every proof asset needs sustained senior input.
10. **Over-narrowing too early** — a vertical chosen before Session 03's demand evidence could prove too small.
11. **Strategy-document drift** — `BUSINESS_STRATEGY.md`, `POSITIONING.md`, `ICP.md`, `SERVICE_ARCHITECTURE.md` and `OFFER_ARCHITECTURE.md` currently assert the unmodified hypothesis. ## 12. Questions requiring Session 03 search-market validation

1. What is the verifiable magnitude of AI referral traffic versus organic search? **The largest unresolved gap** — it determines whether AI visibility is a present-revenue or an insurance proposition.
2. What terms do Indian buyers actually search — "SEO agency", "AI SEO", "GEO", or a problem statement? The recommended positioning's naming decision depends entirely on this.
3. Is there independent commercial search demand in India for conversion optimisation, or does CRO only sell as an attachment to search?
4. Which verticals show the strongest intersection of search demand, AI-citation exposure and MappedSkills' credible right to serve — the input to the ALT-3 narrowing decision?
5. Do the highest-intent commercial SERPs in this category reward service pages at all, or are they structurally owned by listicles and directories? This determines whether organic can ever be a primary channel here.
6. Is there an Indian equivalent of the Pew click-loss evidence, or must the client-facing argument rest on US data?
7. What is MappedSkills' actual current search and AI-visibility baseline? None exists, and without it no improvement can be claimed.

## 13. Recommendation

## RECOMMEND MODIFY CURRENT HYPOTHESIS

The journey survives. The spearhead framing, the three-pillar structure, and the assumed sources of defensibility do not.

**Five specific modifications for orchestrator and owner review:**

1. **Reframe the spearhead from a technique category to an outcome.** Replace "AI + Search Visibility" as the primary category claim with getting found — in Google and in AI assistants — and turning that into measurable enquiries. Defer the exact category *term* to Session 03 buyer-language evidence.
2. **Promote CRO from third-listed value multiplier to co-lead inside the spearhead offer.** Its measurement is credible, its recurrence is strong, and it is the only pillar MappedSkills can prove immediately — on its own website.
3. **Demote Marketing Automation from pillar to expansion capability.** Retain it in the journey and in delivery; remove it from the headline positioning.
4. **Treat the proof architecture as the gating constraint, ahead of positioning.** No premium positioning is credible until items 1–4 of §9 exist. Items 3 and 4 require owner action and cannot be produced by Claude.
5. **Make a segment-narrowing decision a Gate 2 requirement.** Undifferentiated, geography-based positioning is already occupied in Pune at both the low and premium price ends. Narrowing by vertical or by buyer situation should be an explicit, evidence-led decision in Session 03/04 — not a drift.

**Also for the owner's explicit attention, since it exceeds a messaging change:** the recommended direction implies an order-of-magnitude increase in price point and a corresponding change of ICP, against currently displayed ₹2,500–₹5,000 per month plans.

**Claude does not approve this recommendation.** `DEC-004` remains Proposed. The orchestrator and owner decide whether to adopt, amend or reject it, and only then should `BUSINESS_STRATEGY.md`, `POSITIONING.md`, `ICP.md`, `SERVICE_ARCHITECTURE.md` and `OFFER_ARCHITECTURE.md` be revised — together, in one pass.
