## Evidence labelling used in this document
- **VERIFIED FACT** — read directly off the named company's live website on 2026-08-31.
- **COMPETITOR CLAIM** — a statement the company makes about itself (results, client counts, rankings). Recorded because positioning matters; **not** treated as true.
- **INFERENCE** — reasoned by this session from the observed evidence.
- **UNKNOWN** — not establishable from public material.

**Scope limit (VERIFIED FACT):** twelve competitors were examined by direct page retrieval. No competitor's revenue, retention, headcount, delivery quality, or actual client results were verified. This is a purposive sample chosen to cover dominant positioning, emerging positioning, and positions that *challenge* the MappedSkills hypothesis — not a market census.

## 3. Findings

### 3.1 The hypothesis is already occupied — including inside Pune
**VERIFIED FACT.** Three companies sell some or all of "AI/GEO + CRO" today, two of them from Pune:
- **upGrowth (Pune)** publishes a complete GEO offer ladder — paid discovery audit ₹5K–₹35K, four-week strategy sprint ₹4L, execution retainer ₹1.5L+/month, fractional CMO ₹3L+/month — plus a named free diagnostic tool ("Grove").
- **SEOTonic (Pune, est. 2005)** lists AI SEO, GEO, AEO, LLM visibility **and** CRO, packaged publicly at **$199–$1,899/month**.
- **DigiMark (Bengaluru)** leads with the literal phrase "SEO, CRO and AI Search Growth" and publishes ₹25,000–₹2,00,000+/month pricing.

**INFERENCE:** The MappedSkills hypothesis is not white space. Its *offer architecture* (diagnostic → build → ongoing) is not novel either — upGrowth already sells exactly that shape. The market has both a **low anchor** (SEOTonic, sub-$2,000/mo packaged) and a **premium anchor** (upGrowth, ₹1.5L+/mo retainers) in the same city. Entering between them with no published proof is the hard case, not the easy one.

### 3.2 The credible players do *not* combine all three pillars
**VERIFIED FACT.** Of the five international agencies examined, **none** offers search visibility, CRO and marketing automation together:
- iPullRank and Rise at Seven: search/AI visibility, **no CRO, no automation**.
- Conversion and Speero: CRO/experimentation, **no SEO, no GEO**.
- Six & Flow: automation/RevOps, **no search**.

**INFERENCE:** At the credible end of every market examined, **depth beats breadth**. The three-pillar model resembles the Indian mid-market pattern (upGrowth, DigiMark, SEOTonic) more than it resembles the specialists MappedSkills says it admires. Breadth is how this market's *commodity* tier is organised.

### 3.3 "AI visibility" is being absorbed, not established, as a category
**VERIFIED FACT.** Rise at Seven lists "AI Visibility & GEO" as one line among eight. SEOTonic — a twenty-year-old general SEO shop — lists GEO, AEO and LLM visibility alongside white-label SEO and ORM. Brainmine puts "AI Powered" in its headline with no AI-search service behind it.

**INFERENCE:** Three distinct absorption behaviours are visible simultaneously — **absorbed as a service line** (Rise at Seven), **absorbed as a price-packaged commodity** (SEOTonic), and **absorbed as a headline veneer** (Brainmine). A category showing all three at once is not emerging; it is being consumed. The terminology gives MappedSkills nothing a competitor cannot add in a week.

### 3.4 The tooling layer has taken the measurement business
**VERIFIED FACT.** Profound sells AI-visibility measurement from **$99/month** (Starter) and **$399/month** (Growth), explicitly serving agencies, and publishes its own industry benchmark ("Profound Index") (S-11). Otterly sells 15 tracked prompts for **$29/month** and 400 for **$489/month** (S-10). Ahrefs and Semrush both publish **free** AI visibility checkers (S-12, S-13).

**INFERENCE:** A MappedSkills "AI Visibility Score" delivered as a lead magnet would be **table stakes competing against free tools from Ahrefs and Semrush**, not proprietary IP. The measurement itself is a $29–$399/month commodity input. Any defensibility must sit in *interpretation, method and remediation*, not in the score.

### 3.5 Proof architecture is the real entry barrier
**VERIFIED FACT.** Every competitor examined that occupies a premium position leads with proof of a kind MappedSkills does not currently have:
- named clients with figures (Conversion, Speero, iPullRank, upGrowth, Qoulomb, DigiMark);
- institutional credentials (SRV Media: ISO 9001, Google Premier Partner ×4; Six & Flow: HubSpot Elite, ISO 27001);
- original research at scale (iPullRank's zero-click analysis, Rise at Seven's Category Leaderboard, Profound Index);
- a named proprietary system (Liftmap, XOS, Relevance Engineering, FLAIR, The Growth Formula, Grove).

**VERIFIED FACT (from Session 01/01B and owner statement):** MappedSkills' production site currently publishes **zero blog articles and zero case studies**; the owner has confirmed **no Contentful blog or case-study assets exist** to preserve, and that **the contact form has not been generating enquiries**.

**INFERENCE:** MappedSkills' binding constraint is not which category it picks. It is that it would enter *any* of these categories with no publishable proof, no research asset, no named method, and no platform credential, against competitors who have all four. Category choice changes the shape of the proof required. It does not remove the requirement.

### 3.6 The automation moat is structural and out of reach in the short term
**VERIFIED FACT.** Six & Flow's differentiation rests on HubSpot **Elite** partner status. HubSpot's own documentation (S-09) states Elite requires 2,750 sourced points and 11,000 total points (points accrue per $100 USD MRR sold/managed), 80% gross revenue retention, at least 100 certified employees, and an explicit invitation. Diamond requires 1,250 sourced / 3,750 total points and 75% GRR.

**INFERENCE:** In marketing automation, the durable differentiator is a **platform tier plus certified headcount**, accumulated over years. MappedSkills cannot buy or message its way to it. Without a platform anchor, automation work becomes bespoke integration — senior-talent-dependent, low-recurrence per project, and directly exposed to the large and growing population of low-cost AI-automation operators. Speero shows the credible alternative: fold it in as *data and growth engineering infrastructure* under a broader outcome, not as a named commercial pillar.

### 3.7 Elite CRO agencies impose a client-size floor
**VERIFIED FACT.** Speero states its ICP as marketing/product leaders at companies with **$50M+ in annual revenue**. Conversion's named clients are Meta, Microsoft, Domino's, HP, Clarks and Gousto.

**INFERENCE:** Credible experimentation requires enough traffic and conversion volume to reach statistical significance. That is a **hard structural constraint on ICP**, not a marketing preference. It means CRO-as-experimentation is only sellable to clients with meaningful traffic; for everyone below that threshold, "CRO" must be sold as *conversion design, research and diagnosis* rather than as a testing programme — a different, more defensible, and more honest promise.

### 3.8 The SERP itself shows how this market is acquired
**RESEARCH FINDING (qualitative SERP observation, no volume or difficulty data — Session 03 owns those).** Queries such as *"GEO agencies India 2026"*, *"best AI visibility agency"* and *"generative engine optimization agencies"* return SERPs dominated by **listicles written by GEO agencies and AI-visibility tool vendors ranking themselves and each other**, plus directories (Clutch, GoodFirms, Semrush Agency Partners, DesignRush).

**INFERENCE:** In this category the ranking asset is the *comparison listicle*, not the service page — and the incumbents already own that format. A new entrant's service page competes for a position that listicles and directories occupy. This has a direct consequence for launch: **organic acquisition in this category is a 12-month-plus play, not a launch channel.**

## 5. Where a gap does appear

Three observations point at genuine, if narrow, openings. All are **WORKING HYPOTHESES** requiring Session 03/04 validation.

1. **Measurement honesty is unclaimed.** Independent research (S-14) shows AI brand recommendations are extremely unstable — under a 1-in-100 chance of an identical list on repeat runs, roughly 1-in-1,000 for identical ordering — while leading names still appear in 60%–90% of responses for a given intent. Every tool examined nevertheless sells score-and-rank dashboards. **No competitor in this sample publishes a variance-aware methodology.** An agency that measures *frequency across repeated runs* and says plainly that "rank in AI" is not a real number would be saying something true that the category is not saying.
2. **Google's own guidance is an unused weapon.** Google states plainly that generative-AI optimisation "is thus still SEO" and that no new files, markup, chunking or schema are required (S-01, S-02). Most GEO marketing sells the opposite. An agency that aligns with Google's documented position, and is explicit about which surfaces (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude) genuinely *do* behave differently, occupies a position competitors would have to contradict themselves to take.
3. **Nobody in the Indian sample connects visibility to a working measurement and conversion layer.** DigiMark comes closest by naming CRO in its headline but reports analytics dashboards rather than automation; upGrowth's GEO metrics stop at "conversion rate by source". **INFERENCE:** the credible integration is *visibility measured honestly → conversion instrumented properly*, which is narrower and more provable than a three-pillar journey.

**Caution required.** None of these gaps is a moat by itself. Gaps 1 and 2 are *editorial positions* — copyable in a week by anyone who reads the same sources. They only become defensible if they are backed by a dataset and a published method, which is the subject of the defensibility section in `docs/01-business/STRATEGIC_VALIDATION.md`.
