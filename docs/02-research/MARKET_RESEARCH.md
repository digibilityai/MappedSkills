## 1. What the demand evidence actually establishes

### 1.1 The buyer's underlying problem is real and independently evidenced
**VERIFIED FACT (S-07, Pew Research Center, published 2025-07-22).** In a study of 900 US adults and 68,879 Google searches from March 2025:
- users clicked a traditional result in **8%** of visits where an AI summary appeared, versus **15%** where none did;
- only **1%** of AI-summary visits involved clicking a link *inside* the summary;
- **26%** of AI-summary visits ended the browsing session, versus **16%** without;
- roughly **18%** of searches in the sample produced an AI summary.

**INFERENCE:** the client-side pain — "my organic clicks are falling even though my rankings held" — is a measurable phenomenon, not agency invention. This is the strongest demand signal found in Session 02, and it is the honest basis for any AI-search proposition.

**Limitation (VERIFIED FACT):** the study is **US-only and reflects March 2025**. No equivalent Indian study was found. Its transfer to Pune/Mumbai buyers is **UNKNOWN**.

### 1.2 The AI surfaces themselves are real, large, and live in India
- **VERIFIED FACT (S-05):** Google launched AI Mode in India on **2025-06-24**, initially in English via Labs.
- **VERIFIED FACT (S-06):** AI Mode expanded to Hindi from 2025-09-08 and to seven further Indian languages, with Search Live, by October 2025.
- **RESEARCH FINDING (S-16):** on 2026-02-15, Sam Altman stated in the Times of India that India has **100 million weekly active ChatGPT users**, is ChatGPT's **second-largest market**, and has the largest student user base worldwide. This is a **vendor CEO's self-reported figure**, not an audited metric.
- **RESEARCH FINDING (S-08, Similarweb, published 2026-07-29):** generative-AI category visits grew ~**70% year over year to 9.5 billion monthly**; ChatGPT's share of that traffic fell from ~76% to ~53% (Jun 2025 → May 2026) while Gemini rose to ~27–28% and Claude to ~9%. Vendor estimate; methodology not auditable.

**INFERENCE:** the surface is not hype. But note what these figures establish and what they do not: they establish **usage**. They do **not** establish that businesses are **buying agency services** to be visible on those surfaces, and they do not establish the size of referral traffic reaching websites.

### 1.3 What is *not* established — and this matters
**RESEARCH REQUIRED.** The following were attributed to Similarweb in search-result snippets but were **not present on the Similarweb page actually retrieved (S-08)** and are therefore **not used** anywhere in this analysis:
- AI search as a share of total web referral traffic (a "0.13%" figure appeared in snippets);
- AI referral conversion rate versus organic search (an "11.4% vs 5.3%" pair appeared in snippets);
- organic search driving "345x" the traffic of AI assistants combined;
- Google AI Mode referral rates.

**This is a material gap.** Whether AI referral traffic is currently a rounding error or a meaningful channel determines whether "AI visibility" is a *present-revenue* proposition or an *insurance* proposition. Session 03 must resolve it. Until then, any MappedSkills claim that AI visibility drives revenue today is **unsupported**.

### 1.4 The commercial-upside signal for MappedSkills' own category is weak
**RESEARCH FINDING (S-08).** Similarweb reports ChatGPT's overall citation rate rose from 1.6% (Jun 2025) to 6.8% (May 2026), broken down by category: Travel & Hospitality ~23%, Automotive ~20%, **Professional Services under 4%** — the weakest band reported.

**INFERENCE (important, and uncomfortable):** MappedSkills' own category — professional services — is among the *poorest* surfaces for AI citation. Two consequences follow:
1. "We will win our own category in AI search, and that will be our proof" is a **slow and unreliable** proof strategy.
2. A MappedSkills client base concentrated in professional services would be buying into the weakest citation environment. Client verticals with genuinely high AI-citation exposure (travel, automotive, retail, healthcare) are a different ICP conversation than the one currently in `ICP.md`.

## 2. Category-by-category assessment

Classifications are **INFERENCES** from the evidence in the source register. They carry no numeric precision. Following the session brief, "few agencies use the phrase" is explicitly **not** treated as evidence of white space; buyer awareness, entry barriers, copyability, proof burden, pricing, recurrence and commoditisation risk are weighed instead.

### 2.1 Digital Marketing Agency — **RED OCEAN**
**Evidence:** MappedSkills' current position (title tag: "Performance Marketing Agency | 300%+ ROI"). Pune alone contains long-established full-service incumbents — SRV Media (five offices incl. Dubai, ISO 9001, Google Premier Partner ×4), Brainmine (nine locations), SEOTonic (since 2005) (S-25, S-26, S-27). Directory SERPs (Clutch, GoodFirms, Semrush Agency Partners, DesignRush) mediate buyer discovery.
**Assessment:** maximum crowding, price-comparison buying, no defensible language available. **This is the category MappedSkills must leave.**

### 2.2 Performance Marketing Agency — **RED OCEAN**
**Evidence:** ubiquitous in the Pune and India sample; MappedSkills' current service set (Google Ads, social ads, lead generation) sits here.
**Assessment:** additionally exposed to platform automation (Performance Max and equivalents) absorbing the optimisation labour that once justified the fee. Media-buying skill is being commoditised by the ad platforms themselves. **Leave.**

### 2.3 Growth Marketing Agency — **RED OCEAN, and vague**
**Evidence:** upGrowth ("Growth at the Intersection of Digital and AI"), Speero ("Growth Engineering Agency"), Six & Flow ("revenue-focused growth agency") (S-24, S-18, S-28).
**Assessment:** the word "growth" is doing no work — three companies with almost nothing in common all use it. Vague to buyers, invisible to search, and undifferentiating. **Not a viable spearhead.**

### 2.4 SEO Agency — **RED OCEAN, but with the strongest buyer comprehension of any category examined**
**Evidence:** Google's own documentation asserts that generative-AI optimisation "is thus still SEO" (S-01, updated 2026-07-10). SEOTonic packages SEO publicly at $199–$1,899/month (S-26).
**Assessment:** severe price compression at the low end and total buyer familiarity. **But** it is the only category in this set where the buyer needs no education and where Google's own guidance actively points. Its weakness is commoditisation, not comprehension — the opposite failure mode from GEO/AEO.

### 2.5 AI SEO Agency — **CROWDED BUT DIFFERENTIABLE**
**Evidence:** SEOTonic leads with "AI Backed SEO"; Qoulomb positions as AI-SEO specialists; iPullRank as an "AI Search Agency" (S-26, S-22, S-19).
**Assessment:** the phrase is already generic and covers two unrelated things — *using AI to do SEO* and *being visible inside AI*. That ambiguity is a liability in messaging but the underlying work is differentiable through depth. iPullRank shows how: query fan-out, passage retrieval, embeddings — technical substance a commodity shop cannot fake.

### 2.6 AI Search Optimisation / GEO / AEO — **CROWDED, RAPIDLY COMMODITISING, AND CONTESTED BY GOOGLE**
Assessed together because the market treats them as synonyms.

**Evidence for the classification:**
1. **Google contradicts the category's core sales narrative.** Google states (S-01, last updated 2026-07-10): "From Google Search's perspective, optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO." It explicitly busts the myths that new machine-readable files, AI text files, content chunking, special writing styles or special schema are needed. The earlier appearance documentation (S-02) states: "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary."
2. **Google is nationalising the measurement.** Search Console gained generative-AI performance reporting in June 2026 (S-04). Measurement of Google's AI surfaces is becoming a free, native feature.
3. **The measurement layer is a cheap commodity.** Profound from $99/month, Otterly from $29/month, and **free** AI visibility checkers from both Ahrefs and Semrush (S-11, S-10, S-12, S-13).
4. **Adoption is near-universal among incumbents.** A twenty-year-old general SEO shop in Pune already lists GEO, AEO and LLM visibility (S-26); a UK organic-media agency lists "AI Visibility & GEO" as one line among eight (S-21); a Pune agency ships a free AI diagnostic tool (S-24).
5. **The outcome is genuinely hard to measure.** Independent research (S-14, published 2026-01-28) found under a **1-in-100** chance of two identical brand lists on repeat runs and roughly **1-in-1,000** for identical ordering — while leading names still appeared in **60%–90%** of responses for a given intent.

**Assessment:** **not** an opening. The terminology is free to copy, the tooling is $29–$399/month, the platform owner publicly disputes the premise on its own surfaces, and the outcome resists the rank-style reporting the category sells. Point 5 cuts both ways: it destroys "we'll get you ranked in AI" while validating **frequency-of-appearance across repeated runs** as a legitimate metric.
**Where a real opening survives:** the non-Google surfaces. Google's guidance covers Google. It says nothing about ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude or Copilot, where retrieval and citation behaviour genuinely differ. That is a much narrower, more honest, and more technically demanding claim than "GEO".

### 2.7 Search Visibility — **TOO EARLY / BUYER EDUCATION HEAVY**
**Evidence:** not observed as a category label in any of the twelve competitors examined. Competitors use "AI visibility", "organic", "search", or "growth".
**Assessment:** the *absence* is the warning, not the opportunity. An abstract noun phrase no buyer types and no competitor uses means MappedSkills would fund the category education alone — and any competitor could adopt the term the moment it started working. Usable as *explanatory* language inside a proposition; **not** viable as the acquisition category.

### 2.8 Organic Growth / Organic Search — **CROWDED BUT DIFFERENTIABLE**
**Evidence:** Rise at Seven's "organic media planners… for SEO, Social, PR, AI and LLM search" (S-21).
**Assessment:** honest, durable, and terminology-proof — it survives the next renaming of GEO. Buyer comprehension is moderate. Weaker commercial urgency than a problem-led proposition, and it presumes the client already believes in organic.

### 2.9 CRO Agency / Conversion Optimisation — **CROWDED BUT DIFFERENTIABLE, with a hard client-size floor**
**Evidence:** Conversion positions as "the world's leading dedicated CRO agency" with Liftmap, the Lift Model and the Lever Framework and names Meta, Microsoft and Domino's; Speero states an ICP of **$50M+ annual revenue** with its XOS framework (S-17, S-18). In India, DigiMark puts CRO in its headline (S-23) and Tenet packages it as a 300+ point audit checklist.
**Assessment:** buyer comprehension is good, measurement credibility is far higher than for AI visibility, and recurrence is strong where a testing programme is genuinely running. **The structural constraint is decisive:** statistically valid experimentation needs traffic and conversion volume. Below that threshold, "CRO" honestly means conversion research, design and diagnosis — a different promise that must not be sold as testing. Proof burden is high but *achievable*, because results are attributable in a way AI-visibility results are not.

### 2.10 Marketing Automation Agency — **CROWDED BUT DIFFERENTIABLE — and defensible only through a platform**
**Evidence:** Six & Flow's differentiation rests on **HubSpot Elite** status plus ISO 27001 (S-28). HubSpot's own tier documentation (S-09) puts Elite at 2,750 sourced points, 11,000 total points (points accrue per $100 USD MRR), 80% gross revenue retention, **at least 100 certified employees**, and an explicit invitation; Diamond at 1,250/3,750 and 75% GRR.
**Assessment:** buyer awareness is decent and recurrence is real, but the moat belongs to platform partners with certified headcount accumulated over years. Simultaneously, the low end is under attack from a large and visibly growing population of low-cost AI-automation operators building on n8n/Make-class tooling (**qualitative observation; no count is asserted — see Source Register §D**). Delivery is implementation-heavy, platform-fragmented, and senior-talent-dependent.
**INFERENCE:** without a platform anchor, automation is a **capability**, not a pillar. Speero shows the credible framing: fold it in as data and growth *engineering* beneath a broader outcome.

### 2.11 RevOps — **TOO EARLY / BUYER EDUCATION HEAVY for MappedSkills' likely market**
**Evidence:** used by Six & Flow alongside HubSpot Elite status (S-28); absent from every Indian competitor examined.
**Assessment:** RevOps presupposes a buyer with separate marketing, sales and CS functions and a mature CRM. It is an enterprise vocabulary. `POSITIONING.md` already flags it as jargon to avoid, and the evidence supports that.

### 2.12 Demand Generation — **CROWDED BUT DIFFERENTIABLE (B2B only)**
**Evidence:** appears as a service under Six & Flow's RevOps; Qoulomb's B2B/long-sales-cycle framing is adjacent (S-28, S-22).
**Assessment:** strong commercial clarity to B2B SaaS and technology buyers, near-meaningless to local services businesses. Viable only alongside a hard B2B ICP decision.

### 2.13 Revenue Growth Agency / Revenue Marketing — **RED OCEAN (as language)**
**Evidence:** "revenue-focused growth agency" (Six & Flow); "turns into real revenue" (Qoulomb); "We improve revenue & deliver outcomes that matter" (Tenet) (S-28, S-22).
**Assessment:** every agency claims revenue. The claim is unfalsifiable at the point of sale and therefore carries no information. Attribution to an agency's work is exactly what buyers dispute. **Not a spearhead.**

### 2.14 Adjacent category identified in research: measurement / instrumentation
**Not in the brief's list; surfaced by the evidence.** No competitor in the sample leads with measurement integrity: honest instrumentation, attribution readiness, and a stated method for what can and cannot be attributed.
**Assessment:** **POTENTIAL WHITE SPACE as a *differentiator*, but not viable as a standalone category** — buyers do not shop for "measurement", they shop for outcomes. Its value is as the **proof layer beneath** whichever spearhead is chosen. It is also the one thing the SparkToro variance evidence (S-14) and Google's own guidance (S-01) both reward.

## 3. Interrogating the three proposed pillars

### 3.1 AI + Search Visibility as spearhead — the brief's ten questions, answered

| Question | Answer | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Understandable to buyers? | **Partly.** "Show up when customers ask AI" lands. "AI + Search Visibility" as a *category label* does not — no competitor and no buyer uses it. | S-21…S-28; no counter-evidence found |

| Can outcomes be measured credibly? | **Only with a variance-aware method.** Under 1-in-100 odds of an identical brand list on repeat runs; ~1-in-1,000 for identical order; but leading names appear in 60–90% of responses. Frequency is defensible; "rank in AI" is not. | S-14 |

**Category opportunity versus durable advantage — the distinction the brief demands:**
There **is** a category opportunity: buyer anxiety is real (S-07), the surfaces are real and live in India (S-05, S-06, S-16), and businesses are actively looking for help. There is **no durable competitive advantage in the category itself**: the language is free, the tooling is $29–$399/month, free checkers exist from Ahrefs and Semrush, Google disputes the premise on its own surfaces, and two Pune agencies got there first. **Opportunity to sell ≠ ability to defend.**

### 3.2 CRO — assessed in all four proposed roles

| Role | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Standalone spearhead** | **Viable but demanding.** Conversion and Speero define the proof standard, and Speero's stated $50M+ ICP shows the client-size floor. MappedSkills cannot meet that standard from a standing start. |
| **Second pillar** | **Recommended.** Its measurement is credible in a way AI visibility's is not; it multiplies the value of any traffic gained; and it is the pillar MappedSkills can prove *first and fastest — on its own website*. |

| **Integrated part of another offer** | **Strongest configuration.** "Discovery that produces measurable enquiries" is one promise, not two services. DigiMark already sells "SEO + CRO" as one headline (S-23) — the shape is validated; the differentiation must come from method and evidence. |

Against the required criteria: **buyer demand** good; **proof requirements** high but attainable; **delivery complexity** moderate and research-led; **recurrence** strong only above the traffic floor; **scalability** limited by senior-researcher time; **crowding** moderate; **measurement clarity** the best of the three pillars.

### 3.3 Marketing Automation — assessed

| Criterion | Finding |
|---|---|
| Buyer awareness | Moderate. Indian SMEs understand CRM and WhatsApp/email follow-up; "marketing automation" as a purchase is less established. |
| Implementation complexity | High. Integration-heavy, per-client bespoke. |
| Platform fragmentation | High. HubSpot, Zoho, Salesforce, WhatsApp Business API, n8n/Make-class tooling. Fragmentation blocks productisation. |

Note also the internal contradiction in the current documents: `SERVICE_ARCHITECTURE.md` proposes automation as a *standalone entry service where demand exists*, but the evidence says its entry-level segment is precisely where commoditisation pressure is heaviest.

## 4. Alternative strategies tested

Each alternative below is commercially plausible on the evidence gathered. None was written to satisfy a checklist.

### ALT-1 — Organic & AI Discovery Growth
*"We make you the answer — in Google and in AI assistants."* Search/organic is the spearhead; AI visibility is a **capability and measurement layer inside it**, never the category name. Aligns with Google's own published position that this is "still SEO" (S-01) while treating ChatGPT/Perplexity/Claude as genuinely distinct surfaces.

### ALT-2 — The Enquiry Engine (conversion-led)
*"You don't have a traffic problem. You have a conversion and measurement problem."* Lead with instrumentation, conversion research and enquiry-path repair; add discovery once measurement is trustworthy. Wedge is diagnosable in a week and provable in a quarter. **Note: this is the exact problem MappedSkills has just documented on its own site** — no analytics in production, a contact form transmitting nothing, an empty booking widget (`CURRENT_STATE.md`, findings 9 and 10), and an owner-confirmed absence of enquiries.

### ALT-3 — Vertical specialisation with full-funnel depth
Pick one or two verticals and own discovery-plus-conversion inside them. Moat is a **vertical benchmark dataset** and pattern library — the only asset in this analysis that competitors cannot copy by editing their homepage. SRV Media demonstrates the model works in Pune (education as anchor vertical, S-25). Requires the owner to give up addressable market for depth.

### ALT-4 — AI Visibility Benchmark & Remediation (research-led)
Publish a recurring, methodologically honest **India AI Visibility Benchmark** for defined categories — repeated runs, frequency reported rather than rank, variance disclosed (directly answering S-14) — then sell diagnosis and remediation off the back of it. Mirrors how Profound (Profound Index), Rise at Seven (Category Leaderboard) and iPullRank (large-scale original research) actually acquire attention (S-20, S-21, S-19).

### ALT-5 — Marketing Automation / RevOps-led
Lead with automation and CRM. Included for completeness and assessed as **weakest**: the moat is a platform tier MappedSkills cannot reach (S-09), delivery is bespoke and margin-poor, and the entry tier is under the heaviest AI-commoditisation pressure of any option here.

ALT-4 is best read as the moat-building programme underneath either, and ALT-3 as a narrowing decision that strengthens all of them.

## 5. What a real moat would require

Assume every competitor can copy the words GEO, AEO, AI SEO, AI Search, CRO and Automation **this week**. On the evidence, they already have. What remains?

**Discounted — copyable or already commoditised:**
- *An "AI Visibility Score" as the differentiator.* **WORKING HYPOTHESIS, challenged.** Ahrefs and Semrush give away free AI visibility checkers (S-12, S-13); Otterly starts at $29/mo and Profound at $99/mo (S-10, S-11); upGrowth already ships a free 4-minute diagnostic in Pune (S-24). A score is a **lead magnet at parity**, not IP. It is worth building for acquisition — it must not be claimed as a moat.
- *A "Search Visibility Audit" as the differentiator.* **WORKING HYPOTHESIS, challenged.** Every agency in the sample offers a free or paid audit; upGrowth prices its discovery audit at ₹5K–₹35K. The audit is a sales mechanism. Only its *method* can differentiate.
- *Naming a framework.* Liftmap, XOS, Relevance Engineering, FLAIR, The Growth Formula, Grove all exist. A name is table stakes; without a dataset behind it, it is decoration.

**Genuinely defensible, in order of durability:**
1. **A proprietary benchmark dataset that compounds.** Repeated-run AI-visibility measurement across defined Indian categories, accumulated over quarters. Copyable in principle, but a competitor starting later cannot buy the *history* — and history is what makes trend claims credible. This is the only asset here that gets stronger with time.
2. **A published, variance-aware measurement methodology.** Independent research shows AI answers are wildly unstable (S-14). A method that measures frequency across repeated runs, publishes its confidence intervals, and states plainly that "rank in AI" is not a real number would be **factually correct in a market that is largely not**. Copyable in a week — but only by a competitor willing to devalue the dashboards it currently sells.
3. **Client evidence with method attached.** Not "300% ROI" but "here is what we changed, here is how we measured it, here is what we cannot attribute." Uncopyable because it is *earned*. **This is currently MappedSkills' largest deficit.**
4. **Instrumentation and attribution capability as a delivery asset.** Reusable analytics, event and attribution architecture applied to every client. Boring, unglamorous, hard to copy quickly, and the precondition for every claim above.
5. **Vertical pattern libraries.** Only available if the ALT-3 narrowing decision is taken.

**Not a moat:** office locations, service breadth, years in business, tool subscriptions, or being early to a phrase.

## 6. Trust and proof — the minimum viable architecture

**VERIFIED FACT (current position).** MappedSkills' production site publishes **zero blog articles and zero case studies**; the owner confirms **no Contentful blog or case-study assets exist**; the owner confirms **the contact form has not been generating enquiries**; no analytics of any kind runs in production; neither documented conversion path functions (`CURRENT_STATE.md`).

**INFERENCE.** Against competitors who lead with named clients, third-party ratings, platform credentials and original research, MappedSkills currently has **no proof of any kind on its website**. This — not category choice — is the binding constraint on any repositioning.

**How credible competitors establish trust** (observed, S-17…S-28): named clients with figures; third-party ratings and directory presence; institutional credentials (ISO 9001, ISO 27001, Google Premier Partner, HubSpot Elite); original research; named proprietary systems; free diagnostic tools; transparent methodology.

**Minimum viable proof architecture for a repositioned MappedSkills** — ordered by speed to obtain, and containing nothing MappedSkills would have to invent:

1. **The website as the first case study.** Fix the instrumentation, the forms, the conversion paths, structured data and Core Web Vitals — then publish the before/after with the method stated. This is the only proof asset fully within MappedSkills' control, and the audit findings make it unusually credible: the agency that found and fixed exactly this on itself.
2. **A published methodology page** for how visibility and conversion are measured, including what cannot be attributed. Directly answers the credibility problem S-14 exposes.
3. **Two to three real client case studies**, with permission, method and honest limits. **OWNER REQUIREMENT — must be supplied by the owner. Nothing may be invented (Rule 15).** `ASSUMPTIONS_AND_OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` already flags AnybodyCanBake and Digibility as candidates; their publishability is **UNKNOWN**.
4. **One original research asset** — the benchmark of §5.1, scoped small enough to actually ship and repeat.
5. **A working diagnostic tool** — accepted as parity, not IP; useful for acquisition and for feeding the dataset.
6. **Named authorship and visible expertise.** Every credible competitor has a face; MappedSkills' `/about` page is currently not even linked from the header nav.
7. **Third-party signals** — directory profiles and ratings, since Indian buyers demonstrably discover agencies through Clutch, GoodFirms and Semrush Agency Partners.

**Explicitly excluded (Rule 15, and the audit's own finding):** the existing "300%+ ROI", "₹100Cr+" and "₹10Cr+" claims in `lib/constants.ts` have **unknown provenance** and must not be carried into a repositioned site without documented substantiation.

## 7. Business-model implications

Assessed for the strongest options (ALT-1 and ALT-2, with ALT-3/ALT-4 as narrowing and moat programmes).

### Acquisition
**INFERENCE, and this is the most commercially important finding in Section 7.** MappedSkills cannot acquire clients through organic search at launch. The evidence: no published content and no case studies exist today; category SERPs are owned by listicles and directories written by competitors (§3.8 of `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md`); and professional services is the weakest AI-citation category reported (S-08). Organic and AI visibility are a **12-month-plus compounding engine, not a launch channel.** The launch period requires owner network, outbound, partnerships, directory presence and community — and the plan must say so rather than assume the new website will generate demand on publication.

### Entry offer
A **paid** diagnostic, not a free audit. Free audits are what every competitor offers, and a free audit sets the value of the analysis at zero. Public anchor: upGrowth prices discovery at ₹5K–₹35K (S-24). Paid entry also qualifies buyers — relevant given the owner-confirmed absence of enquiries from the current free-consultation model. **No price is recommended here; pricing is not this session's scope.**

### Core engagement
A recurring engagement combining discovery growth with conversion improvement, on a shared measurement layer. Recurrence comes from content and technical work, ongoing measurement, and conversion iteration.

### Expansion
Automation and lifecycle work, sold **after** the client has more enquiries than they can handle — the point at which the need is felt rather than argued. This preserves the "GET FOUND → CONVERT → AUTOMATE" logic while removing automation from the headline.

### Productisation
Productisable: diagnostics, measurement methodology, reporting, technical fix playbooks, content production systems, benchmark research.
Resistant: bespoke CRM integration, enterprise experimentation programmes, anything requiring deep client-system access.

### Delivery risk
- **Senior-talent dependence** on conversion research, technical SEO and analytics architecture — the parts that cannot be templated.
- **Client-side prerequisites**: CRO requires traffic volume (S-18's $50M+ ICP is the extreme form of this constraint); automation requires CRM access and internal capacity.
- **Concentration risk**: a small agency selling three disciplines needs three competencies. The evidence says the credible players chose one.

**Pricing note.** No price is recommended, per the session brief. Recorded publicly observed anchors only: upGrowth ₹5K–₹35K audit / ₹4L sprint / ₹1.5L+/mo retainer / ₹3L+/mo fractional CMO (S-24); DigiMark ₹25,000–₹2,00,000+/mo with plans from ₹39,999/mo (S-23); SEOTonic $199–$1,899/mo (S-26). **VERIFIED FACT for contrast:** MappedSkills' current `/pricing` page displays ₹2,500 and ₹5,000 per month plans (`lib/constants.ts`). **INFERENCE:** the repositioning implies an order-of-magnitude change in price point and therefore in ICP — a far larger commercial change than a messaging refresh.

## 8. Risks

1. **Terminology churn.** GEO/AEO could be renamed or absorbed within 18 months. Any positioning anchored to the acronym inherits that risk. Google already calls it "still SEO" (S-01).
2. **Google absorbing the measurement.** Native generative-AI reporting in Search Console (S-04) reduces the value of proprietary measurement on Google surfaces.
3. **Tooling commoditisation.** $29–$399/month platforms plus free checkers from Ahrefs and Semrush compress what can be charged for measurement (S-10…S-13).
4. **Measurement credibility.** If MappedSkills publishes a score that behaves like the unstable "AI rank" metrics S-14 discredits, it damages the exact trust it is trying to build.
5. **Local competitors are ahead.** upGrowth and SEOTonic are in Pune with published pricing, tools and client proof (S-24, S-26).
6. **The proof deficit.** No case studies, no articles, no analytics baseline, no working conversion path. Every strategy in this document depends on closing it.
7. **Weak AI-citation category.** Professional services under 4% citation rate (S-08) makes self-demonstration slow.
8. **Price and ICP discontinuity.** Moving from displayed ₹2,500/mo plans to premium retainers is a business-model change, not a copy change.
9. **Owner capacity.** Every proof asset identified in §6 requires sustained senior input. The plan must be sized to actual capacity.
10. **Over-narrowing too early.** The mirror risk. A vertical chosen before Session 03's demand evidence could be a smaller market than assumed.
