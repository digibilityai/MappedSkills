## 0. How to read this — and how not to

### 0.1 No numbers, deliberately
**SEARCH VOLUME NOT VERIFIED · KEYWORD DIFFICULTY NOT VERIFIED · CPC NOT VERIFIED.** No keyword tool was available in this session.

Per the session brief, dimensions are scored **HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW / UNKNOWN** only. **No composite score, weighting or index is calculated.** Multiplying qualitative labels would manufacture precision that the underlying evidence does not support. The overall priority in the final column is a **stated judgement** with its reasoning attached, not an arithmetic result.

### 0.2 The six dimensions
| Dimension | What it means here | How it was judged |
|---|---|---|
| **Commercial Intent** | Would a searcher plausibly hire an agency? | Autocomplete intent composition (`KEYWORD_UNIVERSE.md` §0.3) |
| **Search Demand** | Is the query pattern well-established? | Autocomplete **depth and contamination**, never volume — volume is UNKNOWN throughout |
| **Ranking Feasibility** | Could MappedSkills realistically rank? | Observed SERP page types and ranking-domain identity (`SERP_RESEARCH.md`) |
| **Business Fit** | Can MappedSkills credibly deliver and prove this? | Session 01 capability evidence + Session 02 proof deficit |
| **Topical Authority Value** | Does it compound into a defensible cluster? | Whether the family supports depth without becoming a page grid |
| **AI Citation Potential** | Is it the kind of question AI systems answer by citing sources? | Query shape (question-form, comparison, data-seeking) — **not measured**; see §0.3 |

### 0.3 AI Citation Potential is an unmeasured judgement
**VERIFIED FACT:** no AI-visibility test was run in this session, and AI Overview presence could not be reliably observed. Every AI Citation Potential rating below is an **INFERENCE from query shape alone** — question-form and data-seeking queries are more likely to be answered with citations than transactional ones. **These ratings must be validated in Session 04 and must not be quoted as findings.**

### 0.4 Priority bands
- **PRIORITY 1** — pursue first; evidence is strongest and the route in is real
- **PRIORITY 2** — pursue after P1 is working, or once a specific gate clears
- **PRIORITY 3** — worth doing eventually; low urgency or low value
- **DEFER** — real demand, but blocked by SERP or by a missing prerequisite
- **REJECT** — evidence says do not pursue

## 2. What the matrix says at a glance

**Four PRIORITY 1 families, and they cohere into one proposition rather than three.**
Families 1, 2 and 3 describe the same buyer from three angles: an industrial B2B company that wants qualified enquiries (1), currently gets traffic without them (2), and is worried about being absent from AI answers (3). Family 5 (AI crawler access) is the technical spine underneath. **They are not a menu; they are one story.**

**The rejections are concentrated in the current hypothesis's own vocabulary.**
Families 15, 16 and 17 — GEO/AEO/LLM-SEO category terms, AI-visibility service terms, and "search visibility"/"organic growth" — are exactly the language `BUSINESS_STRATEGY.md` and `POSITIONING.md` propose to build on. Every one is REJECT on search evidence, for a different reason: education intent, tool intent, and prior ownership by other industries respectively.

**The two strongest opportunities are both gated on things Claude cannot produce.**
Family 1 needs a manufacturing case study; family 8 needs client benchmark data. Both require owner action and client permission. This repeats Session 02's finding: **proof, not positioning, is the binding constraint.**

**One uncomfortable result deserves the orchestrator's attention.**
Family 9 shows MappedSkills' *current* category has strong Pune search demand that MappedSkills is not capturing. The owner-supplied fact that the contact form produces no enquiries cannot be blamed on absent demand. The barrier is tenure, reviews and Google Business Profile — none of which a redesign fixes by itself.
