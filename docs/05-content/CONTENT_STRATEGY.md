## 1. What content is for, in this business

The approved commercial outcome is **measurable qualified enquiries**. Content exists to produce them, or to make them possible, and for no other reason.

Three programme facts govern every decision in this document, and none of them is optional:

1. **Organic is a 12-month-plus engine and cannot be the launch acquisition channel.** Three independent evidence bases (Sessions 02, 03, 04) say so. **No content asset in this strategy is expected to generate demand on publication.**
2. **The binding constraint is proof, not page count.** MappedSkills would enter any category today with zero published case studies, zero published articles, no analytics baseline, no directory profile, no surfaced Google Business Profile and zero appearances across 28 unbranded AI runs — while displaying ₹50K–₹2L/month in production.
3. **A material part of search and AI-discovery authority is off-site and cannot be created through owned-site publishing alone.** Directory profiles, reviews, Google Business Profile and third-party listings gate the category SERPs, the Local Pack and the AI answer layer simultaneously — **directory sources appeared in 15 of 28 unbranded AI runs and MappedSkills is on none**. Content does not substitute for them (§16, `AI_CITATION_CONTENT_PRINCIPLES.md` §6).

## 2. The content principle

**Every asset must do at least one of seven jobs, well, and must be able to say which.**

| # | Job | What "doing it well" means here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Search demand capture** | Targets a **named query family with recorded Session 03 evidence** and an assigned page in `SEARCH_ARCHITECTURE.md` §2 |
| 2 | **Buyer education** | Advances a commercially relevant decision for a `ICP.md` §5 buyer role, in that role's own language |
| 3 | **Authority** | Demonstrates a capability MappedSkills can actually perform and evidence today |
| 4 | **Source / citation value** | Is worth referencing by a third party because it contains something not available elsewhere |
| 5 | **Proof** | Shows method, first-party measurement or a real outcome — with sample, date, location and limits attached |
| 6 | **Conversion support** | Helps an evaluator decide whether MappedSkills is credible and relevant, without becoming an advert |
| 7 | **Digital PR / linkability** | Gives a third party a legitimate, non-transactional reason to reference MappedSkills |

**The publish test.** If an asset cannot name its job, name its evidence, and name the page or decision it supports — **it is not published.** "It is a good topic" and "a keyword exists" are not jobs.

**A second test, specific to this business.** If the honest answer to *"what does this contain that a reader could not get from the pages already ranking?"* is nothing, the asset fails §5 and does not publish, regardless of how well it is written.

## 3. Content is not word count

**No word-count target appears anywhere in this strategy, and none may be introduced.**

Format and length are determined from: user intent · observed SERP shape · available information gain · available evidence · buyer usefulness · conversion role. A 700-word evidence-backed diagnostic with a reproducible protocol is a stronger asset for MappedSkills than a 3,000-word restatement of the ranking pages, and on the one SERP where MappedSkills is genuinely competitive (`why your website gets traffic but no leads`) the ranking incumbents are **ordinary small-agency posts**, not long-form institutional content.

**The constraint that replaces length:** every asset must be complete enough to be **useful without hiring anyone**. That is a completeness test, not a length test.

## 5. The information gain standard

**Operational definition for MappedSkills:**

> An asset has information gain when it contains **at least one thing a reader cannot obtain from the pages currently ranking for the same intent** — and that thing is either a first-party observation, a first-party measurement, a disclosed method, or a reusable procedure.

### The five permitted sources of information gain

1. **First-party measurement.** Numbers MappedSkills produced itself, with sample size, date, location, method and run counts attached. The 39-run AI baseline is the launch example.
2. **First-party diagnosis.** Real defects found on a real site — MappedSkills' own, or a client's with permission — including the ones that are unflattering.
3. **Disclosed method.** A protocol a reader can run themselves and get their own answer. This is the strongest available form for MappedSkills today, because it requires no client and no budget.
4. **Expert interpretation of primary sources.** Reading the platform documentation properly and stating what it does and does not say — including where it contradicts the market's sales narrative.
5. **A reusable artefact.** A checklist, decision tree or verification sequence the reader keeps, derived from work actually done.

### What does not count

- Paraphrasing the pages that rank.
- Summarising official documentation without interpretation or a stated consequence.
- Restating another company's published figures as though they were MappedSkills' finding.
- A list of tips with no source behind any tip.
- Volume — more sections is not more gain.

### The failure test, written for editorial QA

Delete every sentence that could have been written by someone who had not done the work. **If the asset still says the same thing, it has no information gain and does not publish.**

### One honest limit on this standard

**Information gain is a quality standard, not a distribution mechanism.** Session 04 looked for research assets being cited in 28 unbranded AI runs and **found none**. Original work earns authority, sales credibility and client trust; on the evidence available it does **not** reliably earn AI citations for agency prompts in this market. Fund it for the former (`POSITIONING.md` §8).

## 6A. Research and editorial separation — the canonical-research rule

**Binding, not advisory.** It exists because the most likely way this content programme degrades is by turning one piece of first-party work into several search pages.

> **An original first-party dataset or measured study has exactly one canonical research URL.**
>
> Editorial content may interpret or apply that research **only where it serves a materially different user intent.**

### What an editorial article may NOT do

An article must not reproduce substantially the same **dataset · methodology · findings · charts · conclusion** as its research source, in order to create another search page. Publishing the same work twice does not double its reach; it splits its authority, confuses which URL is the reference, and produces two weaker assets where one strong one existed.

### What an editorial article must do instead

1. **Summarise only what is necessary** for its own argument to stand.
2. **Link to the canonical research** as the source of the dataset and the method.
3. **Add materially different application or interpretation** — a different question, a different audience, a different decision being made.

### The test, at brief stage

> If the article were published and the research entry were deleted, **would the article still have a distinct reason to exist?** If the answer is no, the article is a duplicate and it does not proceed.

### Applied at launch

| Canonical research | Editorial that may reference it | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **`/research/{ai-visibility-baseline}`** — the canonical home for the Session 04 measured dataset, method, findings, charts and limitations | **Launch article 1 only** | The article answers *"why is my business not appearing, and what do I do"*. **It does not republish the dataset, the run-by-run record, the charts or the methodology** |
| **`/research/{own-site-enquiry-diagnostic}`** | Launch article 3, and the problem page as a worked example | The article is a **verification procedure**; the research entry is the **record of what was found and fixed** |

**Recorded consequence of this rule during the Session 09 correction pass:** a proposed second AI article built on the same 28-run dataset was **withdrawn** and replaced, because the dataset belongs to the research asset. See `EDITORIAL_STRATEGY.md` §2A.

## 8. Internal linking — content-driven

**Principle: links follow buyer movement.** No mechanical SEO linking, no footer link farm, no fixed link quotas per article.

### What every asset must carry

| Asset type | Must link to |
|---|---|
| **Editorial article** | One relevant commercial or problem destination · one adjacent asset in the same cluster · **the canonical research asset its claims rest on, linked rather than reproduced (§6A)** |
| **Research entry** | `/how-it-works` (method) · at most **one or two** contextual commercial links. **It must remain useful to a reader who never becomes a client** |
| **Problem page** | The relevant commercial pages · `/how-it-works` as an offer · the own-site diagnostic as a worked example |
| **Commercial page** | Up to `/services` · across to the matching problem page · **down to the evidence supporting its own claims** |

**The rule that carries the proof deficit:** a commercial page that makes a claim must link to the asset that supports it. A claim with no linked evidence is either removed or reclassified as opinion (`CLAIMS_AND_EVIDENCE_GOVERNANCE.md` §3).

**Prohibited:** proof as a required step between a commercial page and the conversion; reciprocal-link schemes; hub-and-spoke structures built for crawlers rather than readers; links to `/industries/manufacturing` or its article from any unconditional page in a way that breaks if the gate does not clear; **and an editorial asset that duplicates its canonical research rather than linking to it (§6A)**.

### Anchor-text principles (no exact anchor copy is written here)

1. Describe the **destination**, not the keyword target.
2. Vary naturally; identical repeated anchors across a site of this size read as manipulation and help nobody.
3. Never anchor on a claim the destination does not substantiate.
4. Never use "click here", and never use an anchor whose promise is larger than the page it leads to.

## 9. Content structure — for human and machine readability

Applies to every published asset.

1. **Answer the question the reader arrived with, near the top, in plain language.** Then justify it.
2. **Headings describe the content beneath them.** A reader scanning only the headings should get the argument.
3. **Sections are self-contained** — a section lifted out of context should still be accurate. This is the single most useful structural property for both a skim-reader and a retrieval system.
4. **Tables only where the content is genuinely tabular.** A table used for layout is worse than a paragraph.
5. **Concise definitions where a term is unavoidable** — one or two sentences, in place, not a glossary page.
6. **A visible method block** on any asset containing a number: what was done, when, where, how many runs, what was not covered.
7. **A visible limitations block** on any asset making a measured claim.
8. **Source notes at the point of the claim**, not only in a bibliography.
9. **Visible authorship and dates** — published and genuinely updated, never a cosmetic date change (§14).
10. **Related content at the end**, chosen for the reader, not for a link graph.

**Do not turn every page into an FAQ.** An FAQ section is justified where real recurring questions exist and the answers are genuinely short. It is not a structural default, and FAQ rich-result eligibility must be re-verified at implementation (Project Rule 19).

## 11. Entity and trust content

Session 04 established that MappedSkills has **working branded retrieval and zero unbranded discovery**, and that branded AI answers are assembled almost entirely from **first-party sources**. Whatever the site asserts is what those systems repeat — which makes entity content a correctness problem, not a marketing one.

| Surface | Content requirement |
|---|---|
| **`/about`** | Named people, real roles, real expertise, real history. Company name used consistently. **No invented milestones, no invented founding narrative** |
| **`/contact`** | Complete NAP — legal name, complete postal address, phone, email. **The current entity declaration's `postalCode` field contains the word "India"**; the content and the schema must be corrected together |
| **`/how-it-works`** | The method, the measurement, and the explicit statement of what cannot be attributed |
| **`/research`** | Evidence standards visible on the index, not only inside entries |
| **Editorial authorship** | A named author on every article and research entry, with a real bio |
| **Organisation facts** | One consistent set of facts — name, location, expertise, contact details, leadership, capabilities — across `/about`, `/contact`, the footer, schema and every third-party profile |
| **`/faq`** | Honest scope, limits and what is not promised. Its real value is as a **first-party source surface** that AI systems read |

**Facts requiring owner verification before any of this is written** — recorded in full in `OWNER_INPUT_REGISTER.md`: the founding year (AI systems report **2016** from LinkedIn; **NOT VERIFIED** in this programme), the complete registered postal address, the correct legal entity name, current team members and their roles (a staff name surfaced by Google AI Mode is **NOT VERIFIED**), which social and directory profiles are genuinely current, and the status of a Google Business Profile.

**Two live entity exposures that content must not reproduce:**
- **"300%+ ROI", "₹100Cr+", "₹10Cr+"** are unsupported claims currently in MappedSkills' own material and **actively being redistributed by Google AI Mode and Perplexity as fact about the company** (`DEC-007`). They may not enter any new asset.
- **`testing.mappedskills.com`** was observed being cited by Perplexity as a source about MappedSkills. Content published anywhere that a staging host also serves becomes an entity risk. This is an engineering item, recorded here because it affects what the entity says about itself.

## 12. E-E-A-T as an evidence model, not a score

**E-E-A-T is not a metric and must never be reported as one.** It is used here only as four questions with four content answers.

| Dimension | The honest question | What MappedSkills can show **today** | What is missing, and when it arrives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Experience** | What has this firm actually done? | Its own site diagnosed and fixed in public; its own AI-visibility baseline measured across 39 runs; a documented technical audit | **Client outcomes. Zero exist.** Owner-gated (`OWNER_INPUT_REGISTER.md` §1) |
| **Expertise** | Who did the work, and can they show it? | Named authorship; the training-vs-retrieval crawler distinction most of the market gets wrong; variance-aware measurement; correct reading of Google's own published position | Depth of authored body of work — accumulates from launch. Author pages are PHASE 2 for that reason |
| **Authoritativeness** | Who else says so? | **Almost nothing.** LinkedIn is the only third-party surface MappedSkills demonstrably occupies | **Directory profiles, reviews, Google Business Profile, third-party listings.** Off-site, self-serve, needs no case study, and is the single highest-leverage action available (§16) |
| **Trust** | Can a stranger check the claims? | Published method · published limits · published run counts · stated attribution boundaries · no guarantees · named people | Response-time performance, published only once measured. Client references, owner-gated |

**The load-bearing observation:** three of the four dimensions can be improved by content MappedSkills can write this month. **Authoritativeness cannot.** It is bought with profiles, reviews and third-party references, and no volume of publishing substitutes for it.

## 14. Content freshness

| Content type | Review model | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| **AI-search articles and guides** | **Scheduled — the fastest-decaying content on the site** | Plus event-triggered on any material platform or documentation change |
| **AI-visibility research** | **Versioned re-measurement**, never an edit | A new measurement round. Prior versions are preserved, not overwritten |
| **`/pricing`** | Event-triggered | Any actual price, packaging or scope change. **Owner-owned** |
| **`/how-it-works`** | Event-triggered | Any change to the method, the measurement stack, or what can be attributed |
| **Own-site diagnostic research** | Event-triggered | Each fix actually shipped. **It is a live record of work, and only of work completed** |
| **Problem page** | **Scheduled** | Freshness is a live ranking lever on this SERP — results dated "4 days ago" and "1 week ago" were observed ranking. A cluster with no cadence decays here |
| **Commercial pages** | Event-triggered | Scope, capability, claim or evidence change |
| **Evergreen concept content** | No routine refresh | Corrected when wrong |
| **Case studies** *(when they exist)* | Event-triggered | Permission change, or a material change to the reported result |

**Two hard rules.**
1. **No date is changed without a substantive change to the content.** A cosmetic freshness edit is a small dishonesty on a site selling measurement honesty, and `Article.dateModified` currently maps to any CMS republish — that defect must be fixed before this policy means anything.
2. **A research entry is re-measured and re-versioned, never quietly edited.** Editing a published measurement destroys the trend it exists to establish.

## 16. What MappedSkills must not publish

Specific to this business, each with the evidence that rejects it.

| # | Rejected | Why, here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"What is SEO?" / "What is CRO?" and commodity definitional content** | The definitional layer is held by Coursera, PwC, Forbes, HubSpot and Ahrefs. A Pune agency's explainer has no route in |
| 2 | **One article per AI acronym** — GEO, AEO, LLMO, AIO | Upstream intent is **course, meaning, jobs, certification**; the terminology is still fragmenting. Ranking here delivers students, job-seekers and competitors — who are also **hard disqualifiers on the enquiry form** |
| 3 | **A GEO/AEO/AIO glossary** | Same SERP, same audience problem |
| 4 | **A page or article per AI platform** — ChatGPT / Gemini / Perplexity / Claude / Grok | Thin permutations of one topic. **And MappedSkills has no baseline at all for Claude or Grok** |
| 5 | **Anything built around an "AI Visibility Score" or an average AI rank** | Rejected on the classic SERP (page 1 is ten free tools led by Ahrefs and Semrush), inside AI answers (Semrush, SE Ranking, Ahrefs and Profound are named ahead of agencies), **and methodologically** — order was unstable in every repeated prompt and cross-system agreement collapsed |
| 6 | **A borrowed-statistics compilation** | `DEC-007` and the source policy. Session 04 showed AI systems redistribute whatever the site asserts, so a page of other people's numbers becomes MappedSkills' claim |
| 7 | **AI-generated listicles with no information gain** | Fails §5 outright. Project Rule 10 |
| 8 | **A fake or unsourced trend report** | MappedSkills has one day of AI measurement and no trend. **"AI visibility improved x%" cannot be said from one measurement** |
| 9 | **Unsupported ROI or client-result content** | Zero permissioned client results exist. Project Rule 15, `DEC-007` |
| 10 | **City content** — Pune, Mumbai, and permutations | The Local Pack decides this SERP; ChatGPT answered the same intent with a **16-entry local business list**. Content cannot win either surface |
| 11 | **Thin industry pages beyond the one conditional beachhead page** | One beachhead. A second vertical only after three published results |
| 12 | **A "top agencies in Pune" listicle naming competitors** | The mechanism was observed working, but it is a low-trust format close to Project Rule 10 — **and MappedSkills has no standing to arbitrate a category it appears in zero times out of 28** |
| 13 | **Rewritten Google or platform documentation** | Google's own position is that optimising for generative AI "is thus still SEO". Restating it adds nothing; **interpreting it and stating the commercial consequence is a different asset and is permitted** |
| 14 | **Daily news commentary** | No strategic relevance, no cadence capacity, and it decays instantly |
| 15 | **A `/resources` hub** — templates, checklists, ebooks with no query family | The graveyard pattern |
| 16 | **Lead-response-time or marketing-automation content** | **`how to improve lead response time` returns (NONE).** A parked expired domain ranks page 1 for `marketing automation agency india` |
| 17 | **A gated asset whose only purpose is capturing an email** | A lead gate presented as a resource will be judged as one |
