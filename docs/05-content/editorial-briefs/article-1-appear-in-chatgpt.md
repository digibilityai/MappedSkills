## 1. Working topic concept
**What actually decides whether a business appears in ChatGPT — and why crawler access is not the answer.**

*A working concept, not a headline. No final title is written in this session.*

## 2. Target query / question
**`how to appear in chatgpt`** — **8 clean variants**, PRIORITY 1: results · search · search results · answers · show in ChatGPT · show up in ChatGPT results · show up in ChatGPT searches · show up in ChatGPT.

The buyer articulates this outcome **without using GEO, AEO or LLMO**. That is the whole reason the article exists in this form.

## 3. Intent
**PA / I** — problem-aware, informational. TOFU.

## 4. Audience
A marketer or business owner who has typed their own company into an AI assistant, found nothing, and wants to know why. **Also, by design, a meaningful share of students and practitioners** — an accepted cost of ranking for a buyer-phrased family, and the reason the CTA stays low-commitment.

## 5. Why now
The SERP is **COMPETITIVE BUT POSSIBLE** — Reddit, YouTube, HubSpot and OpenAI's own Help Centre are present but the SERP is enterable, and no institutional publisher owns it the way Forbes, PwC and Coursera own the definitional queries. **And MappedSkills holds a first-party measurement on exactly this question**, which nobody ranking has.

## 6. Information gain
**Class A — first-party measurement**, and it is the article's entire justification:

- MappedSkills' own site serves **full server-rendered HTML to `OAI-SearchBot/1.4` and `ClaudeBot/1.0`**, `robots.txt` blocks no AI crawler — and MappedSkills appeared in **zero of 28** unbranded runs. **Access is necessary and demonstrably not sufficient**, demonstrated on the author's own site rather than asserted.
- The **four layers** — retrieval, discovery, recommendation, citation — separated, with MappedSkills' own result on each: **retrieval works (7 of 8 branded runs accurate), the other three are zero.** That distinction is absent from the pages currently ranking and it changes what a reader should do.
- **ChatGPT with web search disabled did not know the company and read the name compositionally** — a concrete demonstration that this visibility is retrieval-dependent, not a property of the model.
- The **training-crawler versus retrieval-crawler** distinction, with the actual tokens, and Google's verbatim statement that Google-Extended "does not impact a site's inclusion in Google Search".

**The delete-test:** remove every sentence that could be written by someone who had not run 39 measured prompts, and the article still has its central argument — because that argument *is* the measurement.

## 15. What would make this article generic and therefore unacceptable
- A numbered list of "tips to optimise for ChatGPT" with no measurement behind any of them.
- Restating OpenAI or Google documentation without interpretation or a stated consequence.
- **Selling crawler configuration as the answer** — which is the article's own counter-example.
- Any implied ranking, score, guarantee or percentage improvement.
- Using GEO, AEO or LLMO as framing.
- Borrowed statistics with no reachable source.
- **Omitting the unflattering result.** The measured zero is the article's credibility; removing it removes the reason to believe anything else in it.
- **Reproducing the baseline's dataset, methodology, charts or conclusion** rather than linking to the canonical entry and adding application (`CONTENT_STRATEGY.md` §6A).
