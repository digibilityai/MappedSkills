## 4. Audience

## 5. Why now

It also does the job the withdrawn article 2 was reaching for, **without duplicating a research dataset.**

## 6. The argument the article must make

Six steps, in order. This is the article's spine:

1. **Rankings are not the business outcome.** A position is a proxy, and a proxy that moves without the number that matters moving.
2. **Traffic is not the business outcome.** Volume can rise while enquiries are flat, and on the target vocabulary a meaningful share of visitors are students, job-seekers and vendors by design.
3. **A form submission alone is not necessarily the business outcome.** It may be spam, a job application, a course enquiry, a vendor pitch, or a duplicate. **Counting raw submissions flatters the number that decisions get made on.**
4. **Source and landing context have to survive into the enquiry record.** If the record does not carry where the enquiry came from and what it landed on, no channel decision can be made from it — and the capture has to happen **in the enquiry itself**, not reconstructed later.
5. **Qualified enquiries are the useful website-level commercial outcome** — real, reachable, in-context, non-duplicate, and not disqualified. That is what a website can honestly be held to.
6. **Opportunity and revenue data improve the picture without pretending attribution is perfect.** Client close data can be joined back; **what cannot be attributed is stated rather than smoothed over.**

**And the rule that must not be dropped:** a measurement gap **never** disqualifies a legitimate enquiry. Qualification and attribution completeness are independent dimensions, and the gap is reported rather than hidden.

## 7. Information gain
**Class A and E — first-party diagnosis plus a disclosed, reusable method.** The article is not a general essay about SEO metrics; it is grounded in work actually done:

- **MappedSkills ran no analytics at all in production** — `dataLayer`, `gtag` and `fbq` all undefined in a real browser. **It could not have answered its own question**, and it says so.
- **`/thank-you` was orphaned**, so submissions could not have been counted even if they had arrived.
- **A published, machine-evaluable definition of a qualified enquiry** — five conditions, four states, and named disqualifier categories — which almost nobody in this category publishes.
- **The independence of qualification and attribution**, stated as a rule with its reasoning: much of the demand this strategy depends on arrives with weak attribution by nature, and downgrading it would let a measurement failure masquerade as a demand failure.
- **A reusable check** the reader can run: can you answer, for last month, how many enquiries were real, where they came from, and what they landed on?

**The delete-test:** remove every sentence that could be written by someone who had not built a qualification definition and audited a real measurement layer, and the argument still stands — because that work *is* the argument.
