## 3. Intent

## 4. Audience
A business owner or marketing lead who suspects something is wrong and does not know how to check. Frequently non-technical.

## 5. Why now
Three reasons, and the first is the strongest:

1. **MappedSkills found exactly this failure on its own site**, and the evidence is verified, dated and reproducible.
2. The cluster's anchor sits on **the most winnable SERP in the programme**, and **freshness is a live ranking lever there** — results dated "4 days ago" rank. A cluster with one page and no cadence decays.
3. It gives the cluster a **second entry point** without competing with the anchor.

## 6. Information gain
**Class A and B — first-party diagnosis plus a disclosed, reusable method:**

- **A form that shows success while transmitting nothing.** VERIFIED on MappedSkills' own production site: no `action`, no `method`. **Every enquiry since that code shipped was lost, and the visitor was told it succeeded.** This is the article's central, uncomfortable example and it is about the author's own site.
- **A booking widget that renders empty** — zero iframes on the page, with a "not configured yet" fallback in the pre-hydration HTML, which is what non-JS crawlers see.
- **An orphaned `/thank-you`** — nothing links or redirects to it, so submissions could not be counted even if they arrived.
- **No analytics of any kind in production** — `dataLayer`, `gtag` and `fbq` all undefined in a real browser. **The loss was invisible because nothing was watching.**
- A **reusable verification sequence** the reader can run on their own site today, ending in an observable answer at each step.

**The delete-test:** the sequence exists because the author ran it and found four real failures. Nobody paraphrasing ranking pages produces that.

## 7. Evidence needed
`/research/{own-site-enquiry-diagnostic}` published first — **which means the fixes must be shipped first.** Dated evidence from the diagnostic. **No client examples, and none may be substituted.**

## 8. Indicative structure
1. The uncomfortable premise: a form can show success and send nothing.
2. Why this is invisible without measurement.
3. **The verification sequence** — five checks, each ending in an observable outcome: does a test enquiry actually arrive · does the booking surface render and complete · is the conversion recorded, and where · does the source survive to the record · is the enquiry reaching a monitored destination.
4. **What was found on this site**, with dates.
5. What was changed, and what could not be measured afterwards — **including that a pre/post comparison is impossible because no baseline existed.**
6. When this is not your problem — pointing up to the anchor page's four loss zones.

## 15. What would make this article generic and therefore unacceptable
- A list of "10 reasons your website is not converting" — that is the anchor page's territory, and done generically it is the exact content this cluster exists to beat.
- **Any conversion benchmark, average or uplift figure.**
- **A fabricated before/after.** No baseline existed; the article must say so.
- Publishing the diagnostic **before the fixes are shipped**, which turns a proof asset into a list of live defects.
- Competing with the anchor page on its head phrasings.
- Selling a paid audit as the answer to every check — **the sequence must be completable without contact.**
- Presenting the own-site failure as a clever marketing device rather than a real failure that lost real enquiries.
