## 6. Information gain
**Class A and E — first-party observation plus disclosed interpretation.** The article must be grounded in observed buyer and RFQ behaviour, not in generic vertical advice:

- **"RFQ" is the buyer's word and the systems' word.** Google AI Mode, ChatGPT and Perplexity all used RFQ / Request for Quotation **unprompted**, alongside dealer enquiries and export orders. A ranking Pune specialist promises "Get RFQs Fast". **The vertical's outcome is conversion-shaped, not ranking-shaped** — which most vertical SEO content misses entirely.
- **Marketplaces are part of the path.** IndiaMART and TradeIndia surfaced in an AI answer as **channels** for manufacturer enquiries. An article that pretends Google is the only route is wrong about how these buyers behave.
- **The observed entry mechanism into this vertical was a directory or a single listicle — not vertical content.** Stating that honestly is more useful to the reader than pretending publishing solves it.
- **Specialisation substitutes for tenure, observed directly**: a 3-year-old Pune agency with 17 reviews holds a Local Pack slot on this exact query beside 486- and 507-review generalists.
- A **worked RFQ path** — specification search → supplier evaluation → RFQ → quote → order — **with the loss points named.**

**The delete-test:** if the article would read the same written by someone who had never looked at an industrial SERP or an AI answer about manufacturers, it fails.
