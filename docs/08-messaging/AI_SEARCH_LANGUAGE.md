**Governed by:** `BUSINESS_STRATEGY.md` §10 (frozen) · `POSITIONING.md` §10 (frozen) · `SERVICE_ARCHITECTURE.md` §5 (frozen) · `AI_CITATION_CONTENT_PRINCIPLES.md` and `CLAIMS_AND_EVIDENCE_GOVERNANCE.md` §2.6 (approved, Gate 7) · Session 04 measurement.

**This is the highest-risk vocabulary on the site.** It is the area where the market's language is loosest, where the temptation to overclaim is strongest, and where an error is most likely to be redistributed by the systems being written about.

---

## 0. The position this language must express, both halves of it

> **AI search is an important, highly visible supporting capability and differentiator — and it is subordinate to the enquiry-generation outcome. It is not the company category.**

**Both halves are load-bearing and neither may be collapsed.**

**Visible** means: it has a commercial page, a launch article, a published research baseline and a PHASE 2 technical guide, and the site demonstrates genuine current expertise.

**Subordinate** means: it is never at Level 1 of the message hierarchy, never in the company description, never on the homepage's first screen, and never the reason the company exists.

**The architectural test, restated as a messaging test:** AI search is visible in four surfaces and is the company's category in **none**. If the homepage or `/services` reads as an AI-search agency, this has been violated regardless of what `/ai-seo` says.

---

## 1. The preferred umbrella term

> **"AI search"** — lowercase *search*, used as an ordinary noun phrase.

**Acceptable alternates, by context:**

| Term | Use where |
|---|---|
| **"AI search"** | Default. Page titles, navigation, capability descriptions, headings |
| **"AI-assisted discovery"** | Explanatory and method contexts, where the point is how buyers find things rather than a product category. Preferred in `/how-it-works` and research |
| **"showing up in AI answers"** | Plain-language buyer framing. Preferred on the homepage, `/faq` and in objection handling |
| **"AI answers"** | When referring to the output of these systems generically |

**Never as an umbrella term:** "AI visibility" *(the query resolves to free tools, not agencies, and it names a score MappedSkills rejects)* · "generative search" · "answer engines" · "LLM optimisation" · "AI SEO" as the company's category *(the page URL `/ai-seo` targets the query; it is not what the company calls itself)*.

## 3. Claims permitted

Each is supportable from first-party measurement or primary documentation today.

1. **"We measure presence per system, with the run counts published, and we do not blend systems."** — Cross-system agreement collapsed in direct measurement; blending destroys the only stable signal.
2. **"We measured our own presence and published the result, including a zero."** — 7 of 8 branded runs accurate; **0 of 28** unbranded appearances; dated, single-day, signed-out, India lens.
3. **"Being present in AI answers is mostly a source-authority problem."** — Directory sources appeared in **15 of 28** unbranded runs, and ChatGPT stated in plain text that it selected brands by reading two directories.
4. **"Crawler access is necessary and demonstrably not sufficient."** — Full server-rendered HTML was served to `OAI-SearchBot/1.4` and `ClaudeBot/1.0`, `robots.txt` blocks no AI crawler, and the result was still zero.
5. **"Training crawlers and retrieval crawlers are different things, and most of the market conflates them."** — With the actual tokens named, from primary documentation.
6. **"Google's own published position is that optimising for generative AI is still SEO."** — Quoted accurately, with the source, its date and the retrieval date, and re-verified at publication.
7. **"Order in AI answers is not a stable number."** — Order changed in every repeated prompt measured.

**Every one of these carries its scope:** the date, the systems, the location, the signed-out tier, and the run counts.

---

## 4. Claims prohibited

**Absolute. No exception, no softened variant, no implication through illustration or CTA.**

1. **Any guarantee of appearance, mention, citation, recommendation or position** in any AI system.
2. **"Rank in ChatGPT", "AI rank", "average AI rank"** — order was unstable in every repeated prompt. **There is no universal AI ranking, and messaging must not invent one.**
3. **An "AI Visibility Score", or any blended cross-system figure.** Rejected on the classic SERP, rejected inside AI answers, and **rejected methodologically** — blending destroys the only stable signal.
4. **Any claim that crawler access, `llms.txt`, schema or AI-specific markup produces visibility.** MappedSkills' own baseline is the counter-example, and nothing observed suggests these drove any selection.
5. **Any statement about Claude or Grok.** Not testable; no baseline exists.
6. **Any trend, improvement or percentage change.** There is **one** measurement. "One measurement is not a trend" is stated in the asset rather than implied by omission.
7. **Any claim that AI visibility drives revenue or enquiries today.** All four layers are currently zero or unmeasurable for MappedSkills, and no independent, auditable data supports the general claim.
8. **Any competitor characterised as having "no AI visibility."** Zero appearances in a 28-run signed-out sample is an observation, not an absence.
9. **The Ahrefs 23× and Buffer 185% AI-visitor conversion multipliers quoted as benchmarks.** Single-company figures with self-selecting audiences.
10. **GEO, AEO or LLMO as a service MappedSkills sells.**

**One figure with a mandatory caveat:** the ~0.28% AI-referral-traffic share against Google's 28.12% (March 2026) is a **vendor blog citing an unaudited third-party panel**. It may be used to size the opportunity honestly, and the caveat must travel with it every time.

## 5. How to talk about the mechanism — the diagnostic chain

**This is the differentiated message, and it is a chain rather than a score.**

> Absent from AI answers → because those answers are assembled from directories, listings and third-party lists → which this business is not on.

**Why the chain is the message:** the tools report the absence. Nobody observed in the research sells the chain. And the chain is what tells a business *what to do*, which a score never does.

**The honest half that must travel with it:** a material part of this work is **off-site** — directory profiles, reviews, a complete Google Business Profile, being listed in other people's lists — and **owned-site publishing cannot substitute for it.** MappedSkills says this rather than implying its own pages are the remedy.

---

## 6. The four layers, kept separate

**Never combined into one number, and never described as one thing.**

| Layer | The question | MappedSkills' own result |
|---|---|---|
| **Retrieval** | Can a system find and read the site at all? | Works — full access, served correctly |
| **Discovery** | Does the business appear when the buyer does not name it? | **Zero, in 28 unbranded runs** |
| **Recommendation** | Is the business actually recommended? | Zero |
| **Citation** | Is the site used as a source in an unbranded answer? | Zero |

**The whole finding would be invisible under any single score.** That is the argument for separating them, and it is the clearest available demonstration of why a score is the wrong instrument.

## 8. Two sentences that must never be written

> ~~"We'll get you ranking in ChatGPT."~~ — There is no ranking to get.

> ~~"We make sure AI systems can crawl and understand your site, so you show up in AI answers."~~ — The causal link is false, and MappedSkills' own site is the counter-example: complete access, zero appearances.

**The correct shape of the second sentence:** access is checked because its absence would be fatal, and then the real work — entity accuracy, source authority and the listings footprint — begins.
