## 0. The organising rule

**A capability is introduced by what it contributes to the enquiry chain, never as an item on a menu.**

The chain, from `SERVICE_ARCHITECTURE.md` §2:

> discovery / source → landing experience → interaction → enquiry → **qualified enquiry** → opportunity → customer and revenue data supplied by the client

Every capability below occupies a position in that chain. **Messaging names the position before it names the capability.** That single habit is what stops eight capability pages from reading as eight departments.

**Two consequences, both binding:**
1. **No capability appears at Level 1 of the message hierarchy.** Not on the homepage, not on `/services`, not in the company description, not in a directory profile's summary line.
2. **No capability is described as optional-but-recommended, or as a package tier.** Each is described as *"used where it is the right demand source"* or *"part of every engagement"*, whichever is true.

## 2. SEO language — doing SEO without becoming an SEO agency

**The tension, stated plainly:** `/seo` must satisfy someone actively looking for SEO help within one screen, while the company remains a demand-capture and enquiry-generation firm.

**The resolution is sequence, not omission.** The page says the word, then says what the word is for here.

### 2.1 Term rulings

| Term | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **SEO** | **Say it.** On `/seo`, in its title, and wherever a buyer would use it. It is a discipline MappedSkills performs. It is never the noun that describes the company |
| **rankings** | A diagnostic proxy. Reported, never promised, never quantified, never the headline of a result. **No "#1", no "page one", no guaranteed position** |
| **traffic** | An input. Always qualified — "the right traffic". Never a promised increase and never a figure |
| **search demand** | **Preferred.** The clearest available term for what MappedSkills captures, and it correctly implies the demand pre-exists |
| **relevant search visibility** | Avoid the noun phrase. Say **"being found for the searches that actually matter to your business"** |
| **organic enquiries** | **Useful and permitted** — enquiries that came from organic search. It is the bridge term between the discipline and the outcome |
| **commercial search intent** | Permitted where the audience is sophisticated (`/seo` targets the most sophisticated sub-family in the space). Define once, in place |
| **technical SEO** | Permitted on `/seo` and `/how-it-works` as a described competence. **Not a page, not a service name** — the query family is career-contaminated |

### 2.2 What `/seo` must and must not do

**Must:** confirm relevance in the first screen · describe the actual work · **state the 12-month-plus horizon up front and not soften it** · say what happens to the traffic after it arrives · state what cannot be promised · link down to the problem page for the "we tried SEO and nothing happened" reader.

**Must not:** become the page the rest of the site has to live up to · claim a volume, difficulty or CPC figure — **none has ever been obtained in this programme** · take local/Pune framing, which belongs to an off-site programme no page can substitute for · sell technical fixes or crawler configuration as a route to AI visibility.

### 2.3 The bridge sentence pattern

> *"Search is the strongest way to reach buyers who are already looking. It is also only the first half — we stay accountable through the page they land on, the enquiry they make, and the measurement that tells you it happened."*

**Conceptual, not approved copy.** Every capability page needs a bridge of this shape: **confirm → connect → boundary.**

## 3. Conversion language — without defaulting to "CRO"

**"CRO" is prohibited in body copy.** `cro services` returns pharmaceutical Contract Research Organisations in this market, `b2b cro` returns croissants and crown rings, and `conversion rate optimization agency` returns **(NONE)**. The acronym is not shorthand here; it is noise.

### 3.1 Buyer-facing language for each concept

| Concept | Buyer-facing language |
|---|---|
| Conversion optimisation | **"Turning visits into enquiries"** · "getting more of the right enquiries from the traffic you already have" |
| Enquiry-path improvement | **"The enquiry path"** — the whole route from arrival to a received enquiry. **A genuinely useful term and the one to standardise on** |
| Website conversion | "whether visits become enquiries" |
| Form and booking friction | "what makes people abandon the form" · "whether the booking actually works" |
| Landing-page effectiveness | **"whether the page answers the visit"** |
| Enquiry leakage | **"where enquiries are being lost"** · "the break in the path" |

### 3.2 The experimentation boundary — two different things, named differently

**This distinction must appear on `/services` and `/conversion-optimization`, and it must not be blurred.**

| | **Evidence-informed improvement** | **Controlled experimentation** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Diagnosis, research and design change based on observed behaviour, technical findings and buyer language | A test with enough traffic and conversions to reach statistical significance |
| When it applies | **Below a real traffic and conversion threshold — which is most clients** | Only where the volume genuinely supports it |
| Language permitted | "we diagnose", "we found", "we changed", "we observed after the change" | "we tested", "the test reached significance", "A/B test" |
| Language prohibited | **"we tested", "A/B test", "statistically significant", "the winning variant", "uplift"** | Any significance claim without the underlying volume |

**Rule:** promising testing where volume cannot support significance is the same category of dishonesty as an AI visibility rank, and is prohibited (`SERVICE_ARCHITECTURE.md` §4).

### 3.3 The claim that must never be made

**No conversion-rate figure, benchmark, average or before/after.** None exists in this programme's evidence base, MappedSkills has no analytics baseline of its own, and **a pre/post comparison is impossible and must not be presented.**

## 4. Measurement language — useful to an owner, not analytics theatre

Measurement is a differentiator and the easiest thing on this site to make boring. **The test for every measurement sentence: would a business owner know what they would actually see, and what they would not?**

### 4.1 Plain-language equivalents

| Technical | Buyer-facing |
|---|---|
| Attribution | **"where an enquiry came from"** |
| First-touch | "how they first found you" |
| Conversion-touch | "what they were looking at when they enquired" |
| Source capture | "the enquiry records its own source, at the moment it is made" |
| Qualified enquiry | "an enquiry that is real, reachable and from a business with a stated need" |
| Opportunity and revenue linkage | "we join it back to the deals your own system recorded, if you give us that data" |
| Baseline | "the starting number we capture before anything changes" |
| Unattributed | "arrived with no usable trail — we show it as unattributed rather than guessing" |

### 4.2 The four sentences that make measurement credible

Conceptual patterns, not copy. Every measurement section should be able to answer all four.

1. **What we capture:** where an enquiry came from, what it landed on, what it was about, and whether it counts.
2. **What you see:** enquiries against a starting number we capture first, broken down by where they came from and whether we could tell.
3. **What we cannot tell you:** enquiries that arrive with no trail — direct visits, phone calls, word of mouth, consent declined — plus anything about revenue we do not receive from your system.
4. **What a gap does not mean:** an enquiry we could not trace is still a real enquiry. **We never move it out of the count to make the measurement look tidier.**

### 4.3 Prohibited measurement language

"full-funnel visibility" · "single source of truth" · "360-degree view of the customer" · "complete attribution" · "we track everything" · "data-driven" as a self-description · "real-time insights" · any dashboard mock-up containing a number that does not exist · **any illustrative chart with invented data** (Project Rule 15).

### 4.4 The false-precision rule

**No decimal place, index, score or composite that the underlying data does not support.** A mean of unstable values is manufactured precision. Where a figure cannot be obtained, **write UNKNOWN.**

## 5. Paid media language

**Paid media is a demand source inside the enquiry system.** It is retained as a capability and removed from company-level positioning (`DEC-005` principle 8). Its language must reflect that in every appearance.

### 5.1 The four roles it may be described as playing

1. **Capture active demand now** — buy access to buyers already searching, without waiting for the organic horizon.
2. **Reach demand search cannot yet reach** — where an additional audience genuinely exists and the channel fits.
3. **Learn faster** — produce data about messages, offers and enquiry quality more quickly than organic can.
4. **Give the business a controllable acquisition lever** — spend that can be turned up, down or off against a measured outcome.

**Always with the measurement clause attached:** on the same measurement layer as everything else, connected to qualified enquiries rather than to clicks or cost per click.

### 5.2 Rules

- **Never imply every client needs paid media.** The correct sentence shape is *"where it is the right demand source for you"*, and `/social-media-ads` may say outright that paid social is frequently the wrong channel for a considered purchase.
- **Never a ROI, ROAS, cost-per-lead or spend-efficiency figure.** The prohibited production claim originated in this category.
- **Never "performance marketing agency"** — in copy, in a title, in a meta description, or in a directory profile.
- **Spend and fee are stated as separate things**, and `[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — how spend is separated from fee]` until the owner confirms the arrangement.
- Paid pages **must not read as a separate agency bolted onto the site.** They open by confirming the searched capability, then place it in the chain within two sentences, and they link up to `/services` like every other capability page.

## 6. Automation language — deliberately constrained

**Automation is a limited downstream expansion capability. It gets a sentence, not a stage, and never a page.**

### 6.1 Allowed territory, and the words for it

Enquiry routing · acknowledgement to the person who enquired · internal notification · reminders · basic qualification · email and WhatsApp follow-up · no-show recovery.

**Buyer-facing framing:** *"what happens after an enquiry arrives"* — routing it to the right person, acknowledging it, and reminding both sides about a booked call.

### 6.2 Prohibited drift

**Never:** marketing automation as a service · CRM implementation or migration · RevOps · lifecycle marketing or lifecycle architecture · sales-operations consulting · "nurture sequences" *(they are **not built**)* · lead scoring *(no model exists)* · "workflows" or "playbooks" as products · "automation-first" as a descriptor.

**And never a problem statement built around it.** `how to improve lead response time` returns **(NONE)** in this market. There is no demand to address, and manufacturing one would read as an invented need.

### 6.3 Where it appears

One sentence on `/services`, one short section on `/how-it-works` under expansion, and one honest FAQ answer if asked. **Nowhere else.** It is not in the navigation, it has no page, and it is not a supporting value proposition.
