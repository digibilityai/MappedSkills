## 1. The reusable commercial-page pattern

### 1.1 The nine message moves

**Not all nine apply to every page.** §1.2 states which are mandatory and which are optional by intent.

| # | Move | What it does | The failure it prevents |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Confirm the searched capability** | Say, in the first screen and in the searcher's own word, that MappedSkills does this | The visitor cannot tell within one screen whether the page is relevant, and leaves |
| **2** | **Bridge to the commercial outcome** | One or two sentences connecting the capability to enquiries, and stating that it is not sold alone | The page becomes a category page and the company drifts into that category |
| **3** | **Name the buyer's actual problem** | The situation this capability is usually bought to fix, in the buyer's words | The page reads as a service description rather than an argument |
| **4** | **Describe what the work actually involves** | Scope, plainly, month to month | Vagueness, which in this category reads as inexperience |
| **5** | **State the boundary — what is not included and not promised** | Early, not buried | The page over-promises by omission, and the first sales call becomes a correction |
| **6** | **Explain how it is measured and what will be shown** | Including what cannot be attributed | Measurement becomes an unverifiable claim rather than a differentiator |
| **7** | **Place evidence in context, beside the claims it supports** | Method and own-diagnostic proof, at the point of the claim | A proof section nobody reads, and claims with nothing behind them |
| **8** | **Answer this capability's specific objections** | The two or three that actually stop this buyer | Generic objection handling that answers nobody's real concern |
| **9** | **Convert** | One outcome, two surfaces, direct contact visible | A CTA that promises an outcome, or a page that dead-ends |

### 1.2 Which moves are mandatory, by intent

**Do not force identical structures where buyer intent differs.**

| Page | Intent | Mandatory moves | Optional / omitted | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `/seo` | CI / T, **high sophistication** | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 | 7 is compressed — the own-site technical diagnosis is the evidence | **Move 5 carries the 12-month-plus horizon, stated up front and not softened** |
| `/ai-seo` | CI / T, **often misinformed** | **All nine** | — | Moves 5 and 8 are the page. This is the page most likely to be written wrongly |
| `/google-ads` | T | 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 9 | 3 compressed; 7 minimal | **Move 5 must separate spend from fee**, and move 2 must be unambiguous that this is not a performance marketing agency |
| `/social-media-ads` | T | 1, 2, 4, 5, 9 | 3, 7, 8 minimal | The most modest page in the set, and it should read that way. **Move 5 may say the channel is often wrong for a considered purchase** |
| `/lead-generation` | T / CI | 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9 | 4 compressed | **Move 6 is the differentiator**: what counts as a qualified enquiry, published |
| `/conversion-optimization` | CI, **residual** | 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9 | 4 compressed | **Move 5 carries the experimentation boundary.** Move 2 routes down to the problem page, which is the real acquisition route |
| `/industries/manufacturing` *(conditional)* | T | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9 | 6 compressed | **Move 5 must say plainly that no manufacturing result exists.** Move 8 answers "you haven't worked with manufacturers" directly |

### 1.3 Rules that apply to every commercial page

1. **Moves 1 and 2 happen in the first screen.** Always. There is no page on which the bridge is delayed.
2. **Move 5 appears above the midpoint.** A boundary at the bottom of the page is a disclaimer; a boundary near the top is a position.
3. **Move 7 is in context, never a required step.** Proof links prominently to `/how-it-works` and the relevant research entry; **it is never interposed between the page and the conversion.**
4. **Every commercial page links up to `/services`** and across to its matching problem page.
5. **No page contains a number that is not a level B observation with its sample, date, location and method.**
6. **No page uses a rejected category term as its framing**, even where its metadata carries the query phrase.
7. **Friction tolerance is highest here** — the visitor declared a need by choosing the page, which is what justifies the four-field form.

## 2. `/services` — the page that *is* the proposition

**`/services` is not an agency service catalogue.** It is the only true capability hub in the architecture, and it targets **no query family** — deliberately.

### 2.1 Message sequence

| # | Block | Message job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The outcome** | What the whole system produces: qualified enquiries, measured. Level 2, stated as the page's subject |
| 2 | **The three connected parts** | Capture demand → convert it into enquiries → measure the chain. **Stated as parts of one system whose joins are owned, described separately because they are different skills.** Where a buyer asks for one part, the honest answer is that parts can be scoped, and why fixing one alone rarely changes the number — **not that all three must be bought** |
| 3 | **The accountability boundary** | Owned to the qualified enquiry · reported through to revenue on client data · what cannot be attributed |
| 4 | **Where each capability enters the chain** | Capabilities introduced **positionally** — by what they contribute — with a link whose label describes that contribution |
| 5 | **Selective expansion** | What can be added later, when the client feels the pain. **One short paragraph, and automation gets one sentence inside it** |
| 6 | **What we do not do** | CRM implementation, RevOps, lifecycle consulting, standalone development, ecommerce merchandising, social media management. Named plainly |
| 7 | **Scope honesty** | Below a real traffic threshold this is conversion research, diagnosis and design — **not statistically valid A/B testing** |
| 8 | **Fit** | Who this is for and who it is not for |
| 9 | **Action** | PRIMARY, both surfaces |

### 2.2 How capabilities are introduced without becoming disconnected cards

**The rule:** a capability is named **after** the part of the chain it serves, in a sentence that states its contribution — never as an equal item in a grid of six.

**Structurally:**
- **Three groups, not six items:** the capabilities appear under *capture*, *convert* and *measure*. Paid search, paid social and organic search sit together under capture, as alternative demand sources rather than as three services.
- **No "Our services" heading.** No card grid with equal visual and verbal weight. No per-capability feature list.
- **Each link's label describes the contribution**, not the discipline — the discipline name follows.
- **Order reflects the chain, not commercial preference.**
- **AI search occupies one paragraph inside capture.** No dedicated section, no disproportionate space.

### 2.3 Prohibited drift

Reading as an SEO, AI-search, GEO/AEO or performance-marketing agency page — **if it does, the architecture has been violated regardless of what `/ai-seo` says** · restoring the superseded three-pillar structure · any automation or RevOps framing · a service menu in any form · a guarantee · a client result · a prohibited figure.

## 3. `/pricing`

### 3.1 What must be understood before any number appears

1. **What is being bought** — one system, not a service list.
2. **What MappedSkills is accountable for, and where that stops.**
3. **The horizon** — organic is 12 months-plus. **A price with no horizon reads as a fee rather than an investment**, and stating it honestly does more qualification work than any caveat.
4. **That measurement is included**, not sold separately.
5. **What the engagement requires from the client** — access, decisions, product knowledge, follow-up capacity.

### 3.2 How scope and complexity are explained

**The most useful section on the page is what moves the number, not the number itself.** It is a factor matrix, not a paragraph of caveats: scope · how many demand sources · site complexity · existing measurement maturity · content requirements · how competitive the market is.

**Each factor states the direction of movement, in plain terms.** A buyer should be able to place themselves in a band before contacting anyone.

### 3.3 How pricing does the qualification work

A poor-fit buyer should be able to leave **without enquiring**. In plain language, and without disparagement: low value per opportunity · impulse or price-only purchases · demand that has to be created rather than captured · unwillingness to grant measurement access · an expectation of guaranteed outcomes.

**A rising unqualified rate from this page is expected and is not a defect.** The `ICP.md` fit criteria are the mitigation, not the page.

### 3.4 How to avoid defensive pricing language

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| State the number, then state what moves it | Justifying the number before stating it |
| "Here is what this is not for" | "We are not the cheapest, but…" |
| Name what cheaper suppliers genuinely deliver, and what is different here | Disparaging cheaper suppliers |
| "The method is what you can check before you commit" | "You get what you pay for" |
| A plain fit/not-fit indication | "Investment, not a cost" |

**And the hardest objection is answered directly, not dodged:** a premium price with no case studies. What is offered instead is the published method, the two own diagnostics and the accountability boundary. **Do not imply proof that does not exist.**

### 3.5 Uncertainty

Bands are bands. A real number comes from scope, and the page says so without treating that as an evasion. **No ROI figure, no payback period, no value calculator producing an implied return, no "clients typically see".**

### 3.6 CTA language at this buying stage

**Booking is emphasised** — price-stage buyers want a person, not a form. The CTA acknowledges that they are deciding, not researching. **No urgency, no scarcity, no limited-time framing.**

### 3.7 Blocked

**Every figure on this page is `[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — confirmed price bands, inclusions, exclusions, whether ad spend is separate, minimum engagement]`.** No price may be invented, changed, rounded or inferred.

## 4. `/how-it-works` — the published methodology

**The highest-value proof asset available today, the only one requiring no permission from anyone, and the root dependency of the whole content programme.**

### 4.1 Message architecture

| # | Element | Message job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Opening** | Not "our process". **"Here is how the work is done, what gets measured, and what cannot be."** The opening states that the page exists to be checked |
| 2 | **The diagnostic logic** | The chain that connects the disciplines — absent from answers → because the sources are directories and listings → which this business is not on; and traffic without enquiries is one of four failures, with measurement being what tells you which. **This is the page's argument: the disciplines are one chain, which is why the joins between them have to belong to somebody. It is not an argument that every engagement contains all of them** |
| 3 | **The stages** | Each states what happens · what is needed from the client · what is produced · what is measured · **what cannot be concluded from it** |
| 4 | **What is measured** | Discovery and source → landing → interaction → enquiry → **qualified enquiry** → and, on client-supplied data, opportunity and revenue |
| 5 | **What is not measured, and cannot be** | Revenue MappedSkills does not control · a "rank" in any AI system · anything about systems it cannot test · significance below a real traffic threshold · enquiries with no usable acquisition context · a pre/post comparison where no baseline was captured first |
| 6 | **The attribution-honesty section** | **The highest-value single section on the site.** The named limitations, at full strength, plus the governing rule: a measurement gap never disqualifies a legitimate enquiry |
| 7 | **Decision points** | Where the work changes direction, and on what evidence |
| 8 | **Where human judgement enters** | Named explicitly rather than hidden behind process language — which demand is worth capturing · which of four failures a symptom indicates · whether an enquiry is a real fit · what a partial measurement means · when to say a channel is wrong |
| 9 | **Where capabilities enter** | Positionally, inside the stages. **Paid media as a demand source chosen on merit. AI search in its five subordinate roles. Automation gets one sentence** |
| 10 | **What the client must provide** | Access, decisions, product knowledge, follow-up capacity — stated as a requirement, not a request |
| 11 | **What happens after an enquiry** | The real sequence. **No numeric response-time commitment** |
| 12 | **Uncertainty block** | Visible, not a footnote: search volume is UNKNOWN everywhere in this programme · there is no analytics baseline yet · organic is 12 months-plus · AI measurement is one round, one location, English, signed out, with two of six named systems untestable |
| 13 | **CTA transition** | **PROOF is the page's role.** PRIMARY appears **at the end only**, after the method has been delivered. **No sticky CTA** — a persistent sales prompt undercuts the credibility this page exists to build |

### 4.2 Two hard rules

1. **No branded framework name is invented.** A named methodology is not a moat, `POSITIONING.md` §8 says so, and no name is created or approved by this session.
2. **A methodology page describing an invented process is fabricated proof.** Project Rule 15 applies without qualification.

### 4.3 Blocked sections — flagged, not filled

| Section | Status |
|---|---|
| Every stage's actual content — what is delivered, in what order, by whom | **`[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — the real delivery process]`. BLOCKING.** The page cannot be written without it |
| What happens after an enquiry, and who responds | **`[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — enquiry response ownership and business hours]`** |
| Response timing | **`[EVIDENCE REQUIRED — a measured response-time distribution]`.** No numeric SLA until one exists |
| Named people performing each stage | **`[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — team members and roles]`** |
| The own-site diagnostic as a worked example | **Sequence-blocked** until the fixes are shipped |

## 5. `/problems/traffic-but-no-enquiries`

**The binding constraint:** the page must be **materially useful on its own terms**, containing a real diagnostic sequence a reader can follow without hiring anyone. A service pitch behind a question-shaped headline is a doorway page.

### 5.1 Message progression

| # | Move | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Validate the symptom** | Name it in the buyer's own words. **Do not immediately imply a cause** |
| 2 | **Refuse the assumption** | State early that this is one of four things, and that they need different fixes. **This is the page's distinctive move and it must not be softened** |
| 3 | **The four loss zones** | **Demand** — the traffic is the wrong traffic · **Page** — it does not answer the visit · **Path** — the enquiry cannot complete · **Measurement** — enquiries may be arriving unseen |
| 4 | **Self-identification** | Named symptom patterns a reader recognises without instrumentation |
| 5 | **The runnable sequence** | Five checks, **each ending in an observable outcome**, including *submit a real test enquiry and confirm it arrives* — a success screen is not proof of delivery |
| 6 | **What each finding means** | And what the appropriate fix is per zone, **including where conversion work is the wrong intervention** |
| 7 | **How MappedSkills investigates** | The method, briefly, with the own-site diagnostic as the worked example |
| 8 | **Commercial transition** | **Only after the reader has an answer.** DIAGNOSTIC role, below the fold, and it must never dead-end in a service pitch |

### 5.2 What keeps it from becoming `/conversion-optimization` with another title

**Four structural separations, all binding:**

1. **The question differs.** The problem page answers *"why is this happening, and which one is mine?"* `/conversion-optimization` answers *"who does this work, and how?"*
2. **The page's argument is that conversion work is the wrong fix in at least two of the four zones**, and it says so plainly. **That honesty is its credibility and the reason it can rank against agency posts that all sell the same fix.**
3. **The diagnostic must be completable without contact.** That is the answer to "this is just an agency pitch", and it has to be true.
4. **It must not target `conversion rate optimization` or any category phrasing** — those belong to the commercial page, and taking them would collapse the boundary that makes both pages viable.

### 5.3 Prohibited

"CRO" in body copy · any conversion benchmark, average or uplift figure — **none exists** · any fabricated before/after · any claim that conversion optimisation solves every case · a hard sell above the fold.

## 6. Research message architecture

**Research must not sound like a disguised sales page.** The property that makes it work is that it remains useful to a reader who never becomes a client.

### 6.1 Sequence

| # | Element | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Opening** | **The question and why it was asked** — not the conclusion as a hook, and not a company claim. A research asset that opens by selling has already lost the reader it was written for |
| 2 | **The question** | Stated precisely enough that someone could answer it differently and be comparable |
| 3 | **Method** | Systems, surfaces, prompts, run counts, date, location, language, access tier, and what a run recorded. **In enough detail to be reproduced** |
| 4 | **Findings** | Including the adverse ones. **An entry containing only flattering results is not research and will not be read as research** |
| 5 | **Interpretation** | **Labelled as interpretation**, separated from the findings, never phrased as a finding |
| 6 | **Limitations** | What could not be measured and why, including systems that were not testable at all |
| 7 | **Commercial relevance** | **Short, at the end, once.** What this means for a business making a decision |
| 8 | **CTA restraint** | **RESEARCH role. No sticky CTA, no gate, no email wall, no download form.** PRIMARY only well below the fold |

### 6.2 Connecting research to services without contaminating trust

**Three rules:**
1. **The link is "this is the method we use", placed once, after the limitations** — never mid-method, and never inside the findings.
2. **At most one or two contextual commercial links** in the whole entry.
3. **The entry must survive its own commercial section being deleted.** If removing the commercial paragraph would leave the asset pointless, it was marketing.

**And the reason the restraint is commercial rather than modest:** the launch entries publish MappedSkills' own **measured zero** as evidence of measurement honesty. **A hard conversion push on that page contradicts the exact quality it is published to demonstrate.**

## 7. `/about`

Given the proof deficit, About does unusual work: **it is the entity anchor that branded AI answers are assembled from, and it is where specificity substitutes for scale.**

### 7.1 What it must establish

| # | Element | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Why MappedSkills exists** | The commercial belief behind the position — that the enquiry is the unit that matters and it is nobody's job in most arrangements. **Not a founding myth** |
| 2 | **Commercial philosophy** | What the firm will and will not be accountable for, and why it publishes its limits |
| 3 | **Approach** | Short. It links to `/how-it-works` rather than restating it |
| 4 | **People** | **Named individuals, real roles, genuinely relevant expertise.** Named authorship and visible founder expertise are among the few trust signals available |
| 5 | **Evidence** | The method and the research, as evidence of *how* the firm works rather than as results |
| 6 | **Location and entity facts** | Legal name, complete address, phone, email — **identical to `/contact`, the footer, schema and every external profile** |
| 7 | **Who it is for** | The fit criteria, in plain language |
| 8 | **What it refuses to promise** | Stated as a position |
| 9 | **Where the firm is today** | **The distinctive move: state plainly that the evidence base is being built in public.** Answered by specificity, not by inflation |

### 7.2 Owner-input dependencies — flagged, never invented

**`[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — legal entity name · complete registered postal address including a real postal code · phone · email · founding year]`** — note that AI systems report a founding year of **2016 from LinkedIn**, which is **NOT VERIFIED** in this programme.
**`[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — current team members, roles and genuine expertise]`** — a staff name surfaced by Google AI Mode is **NOT VERIFIED**.
**`[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — genuine company history and milestones]`**
**`[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — which social and professional profiles are current]`**
**`[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — any genuine partnerships, credentials, media features or awards]`**

**No founder biography, company history, credential, certification, award, partnership, milestone, client count or team size is invented.** Where the input is missing, the placeholder ships in the draft — never a plausible default.

## 8. Contact and booking language

**The form should feel like *tell us what you're trying to fix*, not *submit lead information*.**

### 8.1 `/contact`

| Element | Language principle |
|---|---|
| **Introduction** | One or two sentences. It asks for the situation, not the details — and it says what the person on the other end will do with it |
| **Field labels** | **Plain nouns describing the thing.** "What you're trying to fix", not "Message". "Work email", not "Email*". A one-line reason beside the company field, because it is the field most likely to be questioned. **Required fields are marked in words, not by an asterisk alone** |
| **The four required fields are unchanged** | Name · work email · company · what you're trying to fix. Phone/WhatsApp, website and marketing consent are optional. **Service or problem is pre-filled from page context and never asked. Budget is not asked** |
| **Privacy reassurance** | One short line adjacent to the submit control, linking the policy. **Marketing consent is separate, unticked and never bundled with submission** |
| **Response expectation** | **What happens, not when.** `[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — who responds and the business hours to publish]`. **No numeric SLA until one has been measured** |
| **Direct contact** | Phone and WhatsApp genuinely visible — a real conversion path in this market, not a fallback |
| **NAP** | Complete and identical to `/about`, the footer and schema. **The verified `postalCode: "India"` defect is fixed here and in schema together** |

### 8.2 `/schedule-call`

**The belief change is from "booking a call means a sales pitch" to "I know who I'm meeting, how long it takes, and what I get out of it even if I don't buy."**

The page states: what the call is · how long · **who is on it, by name** · what the visitor gets whether or not they proceed · what to bring · what it is not.

**`[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — who takes the calls · real availability · call length · business hours]`.** And **no "free audit" or "free strategy session" framing until the entry-offer model is decided.**

### 8.3 Failure-state tone

**Say what happened, in plain words. Keep everything the person typed. Offer a retry and a direct channel.**

Never: a false success · a silent failure · blame directed at the user · a technical error code as the whole message · humour. **Losing a typed message is the most severe recoverable defect this form can have**, and the tone should reflect that it was the site's failure, not the visitor's.

### 8.4 Success-state tone

**Confirm it arrived. Say what happens next. Offer one relevant next thing, and stop.**

Never: a second ask · a response-time number · an upsell · celebration language. **The conversion has happened; next-best content only.**

## 9. `/faq` — what belongs, and what does not

**FAQ resolves genuine buying objections.** Its second job is as a **first-party source surface** — branded AI answers about MappedSkills are assembled from what the site asserts, so an honest, scoped FAQ is an entity asset.

### 9.1 The inclusion test

A question earns a place only if **a real buyer has asked it, or it demonstrably stops a decision** — and only if the answer can be short and honest. **An honest answer that costs an enquiry is correct.**

### 9.2 Recommended launch questions — twelve, grouped

**No answers are written here.**

**Commercial**
1. What does this cost, and what changes that number?
2. Is ad spend separate from your fee?
3. What is the minimum commitment?

**Outcome**
4. What are you accountable for — and what will you not promise?
5. Why won't you guarantee results?
6. How long before anything changes?

**Measurement**
7. How do you know an enquiry came from your work — and what can't you tell?
8. What counts as a qualified enquiry here, and who decides?
9. What access do you need, and what do we keep?

**Fit**
10. Who is this for, and who is it not for?

**AI search**
11. Can you get our business into ChatGPT and AI answers?
12. Is GEO or AEO a separate discipline from SEO?

*If a thirteenth is needed, "What happens after I enquire?" is the strongest candidate — but it is currently owner-blocked and may be better answered on `/contact`.*

### 9.3 What `/faq` must not become

**SEO FAQ spam** — questions written to match query phrasings rather than because buyers ask them · **a definitions page** — the definitional SERPs are institutionally owned and a glossary is on the DO NOT BUILD list · **a duplicate service page** — it summarises and links, it does not restate `/how-it-works` or `/pricing` · **schema bait** — `FAQPage` markup must match the visible content, **the page currently emits schema for one Question against a larger on-page set**, and rich-result eligibility must be re-verified at implementation (Project Rule 19).

**And one correctness requirement:** no answer may contradict `/how-it-works` or `/pricing`. **Systems reading this site have already been observed reporting contradictory service descriptions from it.**
