## 1. Governing language principles

1. **A CTA names what happens next, not what the visitor will get.** "Tell us what you're trying to fix" describes an action. "Get more leads" promises an outcome and is prohibited.
2. **Commitment must match the visitor's state.** A PRIMARY CTA at the top of an article converts nobody and costs the read.
3. **No CTA promises an outcome** — no enquiries, rankings, citations, revenue or ROI.
4. **No CTA references proof that does not exist.** No "see our case studies", no "read client results". `/work` is `noindex` and out of navigation until one is permissioned.
5. **No CTA states a response time** until one has been measured.
6. **No urgency that is not real.** No countdowns, no "limited slots", no scarcity, no exit-intent, no confirmshaming on a decline.
7. **Model-agnostic wording.** The free/paid/hybrid entry decision is open. **Any CTA implying a free diagnostic, a free audit or a free strategy session is a placeholder until that decision is made.**
8. **The CTA and the destination agree.** A button promising a diagnostic must lead somewhere that delivers one.
9. **Mechanism-independent at the outcome level.** Form and booking are two doors to one goal; a page may offer both, and must not present a second competing goal.
10. **Direct contact is always visible** — phone and WhatsApp are real conversion paths in this market, not fallbacks.

## 2. When "Contact us" is too generic

**"Contact us" carries no information about what the contact is for, what happens next, or why now.** It is acceptable in exactly two places: **utility navigation** and **the footer**, where its job is navigational rather than persuasive.

**It is too generic — and therefore wrong — wherever:**
- the page has already established a specific need or symptom;
- the visitor arrived on a commercial or problem page and could be addressed in their own terms;
- it appears as the primary control on a capability page, a vertical page or `/pricing`.

**The replacement rule:** the CTA reflects what the page just established. On `/seo`, it is about search work. On the problem page, it is about finding where the enquiries are going. **The specificity is what makes the click meaningful and the enquiry better.**

## 3. When "Book a call" is appropriate

- **`/pricing`** — price-stage buyers want a person, and booking is emphasised.
- **`/industries/manufacturing`** *(conditional)* — this segment calls.
- **`/schedule-call`** — it is the surface.
- Anywhere the visitor has signalled they would rather talk than write.

**It is not appropriate** at the top of an article, on the problem page above the fold, on `/research` or `/how-it-works` mid-page, or as the only route on `/contact`.

## 4. When a diagnostic framing is appropriate

**Where the visitor is problem-aware but not vendor-selecting** — primarily `/problems/traffic-but-no-enquiries`, and secondarily `/conversion-optimization` and the enquiry-loss article.

**The framing offers to find something, not to sell something.** It converts the reader's own uncertainty into a specific next step, and it earns itself only after the page has already given them something usable.

**All diagnostic wording is blocked on the entry-offer decision.** Until it is made, every candidate below carries `[DECISION REQUIRED — entry-offer model: free / paid / hybrid]`.

## 5. Candidate phrasings

**Candidates only. Maximum five per role. None is approved, and final selection belongs to the copywriting session under the message quality gate.**

### PRIMARY — create a qualified enquiry

1. "Tell us what you're trying to fix"
2. "Start an enquiry"
3. "Tell us about your enquiries"
4. "Send us your situation"
5. "Talk to us about your site"

*Note: candidate 1 matches the required form field exactly, which makes the button and the first field one continuous thought. That is a deliberate advantage and the reason it is listed first.*

### PRIMARY — booking surface *(the "meeting CTA")*

1. "Book a call"
2. "Book a time to talk"
3. "Arrange a call"
4. "Book a call `[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — call length]`"
5. "Speak to `[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — who takes the calls]`"

*Notes: candidates 4 and 5 are stronger than the generic forms and are **blocked** until the owner confirms the call format and who takes it. Do not resolve either by guessing. No candidate may state availability or a response time.*

### DIAGNOSTIC — find where demand is being lost

1. "Find out where your enquiries are going"
2. "Have us look at your enquiry path"
3. "Ask us to check the four places it breaks"
4. "Get a read on where the loss is"
5. "Have us run this on your site"

*All five carry `[DECISION REQUIRED — entry-offer model]`. **None may say "free"** until that decision is recorded.*

### PROOF — see the method or the evidence

1. "Read how we work"
2. "See the method, including what we can't attribute"
3. "How we measure this"
4. "See what we publish"
5. "Read the method"

*A PROOF CTA is an **offer, never a gate.** No page may interpose it between itself and the conversion.*

### RESEARCH — open or subscribe

1. "Read the method and the run counts"
2. "Open the baseline"
3. "See how this was measured"
4. "Get new research when we publish it"
5. "Read the full measurement"

*No email wall, no download form, no gate. A research asset that requires an email to read is a lead gate and will be judged as one.*

### CONTEXTUAL — in-content navigation

1. "Where this fits in the whole path"
2. "This is one of four things it could be"
3. "The same problem, from the search side"
4. "How we measure this part"
5. "If this sounds like your situation"

*Contextual CTAs are placed where the relevant point is made, not at a fixed interval, and they describe the destination rather than the keyword.*

### LOW-COMMITMENT — the editorial CTA

1. "Read the diagnostic guide"
2. "See the related problem page"
3. "Read the measurement this is based on"
4. "The longer version, with the method"
5. "Check yours the same way"

*Placed **after** the answer is delivered. **No sticky CTA, no booking prompt, no modal, no exit-intent on any article.***

### DIRECT — call or WhatsApp

1. "Call `[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — published phone number]`"
2. "WhatsApp us"
3. "Message us on WhatsApp"
4. "Call us `[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — business hours]`"
5. "Phone `[OWNER INPUT REQUIRED — published phone number]`"

*A phone or email click is a **conversion-intent event, never a qualified enquiry.** CTA labels must not imply an outcome, and no hours may be published until the owner confirms them.*

## 6. What CTAs must never say

| Prohibited CTA | Why |
|---|---|
| "Get a free audit" / "Free strategy session" | The entry-offer model is undecided, and every competitor examined offers a free audit — which prices the analysis at zero |
| "See our case studies" / "View client results" | **None exists.** `/work` is `noindex` and out of navigation |
| "Get more leads" / "Start generating enquiries" | Promises an outcome MappedSkills does not guarantee |
| "Get your AI visibility score" | The score is rejected on three independent bases, including methodologically |
| "Rank #1 in ChatGPT" / "Get cited by AI" *(as a CTA)* | Guarantees nothing can support |
| "We'll reply within 24 hours" | **No numeric SLA until one has been measured** |
| "Limited slots" / "Book before [date]" / any countdown | Manufactured urgency. Prohibited by the approved CTA rules |
| "Don't lose more leads" / "Stop wasting your budget" | Fear framing, and it asserts an unverified diagnosis about the reader |
| "Download the guide" *(gated)* | A gate presented as a resource will be judged as one |
| "Learn more" / "Click here" / "Read more" | Carry no information about the destination |
