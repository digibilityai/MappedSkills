## 1. The internal positioning statement — three candidates

A positioning statement here is a **writer's instrument**. Its job is to let any future writer check a sentence against a single agreed articulation of who this is for, what problem it addresses, what outcome it creates, how it works, why it differs, and what it does not promise.

**None of these is a headline. None introduces a new company category. All three preserve the meaning of `DEC-005`.**

### Candidate A — Accountable to the enquiry

> **CORRECTED 2026-09-01 (orchestrator correction pass 1 and 2).** The original opening read *"For considered-purchase businesses in India whose buyers are already searching…"*, which made **being findable in search an eligibility condition for the buyer** — narrowing the approved definition. It also read *"one accountable system rather than three suppliers' problems"*, which asserted an engagement model that remains **owner-blocked**. Both are corrected below. **The original wording is recorded here rather than erased.**

> For considered-purchase businesses where each qualified opportunity has meaningful economic value — and where demand for what they sell already exists or can reasonably be reached — MappedSkills captures that demand and turns it into qualified enquiries the business can count. It connects the work that brings buyers in, the path they take to enquire, and the measurement that shows what happened, so that a break between them is somebody's job rather than nobody's. The boundary is published: MappedSkills owns the chain up to the qualified enquiry, reports through to revenue using the client's own close data, and states plainly what cannot be attributed. It does not promise rankings, AI citations, enquiry counts or revenue.

**Answers:** who (**considered-purchase, meaningful value per opportunity, demand exists or can be reached — the approved definition, unnarrowed**) · problem (demand is not reaching them) · outcome (qualified enquiries, countable) · approach (the parts are connected and the joins are owned) · difference (published accountability boundary) · not promised (four named things).

**Strength:** it states the approved outcome directly, it carries the accountability boundary as the differentiator rather than as a caveat, and every clause is defensible under the claims rules today.

**Weaknesses, both recorded:**
- **"Accountable" is a word agencies also use loosely**, and it only works if the boundary is genuinely published — which makes `/how-it-works` load-bearing, and that page is owner-blocked.
- **It is an internal instrument, not buyer-readable language.** The hero stress test in `HOMEPAGE_MESSAGE_ARCHITECTURE.md` §5 found that "accountability" is a governance word before it is a commercial one. **That is a finding about the *expression*, not the *content*** — the underlying idea survived the test and the hero was reframed in ordinary words. This statement stays as written because its audience is future writers, not buyers.

### Candidate B — Demand you already have

> MappedSkills is for considered-purchase businesses where one good enquiry is worth real money, and where buyers for what they sell already exist. It exists to close the gap between that demand and the enquiries the business actually receives — reaching those buyers, making the enquiry path work, and measuring the whole route so the result can be checked rather than believed. **How the demand is reached is decided per client — organic search is the primary route, and paid is a legitimate one.** It does not create demand where none exists, and it does not promise revenue, rankings or citations.

**Strength:** it is the clearest statement of demand *capture* rather than demand *creation*, which is the actual ICP boundary (`ICP.md` §2 condition 3). It also disqualifies the wrong buyer in its first sentence.

**Weakness:** it defines the firm partly by what it is not, and "demand you already have" can read as an assertion about the reader's market that MappedSkills cannot verify before a diagnostic.

**Correction note (2026-09-01):** the original wording — *"businesses whose buyers are already looking… making the business findable at the moment of the search"* — made **search the route by which the buyer qualifies**. Corrected. Demand existing is the condition; **search is the primary way of reaching it, not a test of whether a business belongs in the market.**

### Candidate C — Where the demand is being lost

> MappedSkills finds where a business is losing the demand it already has — before it is found, on the page, in the enquiry path, or in the measurement — then fixes what it found and measures the result. Its unit of accountability is the qualified enquiry, not the ranking, the click or the report. It does not guarantee outcomes it does not control, and it says which parts of the result cannot be attributed.

**Strength:** it is the sharpest and most buyer-recognisable, it maps directly onto the four loss zones that the problem page and `/conversion-optimization` both use, and it converts the proof deficit into a diagnostic posture.

**Weakness:** **it positions the firm as a diagnostic practice rather than a delivery engagement.** The approved model is entry → **core** → expansion, where the core is a recurring engagement that implements. Making diagnosis the company definition would quietly demote the core.

### Recommendation

**Recommend Candidate A**, with Candidate C's four-loss-zone framing retained as the **entry-offer and diagnostic frame**, not as the company frame.

Reasoning, in order: A states the approved outcome without translation and preserves `DEC-005`'s meaning most exactly; it is the only candidate whose differentiator (a published accountability boundary) is both true today and unclaimed by any competitor examined; and it holds Level 1 of the hierarchy without borrowing a capability. B's demand-capture boundary is preserved inside A's first clause and belongs in the ICP-facing sections rather than the company statement. C is the strongest *language* and the wrong *scope* — it is adopted for `/problems/traffic-but-no-enquiries`, `/conversion-optimization` and the diagnostic CTA, where it does real work.

**And one thing this statement is explicitly not.** A is a **writer's instrument in strategic register**. It is **not** the buyer-facing hero, and the hero must not be derived from it by compression — the approved outcome phrase *"measurable qualified enquiries"* is a strategic foundation and **must not become a slogan**. The buyer-facing expression is decided separately in `HOMEPAGE_MESSAGE_ARCHITECTURE.md` §5, in ordinary words.

**Claude does not approve this recommendation.** It requires an owner decision recorded in `DECISION_LOG.md`.

## 2. "What does MappedSkills do?" — three versions of one answer

All three describe the same business. They differ in length and in the vocabulary the listener already has. **No slogans. No jargon. No version may contain a claim the others contradict.**

### Plain version

> We help businesses get found by the buyers already looking for what they sell, turn more of those visits into real enquiries, and measure the whole path so they can see what worked.

Use when: a prospect asks in conversation · a founder introduces the company · a referral partner needs one line · a listener has no marketing vocabulary.

### Slightly expanded version

> We work with businesses that sell something people take time to decide on — where one good enquiry is worth real money. There are usually buyers out there for what they sell; most never reach the business, and of those who do, too few are worth quoting and nobody can say where they came from. We work on all of it together: reaching those buyers, the enquiry actually arriving, and the measurement that tells you which part is working. We're accountable up to the qualified enquiry. We report through to revenue using your own close data, and we say plainly what can't be attributed.

Use when: a journalist asks · a prospect wants the fuller answer · an About page or a directory profile needs a paragraph · an AI system needs an accurate self-description to extract.

### Search-aware version

> MappedSkills is a demand-capture and enquiry-generation firm in Pune, India. The work involves SEO and organic search, AI search, Google Ads and paid social where they are the right demand source, improving the path from visit to enquiry, and the measurement and attribution that connects them. **Which of those a given engagement includes is decided per client; the measurement is not optional.** The outcome we are accountable for is qualified enquiries, measured.

Use when: a page or profile must be legible to someone searching in capability vocabulary · a directory listing asks what services are offered · an entity description needs to name the capabilities accurately.

**Rule attached to the search-aware version:** it names capabilities because the context requires them, and it is the **only** version in which capability vocabulary leads. It still ends on the outcome, and it still does not call the company an SEO agency, an AI-search agency or a performance marketing agency.

### What none of the three may become

No version may add: a superlative · a number · a client reference · a guarantee · an acronym (GEO, AEO, LLMO, CRO) · an industry ("for manufacturers") · a technique category as the noun describing the company.

### 3.3 Aspirational differentiators — wanted, not claimable

### 3.5 Engagement-model absolutes — prohibited *(added 2026-09-01, orchestrator correction pass 2)*

**The approved strategy describes acquisition, conversion and measurement as the parts of one commercial system.** It does **not** settle packaging, tiering, scope per client, or what any individual engagement contains — `OFFER_ARCHITECTURE.md` §9 leaves offer names, packaging and tiering explicitly undecided, and **the real delivery process is owner-blocked**.

**Five absolutes must therefore not be asserted or implied in any messaging:**

1. **That every client buys all three.**
2. **That every engagement bundles all three**, or that the parts cannot be scoped. *(The approved `/services` brief requires the honest answer that parts can be scoped, and why fixing one alone rarely changes the number.)*
3. **That MappedSkills replaces three suppliers.**
4. **That paid media is part of every engagement.** It is chosen on merit per client, and `/social-media-ads` may say outright that a channel is wrong for a business.
5. **That a particular engagement structure has been approved.** None has.

**What may be said, and is defensible today:** the parts are connected rather than handed off · MappedSkills takes responsibility for the joins between them, so a break between disciplines is somebody's job · **the measurement is the part that is not optional**, because nothing outcome-based may be sold or reported before it works · and the scope of any engagement is decided per client.

**The claim that must not be smuggled back in:** that the loss *is* in the seams. **That is a hypothesis, not a measured finding** — the approved diagnostic explicitly holds that the loss may be in demand, on the page, in the path, or in the measurement, and the problem page's whole credibility rests on not pre-judging which.

### 3.4 The uniqueness rule

**Nothing is described as unique, first, only or proprietary.** Session 02 recorded that named frameworks are table stakes, and `POSITIONING.md` §8 names the things that must never be called a moat: a framework name, a score, tool subscriptions, service breadth, office locations, years in business, being early to terminology, and the measurement-honesty position on its own.

Where a genuine scarcity observation exists it is stated as an **observation with its scope attached** — for example, that no competitor examined in this programme publishes a methodology — never as "the only firm that…".

## 6. Trust language — how to state limits without sounding weak

MappedSkills' position requires it to say a lot of things other agencies do not say. Done badly this reads as hedging. Done well it is the differentiator. **Six principles.**

### Principle 1 — A limit stated with specificity reads as competence; a limit stated with vagueness reads as doubt

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "Enquiries that arrive by phone or direct typing usually carry no source at all. We record those as unattributed rather than assigning them to a channel." | "Attribution is never perfect and results may vary." |
| "We measured this across four systems on one day, signed out. We could not test Claude or Grok at all." | "Our research suggests…" |

### Principle 2 — Say the limit once, at full strength, in the right place

The named attribution limitations belong on `/how-it-works`, in one visible section that is designed to be read. They do **not** belong as a repeated caveat under every mention of measurement. **Defensive disclaimers everywhere destroy the value of stating a limit anywhere.**

### Principle 3 — Never apologise for a limit, and never bury it in legalese

A limit is a position: *"We measure what can be measured and label what cannot."* Not: *"MappedSkills makes no warranty as to the completeness of attribution data."*

### Principle 4 — Uncertainty is expressed as a named unknown, not as a softening adverb

Write **UNKNOWN**, or name what is missing and who could supply it. Do not write "potentially", "typically", "often" or "up to" in place of a figure that does not exist.

### Principle 5 — Forecasting states mechanism and horizon, never a number

Permitted: what would be worked on first, what would be expected to change and in what order, and how long before anything is observable — with the assumptions named.
Prohibited: a projected enquiry count, a projected traffic figure, a projected ranking, a payback period, a modelled ROI, or a "clients typically see" statement.

### Principle 6 — Client-supplied data is always attributed to the client's system

Revenue and close data are reported as *the client's* numbers, from *the client's* system, with what MappedSkills did and did not control stated. MappedSkills never presents them as its own measurement.

### Applied to the recurring cases

| Case | Language principle |
|---|---|
| **Attribution limitations** | Name each limitation specifically. State the governing rule: a measurement gap never disqualifies a legitimate enquiry |
| **AI variability** | Report frequency across runs, per system, with run counts. Say plainly that "rank in AI" is not a real number. Never blend systems |
| **Expected results** | Mechanism and horizon only. **Organic is 12 months-plus, and that is stated up front rather than softened** |
| **Experiments** | Distinguish evidence-informed improvement from controlled experimentation, and say which one the client's traffic can support |
| **Untested systems** | Name them. Claude and Grok have no baseline of any kind, and every AI asset says so |
| **Response time** | No public numeric commitment until one has been measured. Say what happens, not when |
| **One measurement** | "One measurement is not a trend" — stated in the asset, not implied by omission |
