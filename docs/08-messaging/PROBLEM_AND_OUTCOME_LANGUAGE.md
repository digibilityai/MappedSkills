## 1. Problem architecture

### 1.1 The primary problem — the only one MappedSkills leads with

In the buyer's own words, with no marketing taxonomy:

> "Buyers who need what we sell are searching. Most never reach us, too few of those who do are worth quoting, and we cannot tell where any of them came from."

**Three faces, one problem: not found → not converted → not measured.**

**The rule that makes this a single problem rather than three:** fixing one alone changes nothing the buyer can observe. More traffic on a broken path produces nothing; a perfect path fed the wrong traffic produces nothing worth quoting; and without measurement the business cannot tell which failure it has. **Messaging must never split this into three problems sold separately** — that is the service-menu framing the approved strategy exists to replace.

### 1.2 Secondary problems — real, ownable, never the lead

Each is legitimate on the page where the buyer arrives with it. **None may become the company-level problem statement.**

| Secondary problem | Where it is owned | The constraint on it |
|---|---|---|
| **Traffic exists but enquiries do not** | `/problems/traffic-but-no-enquiries` · `/conversion-optimization` | It is a **symptom with four possible causes**, and the page must not assume the conversion one |
| **Enquiries exist but nobody can say where they came from** | `/how-it-works` · launch article 2 · `/lead-generation` | Never framed as a problem MappedSkills solves completely — some enquiries carry no usable trail at all |
| **Marketing produces activity but little commercial visibility** | `/services` · `/how-it-works` | Must not slide into "reporting" as a product. Measurement is a foundation, not a deliverable |
| **AI systems do not surface the business** | `/ai-seo` · launch article 1 | **A credibility hook and an expansion problem, not an entry problem.** It is not why a business picks up the phone, and messaging must not inflate it |
| **Paid acquisition cost per enquiry is rising** | `/google-ads` | A common entry symptom. Answered by distinguishing the account from the landing experience from the enquiry path from the measurement — **with no cost figure of any kind** |
| **Enquiries are not followed up quickly or consistently** | One sentence on `/services` and `/how-it-works` | **Expansion territory only.** `how to improve lead response time` returns **(NONE)** — there is no demand to address, and it gets no page and no cluster |

### 1.3 Symptoms — recognition language, not problem statements

These are how a buyer describes their situation before naming a discipline. They belong in problem-page and diagnostic copy so a reader can self-identify. **They are not the company's problem framing and must not be elevated.**

Sessions are up and enquiries are flat · enquiries stopped after a site change · enquiries only ever come from one channel · the enquiries we do get are not worth quoting · we do not know whether anything arrives at all · we rank for things and nothing happens · we spend on ads and cannot trace the return · we typed our own company into ChatGPT and found nothing.

### 1.4 Problems MappedSkills does not claim to solve

**Stated so that no future page quietly acquires one of them.**

1. **Sales performance after the enquiry** — follow-up discipline, qualification skill, negotiation, close rate. MappedSkills owns the chain **up to** the qualified enquiry.
2. **Creating demand where none exists.** The proposition is demand *capture*. A business whose market is not searching and cannot be reached through capture-shaped channels is a documented disqualifier (`ICP.md` §4).
3. **Revenue, pricing, product and capacity.** All are outside MappedSkills' control, which is exactly why revenue is reported and never promised.
4. **CRM implementation, RevOps, lifecycle architecture and sales-operations transformation.** Excluded entirely from scope (`SERVICE_ARCHITECTURE.md` §8).
5. **Brand awareness, reputation management and PR as an outcome.** Not acquisition capabilities here.
6. **Ecommerce merchandising and ecommerce conversion.** A different discipline, a different measurement model and a different unit of value (`ICP.md` §6).

**Two more, recorded because they are the tempting ones:** MappedSkills does not solve *"we are not on page one for our category term in Pune"* — that surface is decided by tenure, reviews and Google Business Profile, and no page wins it. And it does not solve *"we are not cited by AI"* as a standalone commercial problem — the mechanism is largely off-site, and access is demonstrably not sufficient.

### 1.5 The rule against manufacturing problems

**Not every capability gets a problem statement.** Measurement, attribution, automation and paid social all exist as capabilities without a buyer-recognised problem behind them in this market's evidence. Inventing one — "your attribution is broken", "your follow-up is too slow" — produces a problem statement with no demand behind it and reads as a manufactured need. Where a capability has no buyer problem, **it is described by its contribution to the chain, not by a problem it rescues the buyer from.**

## 2. Outcome architecture — four strict language tiers

### Tier 1 — Outcomes MappedSkills MAY state confidently

Stated as what the firm does and is accountable for. No quantification.

- We work to increase the number of **qualified enquiries** a business receives, and we measure them.
- We are **accountable up to the qualified enquiry**.
- We **instrument the path** — source, landing context, interaction, enquiry — and report against a baseline we capture.
- We **publish what cannot be attributed**.
- We **measure AI-assisted discovery per system**, with run counts, and publish the method.
- We **measured our own AI visibility and published the result, including a zero** *(first-party fact)*.
- We **diagnose which of four failures is losing a business its enquiries** before recommending a fix.

### Tier 2 — Outcomes MappedSkills may state conditionally

Permitted only with the stated condition attached, in the same sentence or the one after.

| Outcome | The condition that must travel with it |
|---|---|
| Improved **rankings** or **search visibility** | Named as a **diagnostic proxy, not the outcome** — and never quantified, promised or given a timeframe |
| More, or better-quality, **traffic** | Only as "the right traffic", and only alongside the point that traffic alone changes nothing |
| **Faster** results through paid media | Only as a role paid plays, with no figure, no cost claim and no implication that every client needs it |
| **Revenue** | Only as *reported* — from the client's own close data, attributed to the client's system, with what could not be isolated stated |
| Improvement in **AI answer presence** | Only as work on entity and source authority, with **no guarantee of appearance, mention, citation or position**, and with the systems that were not tested named |
| **Conversion improvement** | Only as evidence-informed change. **Not** as a percentage, not as a benchmark, and not as A/B testing where traffic cannot support significance |
| **Faster response to enquiries** | Only as routing and notification capability. **No numeric SLA until one has been measured** |

### Tier 3 — Outcomes requiring evidence visible on the page

If the evidence is not on the page or one click away, **the sentence is not written**.

- Any **number**, without exception — carrying sample size, date, location and method.
- Any statement about **what MappedSkills has achieved**, for itself or anyone else.
- Any statement about **a competitor**, the **market**, or **what a technique produces**.
- Any **comparison** — including "unlike most agencies".
- Any statement about **AI system behaviour**, which is dated, per-system and re-verifiable.

### Tier 4 — Outcomes MappedSkills must NOT promise, anywhere, in any form

Guaranteed rankings or positions · guaranteed traffic · guaranteed enquiry or lead counts · guaranteed cost per enquiry or cost per lead · guaranteed conversion-rate improvement · guaranteed AI citations, mentions, recommendations or "rank in ChatGPT" · an AI Visibility Score, an average AI rank or any blended cross-system figure · pipeline value · revenue · ROI or ROAS · payback period · a "typical result" · statistical significance the client's traffic cannot support · a published response-time SLA · **"300%+ ROI", "₹100Cr+", "₹10Cr+"** (`DEC-007`).

### 2.1 The per-term ruling

| Term | May MappedSkills use it? | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Traffic** | Yes, carefully | As an input, never the outcome. "The right traffic." Never a promised figure or increase |
| **Rankings** | Yes, sparingly | As a diagnostic proxy. Never guaranteed, never quantified, never the headline of a result |
| **Visibility** | Yes, as plain language | "Being found when buyers search." **Never as a category noun** — "search visibility" is owned by other industries |
| **Enquiries** | **Yes — the primary buyer-facing outcome word** | Freely, and as the default noun in commercial copy |
| **Qualified enquiries** | **Yes — the accountable outcome** | Wherever qualification is, or can immediately be, explained. See §3 |
| **Leads** | Restricted | Only where the query requires it — page titles, metadata and matching phrasing on `/lead-generation`. **Never as MappedSkills' own outcome word** |
| **Opportunities** | Restricted | Only when describing the **client's** downstream stage in a measurement explanation. Never as something the website produces |
| **Pipeline** | **No** | US SaaS/RevOps register, and it names a stage MappedSkills does not own |
| **Revenue** | Yes — reported only | From the client's own close data, attributed to the client's system, never promised |
| **ROI** | **No** | Not stated, not implied, not calculated, not illustrated. The prohibited production claim lives in this category |
| **AI recommendations** | Yes, descriptively | To describe what an AI answer does. **Never as something MappedSkills can secure** |
| **AI citations** | Yes, descriptively | As a measurable observation with run counts. **Never as a promised outcome, and never as a service name** |

## 3. "Qualified enquiry" — how it appears in buyer-facing language

### 3.1 Is it understandable without explanation?

**Partly, and not enough to carry a headline unassisted.** "Enquiry" is understood by every Indian business buyer. "Qualified" is understood as a general idea — *the ones worth having* — but it carries an implied claim: **that someone decided, on some basis, which ones count.** Left unexplained it reads as either self-serving or as jargon.

**The governing rule:**

> **"Enquiries" is the default word. "Qualified enquiries" is used wherever the page can immediately say what qualification means — and it is always used where MappedSkills states what it is accountable for.**

### 3.2 When to say which

| Say | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **"enquiries"** | Homepage first screen · capability pages · problem page · editorial · CTA language · `/contact`, `/schedule-call` | Immediately legible, no explanation debt |
| **"qualified enquiries"** | Any statement of accountability · `/services` · `/how-it-works` · `/lead-generation` · `/pricing` · `/faq` · reporting language | It is the accountable unit and the honest one — and each of these pages can explain it within a screen |
| **"the enquiries worth quoting" / "enquiries you can act on"** | Where the idea is needed but a definition would interrupt | A plain-language gloss that does not create explanation debt |

### 3.3 When qualification must be explained

Whenever "qualified" appears as the noun MappedSkills is accountable for — and always on `/lead-generation`, `/services`, `/how-it-works`, `/pricing` and `/faq`.

**What the explanation must contain, in buyer language:**
1. What the website can check — that the enquiry is real, reachable, from a business context, with a stated need, and not a duplicate.
2. What the website deliberately does not judge — **whether the business is a good fit, and what an opportunity is worth. Both are decided in conversation, not by a form.**
3. What is excluded — job applications, course enquiries, vendor pitches, student requests, and requests for work MappedSkills does not do.
4. That measurement gaps never change the answer — an enquiry whose source could not be traced is still a qualified enquiry.

**What the explanation must never claim:** that the form qualifies on budget or deal size · that a score exists · that qualification predicts whether a prospect will buy.

### 3.4 Should "lead" be used?

**Not as MappedSkills' own word.** The outcome noun is *enquiry*.

**Permitted:** on `/lead-generation` — in the page title, metadata and where the buyer's phrasing genuinely requires it — because `lead generation agency` is the live Indian phrasing for that intent and the page exists to capture it (`DEC-008`).

**Prohibited:** "leads" as the thing MappedSkills delivers · "lead scoring" *(no model exists; inventing weights against zero historical enquiries would be fabricated precision)* · "lead quality score" · "MQL", "SQL", "lead nurturing" · any phrasing implying purchased or scraped lists.

### 3.5 When does "opportunity" become appropriate?

Only in the **measurement explanation**, describing the client's own downstream stage: *"where your close data allows, we join enquiries back to the opportunities and revenue your system recorded."*

**Never** as a website outcome, never as a count MappedSkills produces, and never in a CTA.

### 3.6 Internal analytics language vs marketing copy

**Hard separation. Internal vocabulary does not appear in buyer-facing copy.**

| Internal — never in copy | Buyer-facing equivalent |
|---|---|
| Website-Qualified Enquiry (WQE) · Sales-Qualified Enquiry (SQE) | "a qualified enquiry" · "a fit we take further after a conversation" |
| `qualification_status`, `attribution_status` | "whether the enquiry counts" · "whether we can tell where it came from" |
| `lead_form_submitted`, `meeting_booked` | "an enquiry" · "a booked call" |
| Review Required · hard disqualifier · soft signal | "we check by hand where it is not clear" · "what we do not treat as an enquiry" |
| first-touch · conversion-touch | "how they first found you" · "what they were looking at when they enquired" |
| `attribution_status = unavailable` | "arrived with no usable trail — recorded as unattributed" |

**One exception, deliberate:** `/how-it-works` and the research entries may name a technical term **once, defined in place**, because their audience includes people who will check the method. Everywhere else, the plain form is used.

## 4. Two language positions that are load-bearing

**"Measurable" does not mean "only what we could measure counts."** `QUALIFIED_ENQUIRY_DEFINITION.md` §2A is explicit that qualification and attribution completeness are independent. Copy must never imply that an untraceable enquiry is a lesser one. The correct sentence shape is: *"we count it, and we tell you we could not see where it came from."*

**"Measured" is not a synonym for "proven".** MappedSkills has one AI measurement round and no analytics baseline of its own. Nothing is described as proven, validated or established on that basis.

## 5. India-first language

Primary market is India, with Pune and the Maharashtra industrial corridor as the credibility base. **The register is ordinary Indian business English — not US SaaS, not RevOps, and not simplified.**

### 5.1 Rulings

| Term | Ruling | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **enquiry** *(not "inquiry")* | **Use.** The site standard, everywhere, including "enquiry form", "enquiry path", "qualified enquiry" | British/Indian convention and consistent with the whole programme's vocabulary |
| **lead** | Restricted — query-matching contexts only | §3.4 |
| **prospect** | Avoid in buyer copy | Sales-desk vocabulary. Say "a buyer", "a business", "someone who enquired" |
| **opportunity** | Restricted — client's downstream stage only | §3.5 |
| **pipeline** | **Avoid** | US SaaS register, and a stage MappedSkills does not own |
| **conversion** | Use as an ordinary word with an object — "turning visits into enquiries" | Avoid "conversions" as a bare plural noun and "conversion rate" as a headline metric |
| **CRO** | **Prohibited in body copy** | In India the acronym resolves to pharmaceutical Contract Research Organisations. Metadata use is confined to what the residual query genuinely requires |
| **attribution** | Use — with a plain gloss at first use | "attribution — where an enquiry came from". Necessary technical vocabulary, not jargon to be avoided |
| **demand generation** | **Avoid** | Collapses into "lead generation agency" in Indian autocomplete; it is not the live phrasing |
| **search visibility** | **Avoid as a noun-category.** Use "being found when buyers search" | The term is owned by social-profile visibility settings in this market |
| **AI visibility** | **Avoid as a service or category name.** Use "showing up in AI answers" | The query resolves to free tools, not agencies |
| **performance marketing** | **Prohibited as identity** | MappedSkills' current, non-performing category |
| **RFQ** | **Use, on the conditional manufacturing page only** | The buyer's own word; all three AI systems tested used it unprompted |
| **WhatsApp** | Use freely as a real contact and follow-up channel | It is an ordinary business channel in this market, not an informal one |
| **lakh / crore, ₹** | Use Indian numbering conventions for any owner-confirmed figure | Never for an invented figure |

### 5.2 Register rules

1. **Do not dumb down necessary technical terms.** "Attribution", "structured data", "indexation", "retrieval" and "canonical" are the right words on the pages where they belong. Define once, in place, in one sentence.
2. **Do not import US SaaS or RevOps vocabulary where ordinary Indian business language is clearer.** Prohibited by register: pipeline · GTM motion · ICP *(internal only)* · funnel velocity · revenue operations · north-star metric · flywheel · playbook *(as a product)* · "book a demo" *(there is no product to demo)*.
3. **Spelling:** British/Indian in prose — optimisation, organisation, analyse, programme, prioritise. **American spelling is preserved only where it is already a URL, a code identifier, or a query phrase the page must match** — `/conversion-optimization` stays as it is, and metadata may carry `conversion rate optimization`.
4. **Avoid Indian-market clichés as much as US ones:** "one-stop solution", "best-in-class", "kindly", "do the needful", "revert back", "as per".
5. **Currency and time:** state a figure only where the owner has confirmed it; state a horizon only where the evidence supports it.
