**Rules, not adjectives.** A voice document that only lists qualities cannot be applied or checked. Everything below is either a rule a writer can follow or a test an editor can run.

## 1. What MappedSkills should sound like

**Commercially intelligent · clear · confident · technically credible · evidence-aware · modern · concise · useful.**

And one quality the evidence made load-bearing: **verifiable.** The distinguishing property of this position is that a reader can check the claims.

**It should not sound:** corporate · pompous · breathless · hype-driven · academic · AI-generated · jargon-heavy · overly clever · childish · combative.

**The working definition that makes those operational:**

> Write as a competent practitioner explaining something to a busy business owner who is paying attention, has been let down before, and will check.

---

## 2. Sentence style

1. **One idea per sentence.** Where a sentence carries two, split it.
2. **Declarative by default.** State the thing. Qualify after, in its own sentence, at full strength.
3. **Vary length deliberately.** Most sentences short. A longer one where a genuine relationship between ideas needs holding together. **A page of uniformly short sentences reads as a pitch; a page of uniformly long ones reads as a report.**
4. **Active voice**, except where the actor is genuinely irrelevant or unknown ("the form was never wired to a backend").
5. **Front-load the subject.** The first six words should tell the reader what the sentence is about.
6. **No sentence longer than about 35 words**, and no paragraph in which every sentence is over 25.
7. **Cut every intensifier that carries no information** — very, really, extremely, incredibly, truly, highly *(except in the approved phrase "highly visible supporting capability", which is a strategy term, not marketing copy)*.
8. **No sentence may need a second reading to parse.** If it does, the problem is the sentence, not the reader.

## 3. Paragraph length

| Context | Target |
|---|---|
| Commercial and problem pages | **1–3 sentences.** Four is the maximum |
| `/how-it-works`, research, editorial | **2–5 sentences**, where the argument genuinely needs development |
| `/contact`, `/schedule-call` | **1–2 sentences.** The visitor has decided |
| Limits, boundaries and what is not promised | **One paragraph, stated once, at full strength.** Never split across the page to soften it |

**No wall of text, anywhere.** If a section runs past four paragraphs without a heading, a table, a list or a figure, the structure is wrong.

## 4. Heading style

1. **Sentence case.** Not Title Case, not ALL CAPS.
2. **Headings describe the content beneath them.** A reader who reads only the headings should get the argument.
3. **No puns, no wordplay, no teasers.** A heading that withholds what the section says is costing the skim-reader and the retrieval system simultaneously.
4. **No single-word headings** ("Approach", "Results", "Solutions") — they carry nothing.
5. **Question headings only where the question is a real buyer question**, in the buyer's own words. Never a rhetorical question.
6. **A heading may not contain a claim the section does not substantiate.**

## 5. Technical terms

1. **Use the right word.** Attribution, indexation, retrieval, canonical, structured data and schema are the correct terms on the pages where they belong. **Do not dumb them down.**
2. **Define once, in place, in one sentence, at first use on that page.** Never a glossary page; never a tooltip carrying the meaning the sentence should have carried.
3. **Plain form first where both work.** "Where an enquiry came from (attribution)" rather than "attribution (i.e. where an enquiry came from)".
4. **An acronym earns its place only if the buyer uses it.** SEO does. RFQ does, in the beachhead segment. **CRO, GEO, AEO and LLMO do not** — see `TERMINOLOGY_DICTIONARY.md`.
5. **Never invent a term.** No coined framework, no branded stage name, no proprietary-sounding noun.

## 6. Contractions

**Use them in commercial, problem, editorial and conversion copy** — "we don't", "you're", "it isn't". They are how the register stays modern rather than corporate.

**Avoid them in three places**, where precision reads better than warmth: statements of what is not promised · the attribution-limits section · anything legal, consent-related or contractual.

## 7. Questions

- **Permitted:** real buyer questions as headings, in the buyer's words; a question that genuinely reframes a decision, used once.
- **Prohibited:** rhetorical openers ("Are you struggling to generate leads?") · questions used as transitions ("So what does this mean for you?") · a question whose answer is obviously yes · more than two question headings on any one page outside `/faq`.

## 8. Statistics

1. **Every number carries sample size, date, location and method** — in the sentence, or in the caption, or immediately adjacent. No exceptions, including in marketing copy.
2. **No number as a hook.** A figure opening a page without its context is the exact behaviour that put "300%+ ROI" into AI answers as a fact about the company.
3. **No borrowed vendor figure presented as MappedSkills' finding**, and vendor sources always labelled as vendor-published.
4. **Where a figure does not exist, write UNKNOWN** — not a range, not an estimate, not "up to".
5. **No decimal precision the underlying data does not support**, and **no mean of unstable values**.
6. **No number in a heading.**

## 9. Metaphors

**Sparing, concrete and mechanical.** The ones that earn their place describe a physical system: *the chain*, *the path*, *the seams*, *where it breaks*, *the loss zones*. They are useful because they are literally what is being described.

**Prohibited registers:** war and combat *(dominate, crush, battle, weapons)* · sport *(playbook, game-changer, winning formula)* · flight and space *(rocket, launchpad, skyrocket, take-off)* · magic *(unlock, secret, silver bullet, hack)* · medicine and cure *(prescription, diagnosis is permitted; "cure" is not)* · journey-as-transformation *(the buyer's journey is a documented path, not a personal odyssey)*.

**One metaphor per page at most**, and never in a limits section.

## 10. Humour

**Dry understatement, at most, and rarely.** MappedSkills has one genuinely wry asset — a firm publishing its own measured zero and its own broken form — and understatement is the right register for it.

**Never humorous:** pricing · limits and what is not promised · attribution honesty · research findings and method · anything on `/contact` or `/schedule-call` · anything about a competitor.

**No jokes, no puns, no exclamation marks, no emoji in body copy.**

## 11. First-person language

- **"We" is the company.** Used throughout. It is a small firm and "MappedSkills believes" reads as a press release.
- **"I" only in a named-author byline context** — an author's note in an article or research entry, where the person is identified.
- **Never "MappedSkills" as the subject of a sentence in body copy on the site's own pages** — that is third-person self-reference, and it is corporate. It is correct in schema, in an entity description, and on a directory profile.
- **Never "our team" as a substitute for a name.** Where a person did the work, the person is named.

## 12. Second-person language

- **"You" throughout commercial, problem, pricing and conversion copy.** It is the buyer's page.
- **"A business" or "the reader" in research and method sections**, where the appropriate distance is analytical rather than persuasive.
- **Never "you" in an accusatory shape.** "Your site is losing enquiries" asserts something unverified about the reader; "this is where enquiries are usually lost, and here is how to check yours" does not.
- **Never "you need"** — it presumes a diagnosis that has not been made.

## 13. Mechanics

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Spelling** | British/Indian in prose — optimisation, organisation, analyse, programme, prioritise, behaviour. **American spelling preserved only in URLs, code identifiers and query phrases the page must match** |
| **Numbers** | Words for one to nine in prose; numerals for 10 and above, and for every measurement, run count, date and currency figure |
| **Currency** | ₹ with Indian conventions — lakh, crore — for any owner-confirmed figure. **Never for an invented one** |
| **Dates** | Written out where a claim depends on them ("measured on 31 August 2026"), so a reader can judge freshness |
| **Emphasis** | Bold for genuinely load-bearing statements, used rarely. **No italics for emphasis, no ALL CAPS, no underline** |
| **Lists** | Only where the items are genuinely parallel. **Never a list of three adjectives** |
| **Tables** | Only where the content is genuinely tabular. A table used for layout is worse than a paragraph |
| **Exclamation marks** | **None** |
| **Links** | Anchor text describes the destination, never "click here", and never a promise larger than the page it leads to |

## 14. The four tests before any page is submitted

1. **The swap test.** Replace "MappedSkills" with a competitor's name. If the opening paragraph still works, it fails.
2. **The delete test.** Remove every sentence that could have been written by someone who had not done the work. If the page still says the same thing, it has no information gain.
3. **The "how do you know that?" test.** A prospect asks it of any sentence on the page. Every sentence must have an answer.
4. **The read-aloud test.** Read the first screen aloud. If it sounds like a brochure, a press release or a chatbot, rewrite it.

## 15. Ten sentences that must never appear

Each is a real pattern this business is at risk of, not a generic warning.

1. "We are a results-driven digital marketing agency."
2. "We help businesses unlock their growth potential."
3. "Our data-driven approach delivers measurable ROI."
4. "In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape…"
5. "We don't just do SEO — we do so much more."
6. "Get found. Convert more. Automate growth." *(A superseded candidate line. It must not be reintroduced.)*
7. "Rank #1 in ChatGPT and AI search."
8. "Trusted by leading brands across India."
9. "Book your free strategy session today!" *(Two failures: the entry-offer model is undecided, and the exclamation mark.)*
10. "Are you tired of your website not generating leads?"
