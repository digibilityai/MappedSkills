# Content Brief — `/lead-generation`

**Session:** 09 · **Status:** **PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** · **Class:** LAUNCH — unconditional
**Page type:** Capability (demand source) · **IA reference:** `PAGE_REASON_TO_EXIST_MATRIX.md` §2
**No copy or design is written in this brief.**

---

## 1. Page job
Hold the **live Indian phrasing** for demand-generation intent, and connect it to the approved outcome. This is the closest existing URL to the words the business actually sells on.

## 2. Audience
A business framing its problem as lead volume — usually an owner or a commercial lead who counts enquiries, not sessions.

## 3. Search intent
**T / CI.**

## 4. Primary query family
**`lead generation agency`** — `demand generation agency` collapses into it in Indian autocomplete, so this is the phrasing that carries the intent. `manufacturing lead generation` is adjacent.

## 5. Buyer problem
Not enough enquiries, and the ones arriving are not worth quoting.

## 6. Buyer sophistication
Low to medium on discipline, **high on enquiry quality** — this buyer knows a bad enquiry when they see one and will judge the page on whether it understands that.

## 7. Desired belief change
From *"we need more leads"* to *"we need more of the right enquiries, and a way to tell the difference."*

## 8. Core proposition
Enquiry generation as the outcome the whole system produces — **quality framing, not volume framing.** In the beachhead segment the buyer's own word is RFQ, and enquiry quality is the language that lands.

## 9. Required evidence
The method · the qualification model, in plain terms · the accountability boundary · the own-site diagnostic.

## 10. Proof limitations
**No lead counts, no cost per lead, no conversion rates, no client results.** MappedSkills has **zero historical enquiries** through its own production form, which transmits nothing.

## 11. Questions the page must answer
1. What counts as a qualified enquiry here, and who decides?
2. Where do the enquiries come from?
3. How do I know they are real and traceable?
4. What happens to enquiry quality as volume rises?
5. What do you not promise?

## 12. Objections
| Objection | Honest handling |
|---|---|
| "Lead gen agencies sell rubbish leads" | The qualification definition is published, and quality is reported alongside volume rather than behind it |
| "How many leads will I get?" | **No guaranteed enquiry count.** Ever |
| "Do you buy or scrape lists?" | No. This is demand capture, not outbound list-building |
| "Can you tell me where they came from?" | Where measurement allows. **And where it does not, the page says so** — attribution completeness never determines whether an enquiry counts |

## 13. Differentiation to express
A **published, machine-evaluable definition of a qualified enquiry**, and honest separation of what the website can verify from what only a conversation can. Almost nobody in this category publishes either.

## 19. Schema and content implications
`Service` · `BreadcrumbList`. Currently emits **no structured data at all**. Existing 308 target from `/lead-generation-services`; **the URL does not move.**

## 20. What NOT to claim
- **No guaranteed lead volume, no guaranteed cost per lead, no guaranteed conversion rate.**
- No "leads" framing that implies purchased or scraped lists.
- **No lead-scoring claim** — no scoring model exists, and inventing weights with zero historical enquiries would be fabricated precision.
- **No automation, nurture or lifecycle framing** — automation is a limited downstream expansion capability and gets no page.
- No prohibited figures; no superlatives; no client results.

## 21. Dependencies and owner input
Sequenced after `/services` and `/how-it-works`. The published qualification definition must be settled before this page describes it.

## 22. Visual opportunities
**What separates a qualified enquiry from an unqualified one** — the four states and the disqualifier categories, shown rather than described. This is the page's core differentiator and it is a diagram, not a paragraph.
