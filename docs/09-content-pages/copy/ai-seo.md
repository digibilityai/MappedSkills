## Move 3 — The buyer's problem

### Heading
The situation this is usually bought to fix

### Copy
Someone typed the company name into ChatGPT and found nothing useful. Or asked it to recommend a supplier of what you sell and got a list you were not on. Or a free tool reported that you are absent from AI answers, without saying why or what to do about it.

That is a reasonable thing to be concerned about, and it is worth sizing before spending anything on it. Discovery through AI assistants is real and it is growing. It is also, on everything we have seen, still much smaller than ordinary search.

We will not put a share figure on it. The published numbers come from vendor panels nobody outside those vendors can audit, and a number we cannot stand behind is worse than no number at all. If your enquiries have to improve this quarter, this is not the work that does it.

## Move 4 — What the work involves

### Heading
What the work involves

### Copy
**Check access first, then stop treating it as the answer.** Whether the retrieval crawlers can reach and read your pages. It takes an afternoon, its absence would be fatal, and its presence proves nothing.

**Entity accuracy.** Whether the basic facts about your business — name, what you do, where you are, how to reach you — are stated consistently on your own site, in your structured data, and everywhere else that describes you. Branded answers are assembled from those sources, and inconsistency is what produces two systems describing the same company two different ways.

**Source authority, which is mostly off your site.** The lists, directories, review platforms and third-party pages those answers are actually built from. This is the substantial part of the work and it is not publishing.

**Citable pages on your own site.** Content that answers a question precisely enough to be quoted, with the claims sourced and dated.

**Measurement, per system.** Repeated prompts, recorded per system, with the run counts, the date, the location, the language and whether the session was signed in — because all of those change the answer.

### Supporting Elements
- **Visual explanation useful here.** The **training crawler versus retrieval crawler** mapping as a two-column table with the actual user-agent tokens. The market conflates them constantly, and the table settles it faster than a paragraph.

## Move 5 — The boundary, stated early and at full strength

### Heading
What we will not tell you about AI search

### Copy
There is no AI visibility score here, and there will not be one. We measured the same prompts repeatedly across systems: membership was often stable inside one system, and the order changed in every repeated prompt. A single blended number across systems destroys the only stable signal there is, and it would be precision we did not earn.

There is no "rank in ChatGPT". There is no ranking to be had.

We do not guarantee appearance, mention, citation or recommendation in any system. Nobody can, and the mechanism is largely outside anyone's site.

We have one measurement round, on one day. One measurement is not a trend, so we will not describe an improvement, a decline or a percentage change.

Two of the systems we wanted to test — Claude and Grok — could not be tested at all. We hold no baseline for either, so we make no claim about either.

### Supporting Elements
- **Visual explanation useful here.** The four layers kept separate — retrieval, discovery, recommendation, citation — with MappedSkills' own result on each. It is the clearest available demonstration of why a single score is the wrong instrument.
- **Prohibited on this page, absolutely:** any score, average AI rank or blended figure · any guarantee · any trend · any statement about Claude or Grok beyond "not testable" · GEO, AEO or LLMO as framing · characterising any competitor as having "no AI visibility".

## Move 6 — What gets measured, and what you will see

### Heading
What we measure, and what we report

### Copy
A set of prompts a real buyer would use, run repeatedly, per system, on a stated date, from a stated location, in a stated language, signed out unless there is a reason not to be. We record whether you appeared, in what form, and which sources the answer was built from.

You get frequency across runs, per system, never blended — and the sources, which are usually the actionable part.

What we cannot give you is a position, a score, or a comparison across systems that means anything.

## Move 7 — Evidence, beside the claim it supports

### Heading
We measured ourselves first, and published the result

### Copy
Across 28 unbranded runs on four systems, on one day, from Pune, in English, signed out, we appeared zero times. In 8 branded runs — where the systems were given our name — 7 described us substantially accurately.

Directory and listing sources appeared in 15 of those 28 unbranded runs. In one of them, ChatGPT stated in plain text that it had chosen the brands it named by reading two directories. We are listed on neither.

Our own site is fully readable by the retrieval crawlers, our `robots.txt` blocks none of them, and the result was still zero. Access is necessary and it is not sufficient — including for us, which is the part of this most often sold the other way round.

## Move 8 — Objections, answered directly

### Heading
The four questions we get asked here

### Copy
**"Everyone sells this now — what makes you different?"** In the same 28 unbranded runs, two agencies in this city that sell exactly that did not appear either. That is one signed-out sample on one day and not a verdict on them, but it does suggest that selling the terminology is not the same as having the mechanism. What we offer instead is the diagnosis — absent from the answers, because those answers are built from sources you are not on — and the work that follows from it.

**"Is this even a separate discipline from SEO?"** Google's own published position is that optimising for its generative AI features "is thus still SEO", with no new files, markup or chunking required. We agree with that for Google's surfaces. Where non-Google systems genuinely differ is in which third-party sources they lean on, and that is where the work differs too.

**"Ahrefs and Semrush give me a checker for free."** True, and they are good tools. They report the absence accurately. What they do not do is tell you which sources the answer was built from, or do the off-site work that changes it. That difference is where the work is — and it is worth being clear that the reporting part is available to you for nothing.

**"Can you get us into ChatGPT?"** No, and anyone who says otherwise is selling you a guarantee they cannot honour. What we can do is fix the entity information, work on the sources those answers are assembled from, measure whether anything changes, and tell you honestly when it has not.
