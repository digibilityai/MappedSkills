## Move 4 (compressed) and Move 6 — What the work is

### Heading
What the work involves

### Copy
**Diagnosis first.** What the traffic actually is and what intent it arrived with. Whether the page answers that intent. Whether the enquiry path can physically complete — on a phone, on a slow connection, with a real submission that we confirm arrives. Whether anything is being recorded at all.

**Then the fixes that follow from it**, in the order the evidence supports. Message and offer where the page is not answering the visit. Form and booking friction where the path is losing people. Delivery and notification where enquiries are being sent and not received. Instrumentation where the loss is invisible.

**Built, not just recommended.** The enquiry path includes the form, the booking surface, what happens after submission, and the measurement. A recommendations deck that stops at the design is where most of this work fails.

**Measured against a starting point captured first.** Without a baseline there is no before, and no honest way to describe an after.

### Supporting Elements
- **Visual explanation useful here.** The four loss zones — demand, page, path, measurement — and where each is diagnosed. **Shared visual language with the problem page, at a shallower depth**, so the two read as one system rather than two pitches.

## Move 5 — The boundary, above the midpoint

### Heading
What we can test, and what we cannot

### Copy
Statistically valid A/B testing needs a volume of traffic and conversions most businesses in this market do not have. Below that threshold a test cannot reach significance, and running one anyway produces a decision dressed as evidence.

So there are two different activities and we name them differently. Below the threshold: research, diagnosis, a change, and an observation afterwards — described exactly that way, with no uplift figure attached. Above the threshold: a real test, with the significance reported.

We do not publish conversion benchmarks, industry averages or typical uplifts. None exists in our evidence base and inventing one would be the same failure this page exists to correct.

We also have no before-and-after of our own to show you. We have no analytics history yet, which means we cannot present a pre/post comparison — and saying so is more useful to you than a chart nobody can audit.

### Supporting Elements
- **Prohibited:** "we tested" below the threshold · "uplift" · any percentage · any fabricated before/after · any benchmark.
