# Homepage headline options considered

**Three expressions inside the approved hero territory *"Judged on the enquiries"* (`DEC-013` item 2 — approved as a strategic territory, not as final copy). These are copy alternatives, not new territories.**

### Option A — the accountability standard, with the contrast that gives it meaning

> **H1:** Judge us on the enquiries, not the traffic.
>
> **Support:** There is buyer demand for what you sell, and some of it never reaches you. We work on what brings them in, the path they take to enquire, and the measurement that shows where each one came from — accountable up to the qualified enquiry, and plain about what cannot be attributed.
>
> **[Corrected 2026-09-01, Gate 9A, `DEC-015`.]** The original wording — *"There are buyers for what you sell, and most of them never reach you"* — made an unsupported second-person quantity assertion ("most") about the specific reader's own buyers. Corrected to describe buyer demand in general, with no invented proportion. No new positioning idea introduced; the H1 and hero territory are unchanged.

**What a buyer understands in about five seconds:** *"They are on the hook for the enquiries rather than the traffic chart, and they will show me the evidence."*

**Strengths.** Ordinary words — enquiries, traffic, evidence. The contrast object ("not the traffic") is what makes it mean something rather than sound like a value. It names no technique, so `/seo` and `/ai-seo` can confirm their search intent freely beneath it. It requires no client result: it states a standard the firm accepts, not an outcome it has produced. The supporting line does the acquisition work with Territory 2's concrete opening, as the approved architecture directs.

**Weaknesses, stated honestly.** The imperative can be read as bravado if the page does not immediately deliver something checkable — which makes block 6 load-bearing rather than optional. And "judge us" invites a comparison the firm cannot yet supply with case studies; the page has to answer that in its own words rather than hope nobody asks.

### Option B — the buyer's own arithmetic, mirrored

> **H1:** You count enquiries. So do we.
>
> **Support:** *(as Option A)*

**Strengths.** The shortest and warmest. It uses the buyer's own unit of measurement and needs no marketing vocabulary at all.

**Weaknesses — and they are disqualifying.** It **fails the swap test** in `MESSAGE_QUALITY_GATE.md` §2: almost any agency in this market could put it on a homepage tomorrow and it would read identically. It agrees with the buyer rather than committing to anything, so it carries no boundary and no differentiator — it is a claim of empathy dressed as a position. And "so do we" is a statement about attention, not accountability, which is precisely the distinction Territory 1 exists to make.

### Option C — the outcome plus its checkability

> **H1:** More enquiries — and you can see where each one came from.
>
> **Support:** *(as Option A)*

**Strengths.** It states the commercial outcome and the differentiator in one line, and the second clause is the thing no competitor examined in this programme offers.

**Weaknesses — and they are disqualifying.** "More enquiries" as a bare headline **reads as a promise of a result**, which is the one thing this business will not give (`PROBLEM_AND_OUTCOME_LANGUAGE.md` Tier 4, `CLAIMS_LADDER.md` level F). The permitted form is *"we work to increase the number of qualified enquiries, and we measure them"* — which does not survive compression into a hero. It also puts measurement at the front of the proposition, and Session 04 found measurement honesty is a proof principle rather than a proposition: a business owner looking for enquiries does not have a methodology problem.

### Recommendation

**Option A.**

It is the only one of the three that scores acceptably on every criterion the approved stress test used: buyer comprehension, commercial relevance, differentiation, defensibility with today's proof, bridging acquisition + enquiry path + measurement, jargon avoidance, and supporting the IA without turning MappedSkills into a technique category. B fails differentiation and the swap test. C fails the claims rules.

**Why Option A is not the mechanical reuse of the approved territory phrase.** *"Judged on the enquiries."* is a passive fragment describing the firm to itself. Option A puts the reader in the sentence, makes it an instruction they can act on, and adds the contrast — *not the traffic* — that carries the whole commercial idea. The territory is preserved; the register is buyer-facing, which is what Session 11 was asked to find.

**The body copy below is built on Option A. Selection remains an orchestrator and owner decision.**

Judge us on the enquiries, not the traffic.

# Hero Supporting Copy
There is buyer demand for what you sell, and some of it never reaches you. We work on the whole route to an enquiry — being found when someone searches, paid demand where it earns its place, the page they arrive on, and the measurement that shows where each enquiry came from.

Traffic is where that route starts. The enquiries are the part we answer for.

## Block 2 — Recognition: name the situation without diagnosing the reader

### Heading
What this usually looks like from the inside

### Copy
Buyers who need what you sell are searching, and finding somebody else. Or the visits arrive and the enquiries do not. Or enquiries do arrive, and nobody can say which came from where — so nobody can say what to do more of.

That reads like three problems. It behaves like one. More visits on a path that does not work produce nothing. A path that works, fed the wrong visits, produces nothing worth quoting. And with no measurement in place, all three failures look identical from the outside.

### Supporting Elements
- Symptom list, in the buyer's own words, for self-identification: sessions up and enquiries flat · enquiries stopped after a site change · enquiries only ever from one channel · the enquiries that do arrive are not worth quoting · no idea whether anything arrives at all.
- **No statistic. No "X% of businesses".** No such figure exists.
- Contextual link where the last symptom is named: *"If that is the shape of it, this page is about exactly that"* → `/problems/traffic-but-no-enquiries`.

## Block 3 — Mechanism: how an enquiry actually gets produced

### Heading
How the work produces an enquiry

### Copy
Three connected parts. Reach the buyers already looking — organic search first, and paid where it is the right source for you. Make the route from that first visit to a sent enquiry work. Instrument the whole chain, so an enquiry records where it came from at the moment it is made.

They are different skills, which is why they are described separately. What decides the result is usually the joins between them: between the search work and the page it lands on, between the page and the form, between the form and wherever the enquiry is supposed to arrive, and between all of it and the measurement. Where responsibility is split across suppliers, those joins can end up belonging to nobody. Here they belong to us.

What any engagement covers is decided with you. The measurement is the part that is not optional, because nothing we report about the outcome means anything until it works.

### Supporting Elements
- **Visual explanation useful here.** The enquiry chain as one continuous object — discovery and source → landing → interaction → enquiry → qualified enquiry → opportunity and revenue from the client's own data — with the accountability boundary marked on it. Copy alone makes the reader hold six stages in their head.
- **PROOF CTA:** *Read the method* → `/how-it-works`.
- **Prohibited here:** a named framework · a three-step process graphic that reads as three services · any statement that all three parts are always bought · any speed claim.

## Block 4 — The accountability boundary, high on the page and stated as a position

### Heading
What we are accountable for, and where that stops

### Copy
We are accountable for the qualified enquiry — one that is real, reachable, from a business context, with a stated need. What counts as one is published, and it is the number we report against a starting point we capture before anything changes.

Where you give us your close data, we report through to the deals and the revenue your own system recorded. Those are your numbers, from your system, and we state what we did and did not control alongside them.

Some enquiries arrive with no usable trail at all. Someone types your name into a browser, or calls, or was told about you by a customer. We record those as unattributed rather than assigning them to a channel — and a gap in the measurement never takes an enquiry out of the count.

We do not promise rankings, a position in AI answers, a number of enquiries, revenue, or a return on spend. None of that is ours to control, and a guarantee would be a claim about a business we do not run.

### Supporting Elements
- **PROOF CTA:** *See what can and cannot be attributed* → `/how-it-works` attribution section.
- **Register note for the writer:** no contractions in this block. It is a statement of what is not promised, and precision reads better than warmth here (`VOICE_AND_TONE.md` §6).
- **Prohibited here:** hedging · legalese · an apology for the limit · a disclaimer typeface treatment.

## Block 7 — Fit: let the wrong buyer leave

### Heading
Who this is built for

### Copy
This works for businesses where a customer takes time to decide and one good enquiry is worth real money — where buyers for what you sell already exist, or can reasonably be reached. It works where you can act on the enquiries when they arrive, and where you are willing to let us measure the path properly. That last one is not negotiable: without it, nothing we tell you afterwards can be checked.

It is a poor fit if the demand has to be created from nothing, if what you sell is bought on impulse or on price alone, or if you need somebody to guarantee the outcome. Those are legitimate requirements. They are not what this is.
