## Move 1 — Validate the symptom, without implying a cause

### Heading
What this looks like

### Copy
Sessions are up and enquiries are flat. Or enquiries stopped after a site change and nobody is sure whether the two are connected. Or they only ever come from one channel. Or they arrive and none of them are worth quoting for. Or nobody is certain whether an enquiry has arrived at all.

All of those produce the same feeling, and they do not have the same cause.

---

## Move 2 — Refuse the assumption. This is the page's distinctive move

### Heading
It is one of four things, and they are not interchangeable

### Copy
The usual response to this symptom is to change the website. Sometimes that is correct. Frequently it is the equivalent of repainting a shop whose door is locked.

Traffic that does not become enquiries is caused by one of four things, and each has a different fix:

**Demand** — the traffic is the wrong traffic. It arrives from searches with no commercial intent, from the wrong audience, at the wrong stage, or from the wrong country. No amount of page improvement makes a student enquire.

**Page** — the page does not answer the visit. The message, the offer, the evidence or the obvious objection is missing, so the visitor gets what they asked for and still has no reason to act.

**Path** — the enquiry cannot complete. The form fails on a phone, the booking widget does not render, the submission goes nowhere, or the enquiry arrives in an inbox nobody reads.

**Measurement** — enquiries may be arriving unseen. With no analytics, no source capture and no recorded conversion, an improvement and a disaster look identical.

**Measurement is the one to check first**, not because it is the most likely cause, but because without it you cannot tell which of the other three you have.

### Supporting Elements
- **Visual explanation useful here.** A decision tree across the four zones. This is the page's core value and prose forces the reader to hold four branches in their head.

## Move 5 — The runnable sequence. Every check ends in something observable

### Heading
Five checks you can run today

### Copy

**Check 1 — Is anything being recorded at all?**
Open your analytics. If there is none, stop here: that is your first finding, and it is the most common one. If there is, look for whether an enquiry produces a recorded event, and whether that event carries where the visitor came from.
*Observable outcome:* you can either see enquiries in the data, or you have just established that you cannot.

**Check 2 — Submit a real enquiry and confirm it arrives.**
Fill in your own form, from a phone, on mobile data rather than office wifi. Use a real address you control. Then check the inbox it was supposed to reach. Check the spam folder. Check whether anyone is monitoring that address at all.
*Observable outcome:* the message is in the inbox, or it is not. **A success screen is not proof of delivery.** A form can display "thank you" and transmit nothing — ours did.
Do the same with your booking link: open it in a private window and try to book a slot.

**Check 3 — What is the traffic actually made of?**
In your analytics, look at the top landing pages and, where you have it, the searches that produced them. Ask of the top ten: would a person searching that be able to buy from you?
*Observable outcome:* a rough split between commercial visits and everything else. If most of the volume is informational, career-related or from countries you do not serve, you have a demand problem, and the site is not at fault.

**Check 4 — Does the landing page answer the visit it received?**
Take the page that receives the most traffic. Read the first screen as though you had arrived from the search that sends it. Can you tell within a few seconds that this business does that specific thing, and what to do next?
*Observable outcome:* yes or no, per page. Do it for the top three, not for the homepage — the homepage is rarely where the loss is.

**Check 5 — Are the enquiries you do get the right ones?**
Take the last twenty, if you have twenty. Sort them into: worth quoting · real but not a fit · not a business enquiry at all.
*Observable outcome:* a ratio. If most of them are real but wrong-fit, you have a demand problem wearing a quality costume, and more traffic will make it worse.

## Move 6 — What each finding means, including where our own work is the wrong answer

### Heading
What each finding means, and what to do about it

### Copy

**If nothing is being recorded** — fix that before anything else, and before believing any change is an improvement. Analytics, a recorded conversion at the moment the enquiry is made, and the source captured with it. Until then every decision is a guess, including ours.

**If enquiries are not arriving** — this is the highest-value finding on the page and the fastest to fix. It is also the one that makes the previous months' marketing spend look like a failure when it was not. Fix the delivery, then look at the numbers again before changing anything else.

**If the traffic is the wrong traffic** — conversion work will not help you, and anyone who sells it to you at this point is selling the wrong thing. The work is in what you are being found for, which is an acquisition problem.

**If the page does not answer the visit** — this is the one case where page-level conversion work is the right intervention. Message, offer, evidence and the obvious objections, in that order. Design comes last and matters least.

**If the enquiries are real but wrong-fit** — the problem is upstream of the website. It is usually what you are ranking or bidding for, or how the offer is described, rather than the form.

**If the checks disagree, or there is too little to go on** — that is a legitimate outcome and worth saying plainly. With a handful of visits a month, or a handful of enquiries a year, no honest diagnosis is available yet: the numbers are too small to tell a real pattern from noise, and anyone confidently diagnosing your business from them is guessing. The useful move is to get the measurement working and let a few months accumulate before deciding anything.

**And two of these you should probably just fix yourself.** If check 1 found no analytics, or check 2 found the form is not delivering, those are specific, bounded jobs — whoever built or maintains your site can do them, usually in a day. You do not need an agency for that, and you should not pay one for it.

### Supporting Elements
- **This section is what stops the page becoming a pitch**, and it has to survive editing. Two of the five findings point away from conversion work, one outcome says the reader has too little evidence to diagnose anything yet, and one tells them to fix it themselves without hiring anyone.

## Move 7 — How we investigate, briefly, with the worked example

### Heading
What we found when we ran this on ourselves

### Copy
We ran these checks on our own site before offering them to anyone. The contact form had no submission target: it showed a success message and sent nothing, so every enquiry it ever received was lost while the visitor was told it had worked. The booking widget rendered empty. The thank-you page existed and nothing linked to it. There was no analytics of any kind, which is exactly why none of that had been noticed.

Four of the five checks above failed on our own site. That is not a comfortable thing to publish, and it is the most useful illustration we have: the loss was not in the design, the copy or the traffic. It was in a part nobody was looking at.
