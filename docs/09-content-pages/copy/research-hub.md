## Section 1 — The standard, stated on the index

### Heading
What everything here meets

### Copy
**Sample, date, location and method** on every measurement, in the entry itself rather than in a methodology appendix nobody opens.

**Run counts published**, because a single prompt or a single check is an anecdote.

**Per system, never blended.** Where several systems are measured, they are reported separately. A combined figure across systems would hide the only stable signal there is.

**Frequency, not rank.** We report how often something appeared across repeated runs. We do not report a position, because the order changed in every repeated prompt we measured and a rank would be a reading of noise.

**Adverse findings published.** The first entry here reports that this business appeared zero times in its own measurement. A research programme that only publishes flattering results is marketing with footnotes.

**Versions, not edits.** A re-measurement creates a new dated version and the previous one stays readable.

## Section 2 — What is research here, and what is not

### Heading
What counts as research here

### Copy
Something we measured ourselves, that somebody else could repeat from what we published, on a stated date.

Not: a summary of other people's statistics, an opinion piece, a trend prediction, or an article with a chart in it. Those, where they are worth writing at all, go on the blog and are labelled as what they are.

**One further rule, because it matters for anyone citing this:** an original dataset lives at exactly one address. Where something written elsewhere on this site draws on a measurement, it links here rather than reproducing the data.

## Section 4 — How to cite this

### Heading
Citing this work

### Copy
Each entry carries its own date, version and author, and the canonical address for the measurement is the entry itself. If you are quoting a figure from here, quote its scope with it — the sample, the date and the conditions — because without those the number means something different from what we measured.

Corrections are welcome and are published as corrections rather than as silent edits.
