## 0. How these four differ, and why they are not variations

Each territory is organised around a different **primary object** — the thing the site is fundamentally made of. That is what makes them genuinely distinct rather than four settings of "minimal modern B2B".

| | Territory | Primary object | The site is fundamentally… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** | **The Record** | The document | …a kept record, dated and open to inspection |
| **T2** | **The Run** | The path | …one continuous traced route, seen in segments |
| **T3** | **The Standing** | The status | …a live statement of what is known and how well |
| **T4** | **The Straight Answer** | The sentence | …a competent firm talking plainly, with nothing between |

All four can express the approved strategy. They differ in what they are *good at*, what they *risk*, and what they *cost*. None is a straw man; T4 in particular is the safest, cheapest and most accessible of the four and is scored accordingly in `05_TERRITORY_EVALUATION.md`.

Every territory below obeys, without exception: no invented proof · no fabricated dashboard · no AI cliché · no funnel · no logo wall · no metric counter · every number with its provenance · WCAG 2.2 AA · light-first unless stated.

# T1 — THE RECORD

## A. Name
**The Record.** Internal working name. Not a tagline, not a brand name, and never surfaced to a visitor.

## B. Core idea
The website behaves like the **working record of a firm that measures things** — something that has been kept rather than composed, dated rather than timeless, revised in the open rather than quietly edited, and deliberately left available for inspection. Its lineage is research institutes, investigative newsrooms and scientific record-keeping, not agencies and not software products. Two registers run through everything: an **argument** register that is plain, direct and commercial, carrying what the firm says; and an **apparatus** register that carries when it was measured, on what sample, by what method, with what limits, and at what confidence. The argument column stays short because the apparatus is doing the work that would otherwise become caveating prose. The site's distinctive quality is that its machinery is visible and is designed to the same standard as its statements.

## C. Buyer impression — first five seconds
*"These people write things down and expect to be checked. This is not a brochure."*

Concretely: a founder should register a plain commercial sentence in ordinary words, and — in peripheral vision, before reading it — should register that there is a **date and a source sitting next to it**. That combination is unusual enough in this category to be the differentiator, and quiet enough not to look like a stunt.

## D. Visual grammar
- **Composition.** A measured primary reading column with a persistent **apparatus margin** beside it. Asymmetric by default and left-anchored — the record has a spine. The margin is not a sidebar of related links; it is where provenance lives.
- **Hierarchy.** Established by type size, weight and register, and by rules. Not by boxes, tints or elevation.
- **Density.** Deliberately variable and page-type-dependent (`07_VISUAL_GRAMMAR.md` §7). The reading column is never dense; the apparatus may be.
- **Whitespace.** Generous and *structural* — the margin and the rhythm of rules create it. It is never the empty half-viewport of a minimal hero.
- **Geometry.** Rectilinear and orthogonal. Right angles, aligned baselines, a visible sense of ruling. No rounded-card language, no organic shapes, no blobs.
- **Line.** The primary structural device. Hairline rules divide, group and annotate. A **broken or dashed rule always means something specific** — an unmeasured stretch, a boundary, a gap in the record — and is never decorative.
- **Borders and containers.** Near-absent. Grouping is done by rule and by space. **A card is used only where the content is genuinely a discrete, comparable item in a set** — a research entry, an article — and even then it is rule-bounded rather than filled and shadowed.
- **Layering.** Flat. Depth exists in exactly one place: **evidence sits one layer beneath its claim** and can be brought forward. Nothing else overlaps, floats or casts a shadow.
- **Rhythm.** Statement → apparatus → statement. A steady, slightly severe cadence that a reader learns within one screen and can then skim reliably.
- **Contrast.** High in type, very low in surface. Almost all the contrast budget is spent on text and on marks; almost none on background differentiation.

## E. Typography character
- **Headline behaviour.** Plain, declarative, sentence case, set at a size that carries authority without shouting. Headings describe their section (`VOICE_AND_TONE.md` §4). No condensed display faces, no all-caps headlines, no tracking effects.
- **Body behaviour.** Long-form-grade. Optimised for sustained reading of an argument, at a comfortable measure. This site will carry `/how-it-works`, `/research` and the problem page's diagnostic, all of which are genuinely read rather than scanned.
- **Two registers, and they are the mechanism** — argument and apparatus, visually distinct at a glance, applied consistently sitewide. Whether that distinction is serif-versus-sans, sans-versus-mono, or one family used across two very different optical sizes is a design-phase decision. **What is decided here is that the distinction exists and is semantic, never decorative.**
- **Scale.** A wide range with few steps. Big jumps between levels, so hierarchy survives without weight tricks or colour.
- **Character.** Editorial in the argument register; technical in the apparatus register. Not academic, and not developer-console.
- **Weight and contrast.** Restrained. Two or three weights total. Emphasis by weight only, used rarely (`VOICE_AND_TONE.md` §13 already prohibits italic emphasis, all-caps and underline).
- **Numerals and data — a hard requirement.** **Tabular, lining figures everywhere.** On this site a number almost always appears beside its sample, its date and often a second number for comparison; proportional figures would misalign every one of those. Numerals should read as *recorded values*, not as display elements.

## F. Colour behaviour
- **Orientation:** **light-first.** A warm off-white or very pale neutral ground, not pure white — pure white plus black is the `Conversion` minimalism trap, and a warm ground reads as paper rather than as a blank canvas. Dark mode may exist as a user preference; it is never the brand.
- **Neutrals:** the whole structural system. Ground, ink, rules and apparatus text are all neutrals, with a slight warmth so the palette is a decision rather than the current `chroma 0` default.
- **Accent:** **one**, and it has one job — **marking**. Annotation ties, current state, the active node in a figure, the focus ring, the link. It is an ink colour, not a brand wash.
- **Restraint:** high. A typical screen shows ground, ink, rule-grey, apparatus-grey and a small quantity of accent. Nothing else.
- **Where colour earns its place:** distinguishing **known from unknown**, marking a boundary, and indicating focus or state. Nowhere else.
- **What colour must never become:** a gradient · a wash behind a hero · a per-service identity · a decorative section tint · a mood · and above all **a judgement of performance.** No red-amber-green. A measured decline is not "red"; it is a measurement (see `08_EVIDENCE_DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md` §6).

## G. Image / graphic language
Photography is **not** the primary material and is not required for the territory to work.

What replaces generic agency imagery, in priority order:

1. **Annotated real artefacts.** A genuine AI answer excerpt with its retrieval date; a real query string; a real analytics view; the firm's own broken form. Annotated with the apparatus register, dated, and labelled as an excerpt. **Real, or absent — never a mock-up.**
2. **Explanatory figures.** A small number of high-quality diagrams doing genuine work: the enquiry run, the four-cause decision tree, the four AI layers, the attribution gap. These are the site's principal visual assets and carry most of its visual identity.
3. **Typographic graphics.** A finding set large as a piece of typography, with its apparatus attached. This is how a "big number" is done here without becoming a counter — the number never appears alone.
4. **Data.** Small, honest, per-system, correctly labelled. Never decorative charts.
5. **Documentary portraits of named people**, used sparingly and only where a real person is identified (`10_IMAGERY_PHOTOGRAPHY_ILLUSTRATION.md` §2).

**Explicitly not used:** stock photography of any kind · illustrated characters · abstract 3D · gradient meshes · icon sets standing in for capabilities · AI-generated collage · browser mockups · invented dashboards.

## H. Data / evidence language
This is the territory's strongest suit and the reason it is recommended.

- **Research findings** are typeset as findings, with the apparatus adjacent: what was asked, of which systems, how many runs, on what date, from where, in what language, at what access tier.
- **Measurements** carry their scope in the apparatus rather than in the sentence.
- **Qualification logic** — what counts as a qualified enquiry — is rendered as a set of stated conditions, visible together, not as a paragraph.
- **Attribution limitations** are given **the same type size and the same visual weight as claims**. This is the single most important rule in the territory. Homepage Block 4 already prohibits "a disclaimer typeface treatment"; The Record makes that prohibition structural rather than a matter of restraint.
- **Methodology** has a named, permanent, recognisable home and a consistent label, so it accumulates as a body of work.
- **Unknowns** are **drawn**. A measured zero, an untested system and an unattributed enquiry are three visually distinct states, and none of them is a blank. See `08_EVIDENCE_DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md` §4.

## I. Motion principle
> **Motion resolves an uncertainty, or it does not ship.**

Permissible meanings: an unknown resolving into a measured value with its date attaching · an annotation tying a claim to its source · a figure revealing its stages in sequence when the reader asks · an evidence layer coming forward from beneath a claim · a version being replaced by a later version while the earlier remains reachable.

Everything else is decoration. Nothing on this site animates to feel modern, nothing enters on load, and no number ever counts up.

## J. Interaction character
**Precise, quiet, immediate.** Interactions feel like consulting a document rather than operating an application.

- **Hover** reveals *supplementary* apparatus only — never anything load-bearing, because touch and keyboard users must get everything.
- **Scroll** is normal scroll. No scroll-jacking, no pinning that traps the reader, no scroll-driven narrative that cannot be skipped.
- **Transitions** are short and few. Page transitions are near-instant.
- **The characteristic interaction is disclosure**: a claim's evidence expands in place, without navigation, without a modal, and without losing the reader's position. Nothing important is ever behind a modal — `PAGE_TYPE_CRO_RULES.md` §11.4 already bans modals and interstitials outright.

## K. Homepage hero expression
**The approved H1 is not rewritten, restyled into a graphic, or broken across lines for effect.**

> Judge us on the enquiries, not the traffic.

In The Record, the hero is **a statement with something checkable placed immediately beside it.** The H1 is set plainly and large in the argument register, left-anchored, with the approved supporting copy beneath. Adjacent — in the apparatus margin, not below the fold — sits one genuine, dated, checkable item: the firm's own measurement, its date, and its result. Not as a badge, not as a metric tile, and not as a claim about capability: as **a record entry with its provenance attached.**

That placement is what makes the imperative "judge us" survive. The Session 11 copy names the risk exactly — the H1 "can be read as bravado if the page does not immediately deliver something checkable — which makes block 6 load-bearing rather than optional." **The Record's answer is to move a fragment of block 6's checkability into the first screen as apparatus, without moving block 6's copy or changing the approved block order.** The hero is a statement standing next to its evidence. No image, no gradient, no illustration, no product panel, no counter.

## L. Commercial page expression
The territory scales because pages differ by **what occupies the apparatus margin and what the single figure on the page is**, not by decoration.

| Page | The page's one figure | What the apparatus carries | How it stays distinct |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/seo` | The demand-to-enquiry run, with the organic segment emphasised and the rest present but recessive | Horizon language, the compounding-and-slow position, scope limits | Emphasis on one segment of a shared object — the visitor sees where SEO sits, which is the bridge move `MESSAGING_ARCHITECTURE.md` §2 requires |
| `/ai-seo` | The four layers — retrieval, discovery, recommendation, citation — separated, with the firm's own results and **two systems marked untestable** | Date, systems, run counts, access tier, location, language | The only page whose figure is per-system. **Its visual signature is separation**, which is the strategy |
| `/google-ads` | The same run, paid segment emphasised, on the same measurement layer | Merit-based selection, what is not promised | Identical structure to `/seo` with a different segment lit — which visually states that paid is one source inside one system |
| `/social-media-ads` | The same run, with the segment shown as **conditional** | When it fits and when it does not | The only page whose figure shows a segment that may correctly be absent |
| `/lead-generation` | The qualification conditions, shown together as a set | The published definition; the hard disqualifiers | The only page whose figure is a *definition* rather than a path |
| `/conversion-optimization` | The path with its loss points — where completion fails | Diagnosis-before-optimisation; the significance threshold | The only page whose figure is about **interruption** |
| Problem pages | The four-cause decision tree, then the runnable checks | Observable outcome per check | The only page whose figure the reader **operates** rather than reads |
| `/pricing` | Sparse. What moves the number, shown as factors | Who this is not for | Deliberately the least figured page — price pages that decorate look defensive |

Sameness is prevented because each page's figure answers a genuinely different question. Sameness is *desirable* between `/seo` and `/google-ads`, because the approved messaging requires them to read as parts of one system.

## M. Long-form expression
The Record is the only one of the four territories designed for long-form from the start.

- A measured reading column, generous leading, and a strong heading hierarchy make `/how-it-works` and the research entries readable at length.
- The apparatus margin **removes** provenance from the prose, so long-form gets shorter rather than longer.
- Rules and figures break the run of text at genuine argument boundaries, so no section runs past four paragraphs unbroken (`VOICE_AND_TONE.md` §3).
- A document's structure — a contents rail on long pages — is part of the record grammar rather than an add-on.
- It does not become exhausting because the density is **in the apparatus, not the prose**, and the reader chooses when to look at it.

## N. Principal risk
**It becomes an academic paper, and the founder buyer bounces.**

The primary buyer is a founder or owner with, per `ICP.md` §5, "low tolerance for jargon" who wants "plain arithmetic". If the apparatus dominates, if the type is too literary, if the ground is too austere, or if the commercial sentence is buried beneath machinery, the site will read as a research organisation that does not want customers — and MappedSkills is a firm that needs enquiries.

**The mitigation is structural, not stylistic:** the argument register always leads, always states the commercial thing first, and always in ordinary words; the apparatus is always adjacent and never in front. Every page's first screen must pass the founder test before its apparatus is designed.

Secondary risk: **the apparatus margin does not survive narrow screens** if it is designed as a desktop-first layout feature rather than as a content relationship. See `13_RESPONSIVE_ACCESSIBILITY_PRINCIPLES.md` §3.

## O. Anti-example
A cream-coloured page in a literary serif, three columns of small grey footnotes, section numbering in Roman numerals, an abstract of the page at the top, a pull-quote in italics, and a "1. Introduction" heading. Somewhere near the bottom, in the smallest type on the page, the price. It looks scholarly, it is unreadable on a phone, it takes four screens to say what the firm does, and no owner of a manufacturing business ever enquires.

That is The Record executed as *costume*. The territory is about a record being **kept and shown**, not about looking like scholarship.

### T1 — REFINEMENT RECORD (2026-09-01, orchestrator correction pass)

**The specification above is preserved unchanged as the original record.** A stress test of T1's weakest score found **one genuine contradiction** in it, and the correction is recorded here rather than by rewriting the section.

**The contradiction:** the territory as specified above has a complete visual vocabulary for **measurement** and **none for action**. A record of a firm that measures things is a record of an observer — and MappedSkills intervenes. Every mechanism by which T1 read as academic, institutional, NGO-like, passive or cold traces to that gap.

**The correction, in one word:** the firm **finds what is broken, *fixes it*, and records what changed.**

**What changes in this territory as a result:**
- **D. Visual grammar** gains an **intervention mark** on the run — work done here, with a date and an owner — distinct from a measurement point.
- **H. Data / evidence language** gains a **`changed`** state: the **state pair** (what was broken → what was done → what changed, dated). A state pair, never a metric pair.
- **A new emphasis rule** governs everything: **emphasis is commercial, presence is evidential.** The apparatus is everywhere and never the loudest thing.
- **A new pace rule:** every page opens fast; no page opens slow.
- **The name gains a binding qualifier:** *The Record is a working record, not an archive.*

**Revised scores:** India-first appropriateness 3 → **4**; buyer comprehension 4 → **5**; total 90 → **92**. Conditional on the refinements being binding. **No other territory was rescored and the competition was not rerun.**

**Full detail:** `16_COMMERCIAL_ENERGY_SYSTEM.md` · `17_ARGUMENT_APPARATUS_HIERARCHY.md`.

# T2 — THE RUN

## A. Name
**The Run.** Internal. From the approved language of "the chain", "the path", "the seams" and "the loss zones" (`VOICE_AND_TONE.md` §9 names these as the metaphors that earn their place).

## B. Core idea
The entire site is built on **one continuous drawn route** — from buyer demand, through discovery, landing, interaction, enquiry and qualified enquiry, to the outcome the client's own system recorded — and every page is a **segment of that one route**, seen closer. The route is not an illustration placed on a page; it is the site's structure. Sections attach to it as nodes. Where measurement stops, the route becomes dashed. Where an enquiry arrives with no usable trail, it arrives without an upstream segment. Where the firm's accountability ends, a boundary is drawn across it. The visitor's experience of the site is literally the experience of following demand to an enquiry.

## C. Buyer impression — first five seconds
*"They are showing me the whole route, including the parts that are broken."*

The intended recognition is spatial before it is verbal: the buyer sees that there is a **thing with a beginning and an end**, that they are somewhere on it, and that parts of it are drawn differently from others.

## D. Visual grammar
- **Composition.** Directional and continuous. There is always an axis, and content is positioned *along* it. Pages begin where the previous context ended.
- **Hierarchy.** Position on the route is a primary hierarchy alongside type. What comes first is upstream.
- **Density.** Low at the route level, higher at node level. The reader zooms in by descending.
- **Whitespace.** Large, and directional — space is what makes a route legible as a route.
- **Geometry.** Line-based. Strokes, nodes, junctions, terminals, boundaries. Very few filled shapes.
- **Line.** The entire identity. Stroke weight, continuity, dash pattern and junction form carry meaning: continuous = measured · dashed = unmeasured · terminated = boundary · unattached = unattributed.
- **Borders and containers.** Almost none. Containment contradicts continuity.
- **Layering.** Two planes only: the route, and content attached to it.
- **Rhythm.** Node → segment → node. Regular and navigational.
- **Contrast.** Concentrated on the route and its states; low elsewhere.

## E. Typography character
- **Headline behaviour.** Positional and labelling — headings often function as node names as well as statements. Restrained in size, because the route must not be shouted over.
- **Body behaviour.** Short-form by construction. Long argument is hard to attach to a line.
- **Scale.** Compressed relative to T1.
- **Character.** Technical and cartographic. Labels, not literature.
- **Weight and contrast.** Light-to-regular for labels; the visual weight lives in the strokes.
- **Numerals.** Tabular, and frequently attached to a point on the route rather than sitting in prose.

## F. Colour behaviour
- **Orientation:** light-first. A route is more legible on a light ground, and dark would push it toward the "glowing network diagram" cliché immediately.
- **Neutrals:** ground and text. The route in the strongest ink available.
- **Accent:** one, meaning **measured / known / active**. Its absence — a desaturated or hairline state — means unmeasured.
- **Restraint:** absolute. This territory fails instantly if segments get individual colours, because a multicoloured route becomes a subway map, and a subway map of a marketing process is a funnel with better graphics.
- **Where colour earns its place:** the known/unknown distinction on the route, and nothing else.
- **What colour must never become:** per-stage identity colours · a gradient along the route · glow · red-amber-green health states.

## G. Image / graphic language
Almost entirely **the route itself, plus annotation**. Real artefacts attach to nodes as evidence (a real query at the discovery node; a real form failure at the path node). Photography is essentially absent, except for named-people portraits. No illustration.

## H. Data / evidence language
Measurements attach to points on the route, which is genuinely powerful: *this* is where the number was taken, and *this* stretch has no number at all. Uncertainty is expressed as route state rather than as a figure — which is honest and legible.

Its weakness is real: a research entry, a methodology page and a long editorial argument **do not attach to a route**. The territory has no native grammar for prose-heavy evidence, and would have to borrow one.

## I. Motion principle
> **Motion traces causality.**

The strongest available motion of any territory: the route running from source to enquiry · a break being exposed · a source connecting to an arriving enquiry · a dashed stretch resolving into a measured one when a measurement is added.

And the largest risk of any territory: this is one design decision away from scroll-jacked path animation, which is expensive, fragile, hostile to reduced motion, and unusable on narrow screens.

## J. Interaction character
Navigational and spatial — the reader moves *along* rather than *down*. Hover surfaces node detail. The characteristic interaction is following a segment to its next node.

**INFERENCE, and it is a serious one:** spatial navigation is the interaction pattern most likely to break for keyboard and screen-reader users, and the one most likely to collapse on mobile.

## K. Homepage hero expression
The approved H1 sits at the **enquiry end** of the route, with "the traffic" visible upstream as the earlier, lighter, larger-volume part of the same drawn object. The contrast in the line — *"not the traffic"* — is rendered as an actual spatial relationship: the traffic is where the route starts; the enquiry is where the firm's name is attached. The accountability boundary is drawn at the qualified-enquiry node in the first screen.

It is the most literal and most immediately comprehensible hero of the four. It is also the one most at risk of reading as a funnel.

## L. Commercial page expression
Excellent, and the territory's second real strength: each commercial page is a **magnified segment** of the same route, which structurally enforces the approved message architecture — a capability is visibly a part rather than the whole. `/seo` is one stretch; `/conversion-optimization` is a different stretch of the same object; `/lead-generation` is the terminal node.

The failure mode is equally structural: **problem pages, research, `/about`, `/pricing` and editorial are not segments of a route**, and forcing them onto one is exactly how a good device becomes a gimmick.

## M. Long-form expression
**The territory's decisive weakness.** A 3,000-word methodology page or a research entry with per-system results has no natural relationship to a drawn route. Either the route becomes vestigial decoration on those pages — which undermines it everywhere else — or long-form is bent onto a spine that does not fit it.

Given that `/how-it-works` is described in the approved architecture as "the highest-value proof asset MappedSkills can publish and the root dependency of the entire content programme", a territory that cannot carry it has a structural problem.

## N. Principal risk
**It becomes a funnel.** The session brief prohibits a generic marketing funnel "unless there is an exceptional reason", and a stylised left-to-right path from demand to enquiry with stages marked on it is a funnel drawn horizontally. The distance between "the enquiry run" and "the conversion funnel" is one designer's decision about tapering.

Secondary: implementation cost, motion fragility, mobile collapse, and accessibility of a spatial navigation model.

## O. Anti-example
A dark hero with a glowing gradient line snaking left to right, five pulsing dots labelled Awareness → Interest → Consideration → Intent → Conversion, particles travelling along the line, a scroll-jacked reveal that fights the trackpad, and on mobile a horizontally-scrolling strip nobody scrolls. It is a funnel with lighting, and it says nothing true about the business.

# T3 — THE STANDING

## A. Name
**The Standing.** As in *where a thing currently stands* — and deliberately not "the score".

## B. Core idea
The site is organised around the **epistemic status of every statement it makes.** Each claim, finding, capability and limitation carries a visible standing: measured · observed · inferred · owner-supplied · unmeasured · not testable · unattributed. The design system *is* that status vocabulary, applied with total consistency from the homepage to a footnote. The visual world is systematic, quiet and instrument-like — but the instrument reads **confidence**, not performance. Its argument to the buyer is: everything on this site is labelled with how well we know it, and you can therefore trust the labels rather than trusting us.

## C. Buyer impression — first five seconds
*"Everything here is labelled with how sure they are. That is unusual."*

**Risk in the same five seconds:** *"…is this a dashboard? Am I being sold a score?"* — which is the exact product `POSITIONING.md` §9 and `AI_SEARCH_LANGUAGE.md` §4.3 prohibit.

## D. Visual grammar
- **Composition.** Systematic and modular. Statements are rows in a consistent structure; the structure is the design.
- **Hierarchy.** Importance and certainty are two separate axes, both visible. This is genuinely novel and genuinely hard.
- **Density.** Medium-to-high everywhere by nature, because every statement carries a marker.
- **Whitespace.** Tight and regular — the rhythm of a well-set table rather than of an editorial page.
- **Geometry.** Strictly gridded. Aligned marks, consistent gutters.
- **Line.** Rules as row separators and as alignment.
- **Borders and containers.** More present than in T1 or T2, because a status attaches to a bounded statement. The risk of a card grid is correspondingly higher.
- **Layering.** Assertion in front, evidence one layer beneath, revealed on demand.
- **Rhythm.** Regular and almost tabular. Highly skimmable; also potentially monotonous.
- **Contrast.** Low in surface; the marks carry a disciplined amount.

## E. Typography character
- **Headline behaviour.** Compact and functional. This territory is not headline-led.
- **Body behaviour.** Short, structured, list- and table-friendly.
- **Scale.** Narrow range — the design does not depend on big type.
- **Character.** Technical throughout, with only mild editorial warmth. Nearer an instrument than a document.
- **Weight and contrast.** Weight is a primary carrier of hierarchy, since size range is narrow.
- **Numerals.** Monospaced and tabular, and unusually prominent — figures appear constantly beside their status.

## F. Colour behaviour
- **Orientation:** light-first, and this matters more here than anywhere: a dark instrument panel with coloured status marks **is** a dashboard, and the strategy forbids one.
- **Neutrals:** dominant to the point of austerity.
- **Accent:** the status vocabulary is the only chromatic system on the site, and it must be small — three or four distinguishable states at most, each also carrying a shape and a label so that colour is never the sole channel.
- **Restraint:** the highest of the four. Any decorative colour anywhere destroys the semantics.
- **Where colour earns its place:** status, and only status.
- **What colour must never become:** a quality judgement. Not green-good, not red-bad. "Unmeasured" must not look like a warning, and "measured" must not look like a pass — otherwise the site is grading itself, which is the score it rejects.

## G. Image / graphic language
Nearly imageless. The status system, dense type and small honest data displays constitute the visual identity. Photography only for named people. No illustration at all.

**INFERENCE:** the absence of imagery is a real commercial risk with a founder audience, and this territory has the fewest tools available to soften it.

## H. Data / evidence language
The strongest of the four **at the level of a single statement** — no other territory labels every assertion. Uncertainty is native. Attribution limits are first-class. The four AI layers with two untestable systems render more naturally here than anywhere else.

But it is the weakest at **argument**. A status system labels conclusions; it does not carry reasoning. `/how-it-works` needs to explain, and this territory's grammar wants to tabulate.

## I. Motion principle
> **Motion is state change.**

An unknown resolving into a measured value with its date attaching · a status changing when new evidence arrives · evidence coming forward from beneath a claim. Precise and small. No transport, no travel, no reveal-on-scroll narrative.

## J. Interaction character
**Systematic and inspectable.** Filter, sort, expand, compare. Hover reveals the status legend; expansion reveals the evidence. It feels like using a well-made reference tool.

Risk: the site starts to feel like software the visitor has to operate, on a page whose job is to make one commercial proposition legible in seconds.

## K. Homepage hero expression
The H1 stands as the site's own **top-level claim, carrying its own standing mark** — an accountability standard the firm accepts, with its status shown as exactly what it is: a stated standard, not a measured result. Immediately beneath, the firm's own current standing on its own measurements, including the zero, shown in the same vocabulary used everywhere else.

It is intellectually the most honest hero of the four and, judged as a first commercial impression, probably the coldest. A founder arriving from a referral meets a system before they meet a sentence.

## L. Commercial page expression
Scales evenly and safely — every page inherits the same status vocabulary. **Too evenly.** The risk is that every page looks identical because the system is stronger than the content, producing the "every section uses the same layout" anti-pattern by design rather than by accident.

## M. Long-form expression
Poor-to-adequate. Status marks in continuous prose become clutter; a reader working through a 3,000-word argument does not want a marker on every second sentence. It would need to switch into a different, quieter mode for long-form — which weakens the claim that the system is universal.

## N. Principal risk
**It becomes the score it rejects.** Any consistent visual system for rating certainty is one small step from a dashboard that rates performance — and `AI_SEARCH_LANGUAGE.md` §4.3 prohibits an AI Visibility Score, `POSITIONING.md` §8 explicitly lists a score as *not* a moat, and the session brief §16 prohibits designing the brand around a dashboard that does not exist. A buyer who reads the status marks as a scorecard has been given exactly the wrong impression by exactly the system built to prevent it.

Secondary: coldness, and a compliance-tool register that suppresses enquiries.

## O. Anti-example
A dark page of dense rows, each with a coloured pill — green "VERIFIED", amber "PARTIAL", red "UNVERIFIED" — a filter bar across the top, a legend, and a summary panel showing "Evidence coverage: 62%". It is a compliance dashboard. It has invented a score, judged itself with a traffic light, and turned a marketing site into an audit report.

# T4 — THE STRAIGHT ANSWER

## A. Name
**The Straight Answer.**

## B. Core idea
Radical legibility. The site looks like **a competent firm talking plainly to a business owner**, with nothing between the two. Very large clear type, one idea per screen, generous space, prices and limits stated early and without defensiveness, no decoration, no apparatus, no diagrams unless one is genuinely unavoidable. Its argument is behavioural: a firm that answers the question first, in ordinary words, and states what it will not do, is a firm that is not managing you. It draws on plainspoken commercial writing and on the directness Indian owner-buyers reward — a real phone number, a real price, a real answer.

## C. Buyer impression — first five seconds
*"They answered the question. No performance."*

## D. Visual grammar
- **Composition.** Single column, left-aligned, one idea per screen.
- **Hierarchy.** Almost entirely type size and space.
- **Density.** Low everywhere. Sparse by conviction.
- **Whitespace.** The dominant material.
- **Geometry.** Barely present. Very few drawn elements of any kind.
- **Line.** Occasional dividing rule; no structural line system.
- **Borders and containers.** None. No cards anywhere.
- **Layering.** Entirely flat.
- **Rhythm.** Statement, space, statement. Slow and even.
- **Contrast.** High in type; nothing else competes.

## E. Typography character
- **Headline behaviour.** Very large, plain, sentence case. The headline is essentially the whole design.
- **Body behaviour.** Large, comfortable, short paragraphs.
- **Scale.** The widest range of the four — the design's only real instrument.
- **Character.** Neither editorial nor technical: **conversational-formal**. The register of a well-written letter.
- **Weight and contrast.** One or two weights. Nothing else.
- **Numerals.** Tabular, but rarer — this territory reaches for numbers least often, which is both a strength and, given `VOICE_AND_TONE.md` §8's provenance requirements, an evasion risk.

## F. Colour behaviour
- **Orientation:** light-first, warm.
- **Neutrals:** ground and ink, with a warmth that keeps it from becoming the `Conversion` monochrome trap.
- **Accent:** one, confident, used for links and the primary action.
- **Restraint:** high, but by *absence* rather than by system — which means it carries no meaning.
- **Where colour earns its place:** action and link only.
- **What colour must never become:** anything decorative; anything atmospheric.

## G. Image / graphic language
Minimal to none. Occasional real documentary photography of actual people or actual work. No illustration, no diagrams by preference, no icons.

**The problem, stated plainly:** the approved copy repeatedly asks for visual explanation — homepage Block 3, the problem page's Move 2 and Move 5, `/how-it-works` §2 — and this territory has no grammar for it. It would either ignore those instructions or import a foreign one.

## H. Data / evidence language
**The weakest of the four.** With no apparatus, provenance has nowhere to go except back into the prose — which is precisely the condition the direction is trying to escape, and which reproduces the text-heaviness the owner rejects. Limits get stated as sentences, honestly, and the site gets longer with every honest sentence added.

## I. Motion principle
> **Almost none.**

Focus and state feedback only. Nothing reveals, nothing traces, nothing resolves. The most performant, most accessible and most durable motion position of the four — and it gives up every explanatory opportunity.

## J. Interaction character
**Minimal and conventional.** Links, buttons, a form. Nothing to learn. The most predictable and least risky interaction model available.

## K. Homepage hero expression
The H1 alone, very large, on a warm ground, with the approved supporting copy beneath it and the two primary actions. Nothing else on the first screen.

It is the most confident-looking hero of the four for about three seconds — and then the Session 11 risk arrives with nothing to meet it. "Judge us" invites a check; this hero offers nothing to check. **The copy itself identifies this as the failure mode**, and this territory has the fewest tools to answer it.

## L. Commercial page expression
Scales trivially — every page is the same structure with different words. That is its strength and its problem: **pages become distinguishable only by their copy**, which means the site is carried entirely by the writing and provides no visual differentiation from any other well-typeset firm.

## M. Long-form expression
Genuinely good for reading — large type, single column, comfortable measure. But with no apparatus and no figures, long-form becomes **longer**, and the reader must hold every structure (six stages, four causes, five checks, four AI layers) in their head, which the approved copy explicitly flags as the problem to solve.

## N. Principal risk
**It is the "premium equals minimal typography" trap the session brief names in §38, and it fails the swap test.**

`Conversion` (observed 2026-09-01) is the live demonstration: monochrome, immaculate, and interchangeable with a hundred other firms. `MESSAGE_QUALITY_GATE.md` §2 applies the swap test to copy; the same test applied to this territory's visual design fails — replace the words with a competitor's and nothing else needs to change.

And the deeper problem: **minimalism reads as confidence only when the reader assumes there is substance behind it.** A firm with named clients can be minimal. A firm with zero case studies, zero reviews and a published measured zero, presented minimally, reads as *empty* rather than as *restrained* — the visual language provides nothing to fill the space that proof would normally occupy.

## O. Anti-example
A white page. An enormous headline. Three lines of text. A black button. Four screens of near-nothing. A footer. Indistinguishable from a design studio, a law firm, a fund and an agency, and entirely dependent on the reader already caring.
