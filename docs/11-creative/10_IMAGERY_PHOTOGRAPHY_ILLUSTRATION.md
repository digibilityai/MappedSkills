## 1. The image hierarchy — what the site is made of

Stated first, because it determines everything below. In priority order:

| Rank | Material | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Explanatory figures** | The primary visual asset class. Diagrams that do genuine work: the run, the four-cause tree, the four AI layers, the attribution gap |
| **2** | **Annotated real artefacts** | A real AI answer excerpt, a real query string, a real analytics view, the firm's own broken form — dated, annotated, labelled |
| **3** | **Typographic graphics** | A finding set large, with its apparatus attached. How a "big number" happens here without becoming a counter |
| **4** | **Documentary portraits of named people** | Highly selective, and only where a real person is identified |
| **5** | **Everything else** | Absent |

**The site's visual identity is carried by figures and typography, not by pictures.** That is a decision, and it is the right one for a firm whose only genuine assets are method, measurement, named people and honesty.

## 2. Photography — DECISION

> ### **RECOMMENDATION: HIGHLY SELECTIVE.**
> **Not a major brand element. Not a supporting element used for atmosphere. Not absent. Used only where a real, identified person or a real, specific place is the subject — and nowhere else.**

### 2.1 Why not a major element
- MappedSkills has **no clients it may show, no offices it needs to display, no product to photograph and no team photograph that has been supplied.** Photography as a brand element would therefore have to be stock or staged, and both are prohibited.
- Stock office-team photography is the single most category-generic device in this market. **Observed on DigiMark, 2026-09-01:** a desaturated photograph of a smiling team around a laptop, darkened behind the hero.
- The session brief §38 is explicit: **trust is not corporate photography.**

### 2.2 Why not absent
`POSITIONING.md` §5 item 7 — **"Name people. Founder expertise and named authorship are currently among the few available trust signals."** Naming a person and then not showing them is a smaller gesture than naming and showing. `PAGE_TYPE_CRO_RULES.md` §8 requires "named people with real credentials" on `/about`, and homepage Block 6 lists "the people doing the work" as one of only four genuine reasons to believe.

An identified person is one of the few things on this website that is unambiguously real. Photography earns its place there and only there.

### 2.3 Where photography is permitted

| Permitted | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Named-person portraits** on `/about` | The primary and possibly only use at launch |
| **Author portraits** on articles and research entries | Small, alongside the byline |
| **A real, specific place**, where locality is genuinely being established | Only if genuine. Local entity signals matter (`POSITIONING.md` §11) — but a real address is established by NAP, a claimed Google Business Profile and consistent listings, **not** by a photograph of a city skyline |
| **Documentary photography of real client work** | **Not available at launch.** Permitted later only with written permission (`POSITIONING.md` §9) |

### 2.4 Treatment, where photography is used
- **Subject matter:** the actual person, in an actual working environment.
- **Framing:** environmental rather than tightly cropped headshot. A person at a desk, in a room that exists.
- **Realism:** unposed, natural light, imperfect. It should look photographed rather than produced.
- **Human presence:** one person, identified by name. Never anonymous crowds, never "a team".
- **Environment:** the real one. Not a rented studio, not a co-working stock set.
- **Treatment:** minimal. Colour, no heavy grade, no duotone, no brand-colour overlay, no cut-out on a coloured field. A photograph on this site should look like a record, consistent with everything else.

### 2.5 Prohibited stock-photo behaviours — binding
Smiling office teams around a laptop · handshakes · people pointing at charts, whiteboards or screens · headsets · diverse-team-in-glass-meeting-room · aspirational lifestyle · rooftop city skylines as a proxy for locality · anyone in a suit striding anywhere · a person viewed from behind facing a screen or a cityscape *(observed as an AI-generated collage on iPullRank, 2026-09-01)* · laptops on white backgrounds · any photograph that implies clients, scale, offices or headcount that do not exist · **any image of a person who does not work at MappedSkills.**

## 3. Illustration — DECISION

> ### **RECOMMENDATION: LIMITED, AND STRICTLY EXPLANATORY. Custom illustration in the conventional sense is ABSENT.**
> **The entire illustration budget goes into a small number of high-quality explanatory figures. No decorative illustration, no character illustration, no spot illustration, no metaphorical scenes.**

### 3.1 Why
- Illustration in this category is where clichés live: growth arrows, rockets, funnels, brains, magnifiers, blobs. **Observed on upGrowth, 2026-09-01:** a flat vector businessman climbing a staircase made from an ascending arrow.
- AI-generated collage is now itself a convention. **Observed on iPullRank, 2026-09-01.**
- The session brief §22 is explicit: do not recommend illustration merely to fill empty areas. On this site the empty areas are where the apparatus goes.
- Most decisively: **a diagram that explains something is worth more here than an illustration that decorates it**, and the approved copy repeatedly asks for explanation rather than atmosphere.

### 3.2 The conceptual role of the figures — what they actually are
Not illustration. **Instruments of explanation.** Each exists to save the reader from holding a structure in their head, and the approved copy names each need explicitly.

| Figure | The copy that asks for it | What it must show |
|---|---|---|
| **The run** | Homepage Block 3 — "the enquiry chain as one continuous object… Copy alone makes the reader hold six stages in their head" | Six points, the accountability boundary, dashed unmeasured stretches, unattached arrivals, the client-data segment |
| **The four-cause decision tree** | Problem page Move 2 — "prose forces the reader to hold four branches in their head" | Demand · Page · Path · Measurement, with measurement marked as the one to check first |
| **The four AI layers** | `AI_SEARCH_LANGUAGE.md` §6 — "The whole finding would be invisible under any single score" | Retrieval working, three layers at zero, per system, two systems not testable |
| **The attribution gap** | Homepage Block 4 · `/how-it-works` §4 | What is measured, what is not, what cannot be — at true proportion |
| **The five-check sequence** | Problem page Move 5 | Each check ending in something observable |

**Quality bar:** these are the site's principal visual assets and are the things most likely to be screenshotted, quoted or cited. They deserve more craft than a hero image would have received.

### 3.3 Prohibited
Character illustration · mascots · isometric scenes · spot illustration to break up text · metaphorical scenes · AI-generated imagery of any kind · abstract 3D · gradient meshes · geometric blobs · anything from the prohibited motif list in `07_VISUAL_GRAMMAR.md` §6.3.

## 4. Iconography — DECISION

> ### **RECOMMENDATION: MINIMAL AND FUNCTIONAL ONLY. Icons are UI affordances, never content.**

### 4.1 The jobs icons are permitted to do
1. **Navigation and interaction affordances** — external link, expand/collapse, copy, download, filter, close, back.
2. **Contact channel identification** — phone, WhatsApp, email. These are genuine channel identifiers, they matter for the India-first buyer, and they are recognised rather than decorative.
3. **Evidence status marks** — the shape channel of the status vocabulary. These are *marks*, not icons: geometric, abstract, always paired with a text label, and part of the type system rather than an icon set.

**Every icon in categories 1 and 2 carries an accessible name. No icon is ever the sole carrier of meaning.**

### 4.2 What icons must never do — binding
- **Represent a capability.** No icon for SEO, AI search, conversion, measurement, paid media or automation.
- **Appear in the pattern `icon + title + two lines of text`, repeated in a card grid.** This is the generic SaaS pattern the brief names in §23, and it is already prohibited for MappedSkills' homepage independently: Block 5 states the capability list "must not be rendered as a grid of equal-weight cards."
- **Decorate a heading, a list bullet, a section or a CTA.**
- **Signify a value or a quality** — no shield for trust, no target for accuracy, no lightbulb for insight.
- **Signify AI.** No sparkle, no chip, no brain, no chat bubble.

### 4.3 Why this is the right decision
Capability icons are the visual form of the service menu. `MESSAGING_ARCHITECTURE.md` §3 Level 5 prohibits capabilities being "presented as co-equal pillars, as a service menu, or as the reason to hire the firm." Six icons in six identical cards states exactly that, regardless of the words inside them. **Removing the icon set removes the temptation.**

## 5. Motif — cross-reference

The graphic motif decision (**the annotation tie**, with **the dashed segment** as its counterpart) is made in `07_VISUAL_GRAMMAR.md` §6 and is not repeated here. Note only that the motif is deliberately **not** an icon and **not** an illustration — it is a typographic mark, which is why it survives at every size, in greyscale, in print, and as a focus indicator.

## 6. Summary of the four decisions

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Photography** | **Highly selective.** Named, identified people and real specific places only. No stock, ever. No atmosphere photography. Environmental, unposed, minimally treated |
| **Illustration** | **Limited and strictly explanatory.** Conventional illustration absent. The budget goes entirely into five explanatory figures, built to a high craft standard |
| **Iconography** | **Minimal and functional.** UI affordances, contact channels, and evidence status marks. **No capability icons. No icon-card grid** |
| **Graphic motif** | **Yes** — the annotation tie, plus the dashed segment for honest absence. Both derived from the strategy. Both typographic marks, not pictures |
