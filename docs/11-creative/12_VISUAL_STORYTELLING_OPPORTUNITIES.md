**Scope discipline, stated first.** This document **identifies and classifies** places in the approved Session 11 copy that should eventually be communicated visually rather than as prose. It does **not** redesign any of them, does not specify a layout, and **does not alter a single word of approved copy.** Every referenced section stays exactly as approved; what changes later, at the design phase, is the *medium* through which some of it reaches the reader.

**Why this matters commercially.** The owner explicitly does not want a text-heavy website. The approved copy is dense in exactly the places where it is carrying a structure — six stages, four causes, five checks, four layers, five conditions — and prose is the wrong instrument for a structure. **Converting structures into figures is how the site gets shorter without getting less honest.**

## 1. Classification scheme

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **DIAGRAM** | A relationship between parts of one system |
| **COMPARISON** | Two or more things set against each other on shared dimensions |
| **SEQUENCE** | Ordered stages with inputs and outputs |
| **DIAGNOSTIC** | Something the reader operates to reach a conclusion about themselves |
| **ANNOTATED EVIDENCE** | A real artefact with marks and provenance attached |
| **DECISION TREE** | Branching logic ending in distinct outcomes |
| **DATA VISUALISATION** | Measured values, with sample, date and method |
| **STATE CHANGE** | The same object shown in two or more conditions |
| **INTERACTIVE EXPLANATION** | A figure whose variant the reader selects |

Building the run once, properly — responsive, static-complete, keyboard-reachable, with a text equivalent — covers most of priority 1 and 2. That is also what makes it recognisable across the site, which is the point of having a recurring figure at all.

## 5. Three rules governing all of the above

1. **A figure replaces the prose that carried the structure — it does not sit beside it as an illustration of it.** If both the paragraph and the diagram survive, the page got longer and nothing was achieved.
2. **No figure may state more than the copy states.** A diagram implying a delivery process, a lead score, a ranking, a trend, a guarantee, or a result that does not exist is fabricated proof — and a figure is *more* likely to be screenshotted, quoted and cited than a sentence.
3. **Every figure has a complete static state and a text or tabular equivalent.** These are the site's most citable assets; they must survive being printed, screenshotted, read by a screen reader, and quoted by a system that cannot see them.
