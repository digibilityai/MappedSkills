## 1. SLICE 1 — Homepage hero

**File:** `prototype/01-homepage-hero.html`

### 1.1 What is on the first screen
Navigation · the approved H1 verbatim · the approved supporting copy verbatim · two action
objects. **Four elements, no fifth.** Nothing else.

### 1.2 What is deliberately absent
| Absent | Why |
|---|---|
| The fifth first-screen slot — one dated checkable item | **Case A omitted.** The homepage first-screen apparatus exception is approved but has **no qualifying occupant at launch** (`24` §4–5). Nothing is drawn at that position: no container, no placeholder, no dotted outline, no label |

### 1.3 How it establishes commercial confidence without a gimmick
The H1 sets at **76px at WIDE across two lines**, occupying nearly the full content width, with
a very large jump to the 20px supporting copy and a further jump to the 17px actions. There are
three type sizes on the screen and one accent mark. **The confidence is carried by scale
contrast and by the sentence itself.**

The two actions are definite objects — square edges, solid or outlined, high contrast against a
quiet ground — with the enquiry route filled and booking outlined. They are the same size, in
the same region, with the same type treatment.

### 1.4 Contradiction reported, not fixed
The Session 14 brief §7 describes the second hero action as a *"secondary booking action."* The
approved wireframe `31_WIREFRAME_MASTER_HOMEPAGE.md` and `21_ORCHESTRATOR_DECISIONS_H1_H5.md`
(H1) describe **two `[ACT]` objects at equal confidence.**

**Rendered per the approved artefact** — identical size, region and type treatment, differing
only in fill — because Session 14 has no authority to alter approved UX. **The wording difference
is reported for the orchestrator and was not resolved here.**

### 1.5 The one thing this slice cannot prove
Whether the hero survives the arrival of the fifth element. The dated checkable item is a real
future occupant of that screen, and a five-element first screen is a different composition from
a four-element one. **This is recorded as a residual risk, not as a pass.**

No hover carries meaning · no colour carries meaning (the status distinction is **shape + word**,

**This is a real gap in the proof and it is stated as one.** The state pair is the territory's

## 3. SLICE 3 — F1, the run

**File:** `prototype/03-f1-the-run.html`

### 3.1 Class and structure
**A — FULL** (Block 3) with **C — STATE** (Block 4) **on the same instance**, not a second
figure. Both states are drawn, so the state change is demonstrable rather than described.

### 3.2 Minimum static version — every required element present

| `19` F1 requirement | In the drawing |
|---|---|
| One continuous object | A single chain, one stroke weight |
| Six approved points labelled | discovery and source · landing · interaction · the enquiry · the qualified enquiry · opportunity and revenue |
| Accountability boundary drawn | A vertical mark-coloured rule crossing the chain between the qualified enquiry and opportunity/revenue, labelled |
| Unmeasured stretch dashed | A dashed lead-in upstream of the first point: *demand we cannot see — not measured* |
| ≥1 arrival with no upstream segment | A dashed stub arriving at *the enquiry* from below, terminating in an open diamond, labelled *counted, never assigned to a channel* |
| Client-data segment marked as theirs | The post-boundary segment drawn as a **double line**, labelled *reported from your system* |
| Intervention marks (`16` §4 A1) | Three marks above the line at the **joins**, labelled *work is done here — the joins are what decide the result* |
| Complete statically, no motion, no colour dependence | There is no motion on the page and no hue carries meaning |
| Text or tabular equivalent | A `<table>` follows each state, plus `<title>`/`<desc>` |

### 3.3 Why the dashed stretch is upstream rather than mid-chain
Dashing a segment the firm actually measures would state something false. The honest unmeasured
stretch is the demand that exists **before any touch the firm can see** — which the approved hero
copy already states in words: *"There is buyer demand for what you sell, and some of it never
reaches you."* The gap is drawn where it genuinely is.

### 3.4 Why it cannot become a funnel
**Structurally, not by discipline.** Stroke width, node size and node spacing are constants in
the stylesheet. There is no class that narrows a path, no per-stage colour, no arrowhead, no
numbered stage, no chevron and no icon. *"The distance between the run and a funnel is one
designer's decision about tapering"* — this vocabulary does not offer that decision.

### 3.5 Arrival as an event, without breaking node equality
`16` §4 A5 asks the run to terminate in an **event**, not a node. All six points are drawn
**identically**, because unequal node sizes are the first step toward a funnel. *The enquiry* is
marked instead by a concentric open square around the point — an arrival registered — and by the
unattached stub converging on it. **A judgement call, and it is flagged for review**: it may be
too quiet to read as an event.

### 3.6 What it is not
Not a logo · not a background · not a watermark · not a divider · not a page transition · not a
loader · not a header element · not a repeated signature. It appears **once**. It carries no
branded name for the method, the chain or any stage.

The largest thing in this section is **"After that, the report is enquiries."** — a capability

## 6. NARROW vs WIDE — findings

Tested in the browser at **1400px (WIDE)**, **900px (MEDIUM)** and **390px (NARROW)**.

### 6.1 What was verified

| Check | WIDE | MEDIUM | NARROW |
|---|---|---|---|
| Page body scrolls horizontally | **No** | **No** | **No** |
| Apparatus margin present | Yes | **No — re-parents inline** | No — inline |
| DOM order (claim, then its apparatus) | Unchanged | Unchanged | Unchanged |
| Limits open and not collapsible | Yes | Yes | **Yes** |
| Wide figure | Native size | Scrolls inside its own container | Replaced by the vertical form |

### 6.2 Findings, in order of consequence

**F-R1 — Figures must be *swapped* at MEDIUM, not scaled or scrolled.**
At 900px the wide figure scrolls inside its own container. That obeys the no-horizontal-body-
scroll rule, but it is the wrong answer: a reader should not have to scroll a figure sideways to
read an argument. The correct behaviour is the **genuine vertical form**, and the threshold at
which it takes over is a Gate 10 decision. **Scaling is not an option** — see F-R2.

**F-R2 — Scaling an SVG scales its labels below the body minimum.**
`max-width: 100%` on a 1120px-wide figure inside a 700px column renders 16px labels at ~10px,
which breaches `13` §2.2. The stylesheet therefore does not scale figures at all. **This is a
binding implementation rule discovered by rendering, and it is a Gate 10 input.**

**F-R3 — MEDIUM is where the boundary could silently become a footnote, and does not.**
At WIDE the limits sit at full measure; at MEDIUM the apparatus margin disappears around them.
Because limits are **not** in the margin system — they are argument-register text at full measure
— nothing about them changes. Keeping limits out of the apparatus margin entirely is what makes
the MEDIUM transformation safe, and it is proposed as a rule.

**F-R4 — Container queries, not viewport queries.**
The proof uses container-relative type (`cqi`) and container queries, so a narrow column renders
as genuinely narrow regardless of screen size. Without this, a 390px artboard on a 1400px screen
renders wide type in a narrow box and the responsive proof is worthless. Proposed for retention.

**F-R5 — The H1 measure is a real design variable.**
At a 17ch measure the approved H1 set in three lines and left the right half of the frame empty —
which is anti-pattern **A12**, the exact device observed on a competitor. At a 30ch measure with
balanced wrapping it sets in two lines and occupies the width. **The hero's success depends on a
measure value**, which is a Gate 10 number, and it is recorded here so it is not rediscovered
late.

**F-R6 — The four-element first screen fits at NARROW.** H1, supporting copy and both stacked
actions are reachable without the bridge or either action being pushed out of the opening screen
at 390×844.

## 7. Accessibility and usability findings

### 7.1 Contrast — computed, not estimated
Ink **15.86:1** · apparatus ink **8.77:1 (AAA)** · accent **6.58:1** · set-piece rule **8.06:1**,
all against the ground. **The apparatus register meets AAA**, which answers the "is the
provenance readable?" question numerically.

**One constraint this produced:** `--ink-recess` is **4.43:1** — below the 4.5:1 text floor. It
is therefore restricted to figure strokes and may never carry text. Recessive figure *labels* use
the apparatus ink.

### 7.2 Focus — verified in the browser
`3px solid` in the accent at `3px` offset, from a single `:focus-visible` rule that no component
overrides. The focus indicator and the annotation tie are the same mark, so the most commonly
neglected accessibility affordance is part of the identity.

### 7.3 Keyboard and no-JavaScript — verified
There is **no `<script>` tag and no inline handler on any page.** Every disclosure is a native
`<details>`, so it is keyboard-operable and works with scripting disabled. The failure state
`45` STATE 4 exists to prohibit — *a control that renders, does nothing, and leaves content
hidden* — **cannot occur**, because there is no script that could fail.

### 7.4 No hover-only and no colour-only meaning
Nothing is revealed by hover. Every status carries **shape + word**; colour contributes nothing
to any status, state or figure. Links are underlined, not colour-only.

### 7.5 Greyscale — run in the browser
With all colour removed, `measured` (filled square), `unmeasured` (open square) and `not testable`
(hatched square) remain distinct by shape, and each carries its word. **A measured zero and a
not-testable state cannot be confused in greyscale** — the one accessibility rule on this site
that is also an honesty rule.

### 7.6 Print — implemented, **not verified**
A print stylesheet forces every disclosure open and removes the control, per `45` STATE 3. It uses
both `::details-content` and a display override because browser support for forcing a closed
`<details>` open in print is inconsistent. **No print output was produced or inspected in this
session, so this is not claimed as verified.** It is a Gate 10 test.

### 7.7 Reduced motion
Trivially satisfied: there is no motion. A `prefers-reduced-motion` block exists as a guard for
anything added later.

### 7.8 Body copy
18px at a 66ch measure with 1.62 line-height. Nothing important is set below 16px anywhere,
including apparatus, captions and figure labels.

### 8.2 "Four AI assistants" — a precision item, flagged rather than asserted as a defect
Approved homepage copy (Block 6) says *"four AI assistants… 28 times"* and *"the two systems we
could not test at all."* The research baseline records **28 unbranded runs across five surfaces**
(Google AI Mode 19, Perplexity 3, Google AI Overviews 3, ChatGPT 2, Gemini 1) and describes
Claude and Grok as *"two of the six systems named in the charter."*

These are **probably reconcilable** — four *assistants* across five *surfaces*, two of them
Google's — and Session 14 does not assert a contradiction. But the reconciliation is not written
down anywhere, and **F3 cannot be drawn without it**, because F3's whole content is per-system
separation. Session 14 avoided the question by not decomposing the measurement per system.

**Recommendation: resolve the system enumeration in writing before F3 is designed.** It is not a
blocker for this session's four slices.
