What this usually looks like from the inside

Buyers who need what you sell are searching, and finding somebody else. Or the visits arrive and the enquiries do not. Or enquiries do arrive, and nobody can say which came from where — so nobody can say what to do more of.

That reads like three problems. It behaves like one.

More visits on a path that does not work produce nothing. A path that works, fed the wrong visits, produces nothing worth quoting. And with no measurement in place, all three failures look identical from the outside.

  • sessions up and enquiries flat
  • enquiries stopped after a site change
  • enquiries only ever from one channel
  • the enquiries that do arrive are not worth quoting
  • no idea whether anything arrives at all

If that is the shape of it, this page is about exactly that

How the work produces an enquiry

Three connected parts. Reach the buyers already looking — organic search first, and paid where it is the right source for you. Make the route from that first visit to a sent enquiry work. Instrument the whole chain, so an enquiry records where it came from at the moment it is made.

They are different skills, which is why they are described separately. What decides the result is usually the joins between them: between the search work and the page it lands on, between the page and the form, between the form and wherever the enquiry is supposed to arrive, and between all of it and the measurement. Where responsibility is split across suppliers, those joins can end up belonging to nobody. Here they belong to us.

What any engagement covers is decided with you. The measurement is the part that is not optional, because nothing we report about the outcome means anything until it works.

What we are accountable for, and where that stops

We are accountable for the qualified enquiry — one that is real, reachable, from a business context, with a stated need. What counts as one is published, and it is the number we report against a starting point we capture before anything changes.

Where you give us your close data, we report through to the deals and the revenue your own system recorded. Those are your numbers, from your system, and we state what we did and did not control alongside them.

Some enquiries arrive with no usable trail at all. Someone types your name into a browser, or calls, or was told about you by a customer. We record those as unattributed rather than assigning them to a channel — and a gap in the measurement never takes an enquiry out of the count.

We do not promise rankings, a position in AI answers, a number of enquiries, revenue, or a return on spend. None of that is ours to control, and a guarantee would be a claim about a business we do not run.

Who this is built for

This works for businesses where a customer takes time to decide and one good enquiry is worth real money — where buyers for what you sell already exist, or can reasonably be reached. It works where you can act on the enquiries when they arrive, and where you are willing to let us measure the path properly. That last one is not negotiable: without it, nothing we tell you afterwards can be checked.

It is a poor fit if the demand has to be created from nothing, if what you sell is bought on impulse or on price alone, or if you need somebody to guarantee the outcome. Those are legitimate requirements. They are not what this is.
