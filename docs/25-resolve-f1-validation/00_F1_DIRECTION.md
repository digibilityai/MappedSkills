## 1. The question this had to answer

Can F1 live naturally inside THE RESOLVE without becoming another resolve animation, competing
with the hero, returning the site to document territory, becoming a SaaS/data diagram, implying
fabricated quantities, or making the page feel more technical?

## 2. The compatibility problem, stated exactly

THE RESOLVE's signature gesture is already a **population changing state**: a field of identical
anonymous marks becomes a few distinct named objects. In the 15C prototype that gesture is applied
to the hero, to capture/convert/measure, to the commercial argument, to the evidence and to the
CTA — **seven times**, which 15C itself recorded as its highest open risk (`03_SELF_CRITIQUE_AND_TESTS.md`
R1) and which `docs/24-resolve-governance/02_RESOLVE_MOTION_BUDGET.md` now caps.

**If F1 were drawn as marks that resolve, it would be the eighth instance of the site's one
gesture.** The figure would stop explaining and start decorating, and the gesture would become the
tic the budget exists to prevent.

## 3. The interpretation — one only

> ### The signature is about **STATE** — what a thing *is*.
> ### F1 is about **STRUCTURE** — how things *connect*.
> ### So F1 contains **no state change at all**.

One continuous object. Six points on it. Nothing resolves, nothing changes appearance, no mark
becomes a different mark. The reader's eye travels a path instead of watching a population
transform.

This is what makes F1 **the same visual family in a different visual role**, which is the target
the session brief set.

### 3.1 How the difference is carried — seven channels, not one

| Channel | The signature gesture | F1 |
|---|---|---|
| **Object count** | A population — hundreds of marks | **One** continuous object |
| **What happens** | Marks change state | **Nothing changes state** |
| **Geometry** | A vertical stack of horizontal bars | A single traversed line |
| **Silhouette** | Tall and dense | **Wide and low** (horizontal), or a single thin column (vertical) |
| **Ground** | The hero's **dark stage** | The ordinary **ivory** ground — no stage, no card, no panel |
| **Direction** | Top-to-bottom accumulation | Left-to-right traversal |
| **Motion** | A 900 ms population resolve | One 900 ms **line draw** — a reveal of extent, not a change of state |

**None of these depends on animation.** With motion off, the silhouette, the ground, the geometry
and the single-object-ness all still distinguish it. That was a deliberate design constraint, not
a happy result.

### 3.2 Deliberately not used to create the distinction
A card, a panel, a bounding box, a tinted band, a different typeface, a second accent, an icon
set, or any device from THE ARRIVAL. The brief's instruction not to solve it with a generic
container was followed.

## 4. What is drawn, and why each element is there

Every element below is required by the approved F1 specification
(`docs/11-creative/19_FIGURE_PRIORITISATION.md` F1 minimum static version, and
`docs/16-ux/11_FIGURE_UX.md` §1 entry state).

| Element | Drawn as | Approved requirement it satisfies |
|---|---|---|
| The six points | Six **identical** ink squares, **equal spacing** | *"six approved points labelled"*, and the anti-taper rule |
| The chain | **One** unbroken 2px ink stroke | *"one continuous object"* |
| Buyer demand before it | A **dashed** lead-in in `--gapc` | *"unmeasured stretches dashed"* |
| Where work is done | Small **tangerine ticks crossing** the line at the joins | The intervention marks (`16` §4 A1); *"the joins are what decide the result"* |
| An arrival with no upstream | An **open square** with a dashed stub into *the enquiry* | *"at least one arrival with no upstream segment"* |
| Accountability boundary | A **rule crossing** the chain after the qualified enquiry, labelled | *"the accountability boundary drawn"* |
| The client's own data | The post-boundary segment as a **double line** | *"the client-data segment marked as the client's"* |
| What we answer for | The qualified-enquiry point in **`--tang-ink`** | Tangerine means *commercially ours* in THE RESOLVE |

### 4.1 Two decisions worth recording

**The intervention marks are ticks, not squares.** They began as small tangerine squares and were
changed because a small tangerine square differs from a point only by **size and colour** — a
colour-borne distinction, and one that invited reading them as extra stages. A **tick crossing the
line** is unmistakably a mark made *on* the chain rather than a point *in* it. **A diamond was
considered and rejected**: in a diagram a diamond reads as a flowchart decision, which is exactly
the SaaS drift this session had to avoid.

**Tangerine is `--tang-ink`, not `--tang`.** On the ivory ground `--tang` (`#FF5B04`) measures
**2.81:1**, under the 3:1 WCAG non-text-contrast floor for a meaningful graphical object.
THE RESOLVE's palette already carries the answer — the same mark, darkened for light grounds.
`--tang-ink` measures **3.97:1**. `--tang` remains correct where 15C uses it: on the dark hero
stage and the full-bleed close.

## 5. Where the annotations live — the anti-document decision

A figure carrying six point labels **plus** four scattered annotations is a technical diagram, and
scattered annotation is one of the named document-drift signals.

**The four honesty annotations are consolidated into a single legend row** — the same `.marks`
pattern the 15C evidence card uses, so the device is native rather than invented. That turns four
floating labels into **one compositional object**, which is the main structural guard against
document drift in this figure.

## 6. Motion — one event, and it is the marginal call

**Budget spent: 0 major resolve moments, 1 sectional explanatory event.** Within
`02_RESOLVE_MOTION_BUDGET.md`.

The one event is the chain **drawing once**, left to right, on entering view. Nothing loops,
nothing is ambient, nothing is scroll-scrubbed, nothing waits, and it never replays.

> **Reported honestly: the static state and the animated end state are identical, so the draw adds
> no information.** It adds emphasis on continuity — which is F1's actual argument — but the brief
> is explicit that F1 may be largely static. **This is the first thing to cut if the homepage
> feels busy after the hero, and cutting it costs nothing and requires no redesign.**

## 7. Content hierarchy

**COMMERCIAL IDEA → FIGURE → SHORT CLARIFICATION.** The section opens on the approved Block 3
heading at display scale, carries two short approved paragraphs, then the figure, then a
four-item legend and one illustrative note. The approved "joins" paragraph is deliberately
compressed because `19` §0 rule 2 requires a figure to **replace** the prose that carried the
structure rather than sit beside it.

## 8. What this does not do

It does not design the homepage, does not build a design system, does not modify the Session 15C
prototype, does not reopen the creative direction, and does not touch F1's approved meaning,
taxonomy or route assignment. **`F17` is BLOCKED and appears nowhere.**
