## 1. Classification

> ### **COMPATIBLE BUT DISTINCT**

**Not REINFORCING**, and deliberately so. Making F1 reinforce the signature would mean giving it
the same population-resolving gesture — which would be the eighth instance of the site's one move
and would collapse the distinction between *activity* and *structure* that the figure exists to
draw. **Reinforcement here would be a defect wearing the costume of coherence.**

**Not COMPETING.** Measured against the signature on every channel that matters: F1 is one object
where the signature is a population, still where the signature transforms, wide-and-low where the
signature is tall-and-dense, on the ivory ground where the signature owns the dark stage. On
first fixation the section's largest element is the approved commercial heading at 67px; the
figure's largest text is 17.9px.

## 6. Does the page feel more technical? — **No, and here is why**

The figure adds **one line, six squares, three ticks, one open square, one rule and a doubled
segment** — 13 drawn elements. It removes the need for the approved "joins" paragraph, which is
the trade `19` §0 rule 2 requires. The section's reading load is a display heading, two short
paragraphs, six two-word labels and a four-item legend.

**The honest residual:** the figure is *quiet*. On a bounded slice with nothing above or below it,
the composition reads slightly airy — a lot of ivory to the right of the headline. On the real
homepage the adjacent blocks would occupy that. This is a composition question for the homepage
session, not a defect in the figure.

## 7. Marketing-company test — **performed literally**

**Method, stated so it is not overclaimed:** a stylesheet applying `filter: blur(5px)` to every
text-bearing element was injected and the page screenshotted at 1440×900. **This was a real blur
render, not a visual approximation.**

**Result:** confident asymmetric composition — a large display mass top-left, generous warm
ground, one restrained horizontal instrument low in the frame with a single accent point. It
reads as a premium contemporary marketing company. It does **not** read as a research paper, an
audit report, a dashboard or a developer tool.

**Limit:** one section at one width, judged by one reader. No user testing.

# Session 17C — Owner Feedback and Motion Refinement

**Date:** 2026-09-03 · **Owner feedback, binding:** *"F1 works but feels too static."*

**Interpretation applied:** the visual direction is **accepted**. The figure is **not redesigned**,
its structural concept is unchanged, and no alternative was created. **Motion only was refined.**

## 12. What actually changed, and why the 17B motion fell flat

**Nothing in the composition changed.** Geometry, node positions, spacing, labels, legend, colours
and copy are byte-identical in intent to the 17B artboards. Only the animation was rebuilt.

**The diagnosis:** in 17B the chain drew while the annotations faded in on *fixed delays unrelated
to where the drawing had reached*. Everything happened at roughly the same time and nothing
appeared to be **caused** by anything else. It read as "a figure appearing", not as "a system
being traversed" — which is exactly the flatness the owner named.

> **The fix is not more motion. It is causality.** Every event is now keyed to the position of
> the drawing front, so the section reads as one thing progressing through a system with things
> happening *as it arrives*.

## 13. The sequence — measured from the rendered page

state-independent and therefore reliable in this environment.

## 14. What each motion means — the "what does this motion mean?" test

| Motion | Meaning | Would removing it lose meaning? |
|---|---|---|
| The chain drawing | The chain is **one object, traversed in order** | Yes — order and continuity are the figure's argument |
| A point scaling up as the front arrives | This point is **registered on the chain**, in sequence | Yes — it is what ties each point to the progression |
| A tick struck across the line | **A mark is being made here** — work is done at this join | Yes — a tick that simply appears reads as a label, not an act |
| The open square and its dashed stub appearing at the enquiry | **An enquiry arrives with no upstream segment** | Yes — arriving is the point |
| The boundary growing **outward from the chain** | Accountability **asserts itself** as a condition of the chain | Yes — a boundary that fades in reads as an annotation, not a limit |
| The doubled segment drawing last, after the boundary | What lies beyond is **reported from someone else's system** | Yes — the ordering is the meaning |

**Nothing survived that could not answer the question.** There is no shimmer, no pulse, no
ambient movement, no travelling dot, orb, comet or particle, no glow. **What moves is the drawing
of the structure itself.**

## 18. Trigger and replay

Fires **once**, when 40 % of the drawing is inside a viewport inset by 12 % at the bottom — so it
waits for a genuine reading position rather than one pixel, and the threshold is reachable at every
width (verified: stage 235 px at WIDE, 557 px at MEDIUM, 575 px at NARROW, each shorter than its
inset root; measured ratio 0.96 at NARROW). The observer **unobserves on first intersection** and
nothing removes the class, so **it cannot replay during scrolling**; leaving and returning finds
the figure complete — verified.

## 19. One defect this refinement introduced, found by rendering, and fixed

> **The draw technique destroyed the honesty dashes.**

`.draw` sets `stroke-dasharray: var(--len)` to animate `stroke-dashoffset`. Applied to the two
`--gapc` lines, it **overwrote their 5-5 dash pattern** — and that pattern is the shape channel
carrying **"not measured"** and **"unattributed"**. The lines rendered solid, silently converting
two honesty states into ordinary strokes.

**Fix:** dashed elements **fade**; they are never drawn. A comment in the stylesheet records the
constraint so it cannot be reintroduced. Verified after the fix: `stroke-dasharray` computes to
`5px, 5px` on both lines at every width, and both render visibly dashed.

**This is the most important finding of the session.** A motion technique quietly overwrote a
semantic property, and only rendering caught it.
