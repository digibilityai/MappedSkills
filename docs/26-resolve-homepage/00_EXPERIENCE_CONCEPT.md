## 1. The owner verdict, and what it actually said

> *"No, I think it is still bland, no animation, no exciting, no journey happening."*

This is **binding** and it **supersedes** the Session 17B/17C recommendation that F1 was ready.

**The reading this session took.** The complaint names four things — bland, no animation, not
exciting, no journey — and only one of them is about animation. The load-bearing word is
**journey**. Session 17C answered a question nobody asked: *how do we animate a diagram?* The
owner was describing a **missing page**, not a missing transition. A figure evaluated on a bounded
slice of ivory, with nothing above it and nothing after it, cannot be anything but a diagram. It
had no before and no after, so it could not be part of a journey; there was no journey to be part
of.

**Therefore the validation method changed.** No Session 17D. No fourth isolated F1. The homepage
is built, and F1 is judged **inside** it.

**The failure being corrected is a category error, not a craft error.** 17C's craft was sound —
seven defects were found and fixed there, and its channel-separation analysis survives. What it
could not do, by construction, was tell us whether the figure belongs.

## 2. The experience idea

> ### ONE POPULATION, FOLLOWED DOWN, AT CHANGING ALTITUDE.

THE RESOLVE gives a native metaphor: **anonymous activity becomes commercial consequence.** Session
15C applied it as a *gesture repeated seven times*. Applied as a **page-level experience** instead,
it stops being a gesture and becomes a **camera**.

The page is one commercial story told at six distances from the same object:

| Distance | Chapter | What the visitor is looking at |
|---|---|---|
| **FAR** | Hero | A population. Anonymous, then resolved into named objects |
| **LEAVING** | Departure | The population goes. **One mark carries on** |
| **NEARER** | Recognition | Three routes that fail differently and end identically |
| **CLOSER** | The re-read | The same six kinds, read under three commercial questions |
| **INSIDE** | The run (F1) | The structure that mark has been travelling through |
| **ACROSS** | Boundary | A rule crosses the structure you are standing in |
| **OUT** | Close | Full-bleed, and they land |

**The atom never changes.** A small square is: a lattice mark → a named object → a route's
progress → a re-sorted kind → a point on the chain → an arrival. Nothing on this page is a new
illustration, and no section introduces a vocabulary the previous one did not already teach.

**This is why it is a journey and not a stack.** A stack of modules can be reordered without loss.
This cannot: each chapter is only legible because of the distance the previous one established.

## 3. What "vibrant" was taken to mean

Per the brief, **not** more colours, particles, glow, or motion on everything. The energy comes
from five sources, none of which is decoration:

1. **Changing scale.** The descent into F1 is a genuine change of distance, not a fade-in.
2. **Spatial reorganisation.** The re-read physically moves six rows to new positions because the
   commercial question changed.
3. **Compositional surprise.** Three routes of visibly different length end in one column.
4. **Typographic scale.** Display statements at 4–6rem, chapter numerals at up to 7.6rem, against
   0.82rem eyebrows — a range of roughly 9×.
5. **Stillness that is earned.** The boundary chapter and the capability list do not move at all,
   and they read as deliberate because the chapters around them do.

## 4. What was deliberately refused

| Refused | Why |
|---|---|
| A persistent spine or thread down the page | F1's dashed line means **an epistemic gap** (B23). A decorative rule motif would dilute the honest device — and the F1 taxonomy §5 prohibits exactly this |
| Sticky/pinned scroll sequences | Zero in the budget, zero in 15C, and they gate the CTA |
| Repeating the resolve per section | The `R1` tic risk. One T1, and it is spent on the proposition |
| A second segmented control | The Traffic/Enquiries control is *the* signature interaction; a second one dilutes it |
| Service cards, dashboards, logo clouds, blobs, glass, neon | §20 of the brief, and `docs/16-ux/18_UX_ANTI_PATTERNS.md` |
| Bars or widths in the re-read | Any width is a magnitude, and a magnitude is a quantity claim |
