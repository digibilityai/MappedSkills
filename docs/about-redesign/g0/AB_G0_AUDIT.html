/about: G0 audit and design direction

This is a read-only review of the live page (https://mappedskills.com/about, HTTP 200) and its origin/main source (a6d75b7, components/cplus/about/). Nothing was implemented, committed or deployed. The page was measured at 1440×900, 820×1180 and 390×844 in Chromium (playwright-core), with the consent banner declined. Raw figures are in screenshots/measurements.json. Brand basis: MappedSkills Brand DNA v1.0, plus the released pages /services (DEC-059) and /how-it-works (DEC-058).

The recommendation in one paragraph. The live page has one strong idea: everyone reports on their own part, and nobody reports on the enquiry. It makes that point in five of its eight sections. It never says who MappedSkills is, and it offers no contact route until 87% of the way down the page (6,279 px of 7,256 on desktop). The redesign should say the idea once and show it once, then use the space it frees up for what an About page is for: who you will work with, why the business works this way, and where its job starts and ends. That takes five sections instead of eight, with a CTA in the hero. The visual story is "one line": the live comparison figure, simplified, becomes the page's single interaction. Three separate suppliers' reports resolve into one line that ends where the enquiry reaches the client.

What is already working

  • The hook. "Everyone has a report" names a frustration a business owner recognises straight away. It is the Brand DNA "core frustration" in customer language.
  • The report slips. Three ticks and one dashed gap tell the story without a paragraph. The idea is honest and on-brand: hatched or dashed fills mark uncertainty.
  • The comparison figure is built well. Both views are real text, it works without JavaScript, it never relies on colour alone, and it does not move on load. It is the page's best visual asset.
  • The Ours / Yours boundary and the "What we do not promise" chips are clear, honest and distinctive. Few agencies publish this.
  • The fit section filters out the wrong buyers, which is useful for a small firm.
  • The tangerine close is a strong full-bleed moment, and it already pairs Contact with "Book a call".

What genuinely needs to improve

  1. Enquiry accountability is explained five times: in the hero lede, the slips, section 2 (three paragraphs and a callout), section 3 (a definition, Ours and Yours) and both views of the figure. The figure then asks again, "Whose report is the enquiry in?", which the hero already answered.
  2. It never says who MappedSkills is. No people, no location and no company in the body. "A small firm in Pune" appears only in the meta description, and Pune and Mumbai only in the footer. The page title promises "Who We Are", and the page does not deliver it. (The absence was an owner decision under DEC-050 and DEC-051, because no facts were confirmed. It is now the page's biggest trust gap.)
  3. The conversion path is buried. No hero CTA, nothing in between, and the close only after about 6,300 px. Every other released page offers Contact plus "Book a call" in the hero.
  4. It duplicates /how-it-works. Section 5 restates the six-step process, and the page links to /how-it-works twice with different labels ("See how the work runs", "Read the method").
  5. The self-measurement does not build trust here. To a business owner, "0 of 28" in large tangerine reads as "this firm is invisible", and it needs four sentences of caveats. The page omits the date (31 August 2026), and its count ("four AI assistants… two further systems") is worded differently from the dated record on /ai-seo ("five AI tools", DEC-054). It belongs to the AI search story, where it already lives in full.
  6. The copy is long and abstract for an About page: 808 words, with phrases like "measured against a starting point", "where measurement access is not a problem", and "Not a good fit: Not businesses that…" (a double negative). There are long dashes throughout, against the owner's no-long-dash rule.
  7. The figure needs one tap to make its point, and the button label "Show who answers for the enquiry" does not say what will change. Many visitors will only ever see the problem view.

3. Recommended page structure (five sections)

1 Hero (cream): who we are and what we stand for. Kicker "About MappedSkills". H1 (see §5). A two-sentence lede says who MappedSkills is and what it does. Contact plus "Book a call". Right: the three report slips and the dashed "Enquiries: not in any report", drawn still. It answers who.
2 "Why we work this way" (ink): one line. Two sentences: separate suppliers each report on their own part, so the enquiry can become nobody's job, and that is why the work is joined up. Then the single interaction: one button resolves three separate brackets into one tangerine line under Capture · Convert · Measure, ending where the enquiry reaches the client. It answers why.
3 "Where our job starts and ends" (paper). Ours / Yours, one sentence each. Five "We don't promise" chips. A fit pair (three short lines each). This is the honest, commercial part of the trust story.
4 "Who you'll work with" (cream). A short founder or team introduction in the owner's own words, with a real photograph if one is supplied (§6). Underneath: one process line plus HW-D01 report wording, linking to /how-it-works. Fallback if no people facts are approved: "How we work with you", three plain principles taken from existing approved copy ("We look before we recommend." "You approve the plan before work starts." "If something won't suit your business, we'll say so."). It answers why trust it.
5 Close (tangerine). "Tell us what's not working." A "What happens next" row with three confirmed facts. Contact as the primary CTA, Book a call as the secondary.

Estimate (WORKING HYPOTHESIS until a G1 mockup is measured): about 350 to 420 visible words against 808 today, and roughly 40% shorter on every width, mainly from saying the accountability idea once and removing the process and self-measurement blocks. The first CTA moves from about 6,300 px into the first screen.

4. Visual storytelling direction: "one line"

The page tells one story with one drawing, in three states. It is THE RESOLVE's own move (fragments resolve into something named), and it reuses the figure the owner has already approved in C+, so it is not a new invention. It also differs from /services ("the route", stations along a path) and /how-it-works (step cards): this page draws who answers for the path, not the path itself.

  • Calm, then one event, then calm. The only motion is the line resolving after a press: the brackets slide together and the tangerine line draws in about 400 ms, once. No autoplay, no loop, nothing sticky. Hover, press and focus states are 150 ms. Under reduced motion it switches instantly; without JavaScript both states show, one under the other, as they do now.
  • Tangerine marks the event only: the gap in the hero, the resolved line, and the CTAs.
  • The boundary is the same drawing. Section 3 reuses the line's end and the hatched "Yours" area, so the responsibility split is seen rather than explained.
  • People, if supplied, are shown plainly: one real photograph and a name with a role, set in the editorial grid. No stock imagery, no "team around a laptop" (Brand DNA p.11).
  • Responsive: horizontal from 1081 px up; vertical brackets (the current mobile pattern, which already works well) at 1080 px and below.

5. Proposed hero headline and primary CTA

About MappedSkills

Judge us on the enquiries, not the traffic.

We're MappedSkills, a small digital marketing team [in Pune: owner to confirm]. We help businesses get found by people already looking, turn more visits into enquiries, and see what's working.

Tell us what's not working → Book a call

Primary → /contact. Secondary → /schedule-call.

  • Why this H1: it is the Brand DNA's north star ("what we are remembered for"), and an About page is the natural home for it. It answers "why should I trust you?" in eight words, and it promises a standard, not an outcome. The lede then answers "who are you?" and states the method in plain English, without repeating /services.
  • Alternative: keep the live H1, "Everyone has a report. Who owns the enquiry?" It is a good hook, but it is a question about a problem, not about MappedSkills, and "owns" carries risk F6. It could move to section 2 as that section's H2.
  • Primary CTA: "Tell us what's not working" follows the Brand DNA CTA language, and the close's current wording ("Tell us what you're trying to fix") is close to it. One wording across hero and close.
