## Block 3 · From budget to enquiry — Path Figure (updated for F-2)

**Heading:** **From your budget to an enquiry — and where it can go wrong.**
**Lead-in:** Google Ads reports what it's set up to record. That isn't always the same as a genuine enquiry.

**Five steps:** 1 **Your budget** · 2 **Someone searches and clicks** · 3 **They visit your website** · 4 **They enquire** · 5 **We learn what worked, and decide what to change**

**Two rails (unchanged logic):**
- **What Google Ads can record:** ad shown · click · a "conversion" (*an action it has been set up to count, such as a form being sent or a button pressed*). Marks to step 4 **only where tracking is set up** (dashed "if set up" marker).
- **What your business needs to know:** whether each one was a **genuine enquiry** — a real person, reachable, asking about your work.

**Definition (the one retained industry word):** *Google Ads calls a recorded action a "conversion". We look at whether each one is a genuine enquiry.* **[PENDING — capability depends on tracking set-up]**

**"What if…" selectors and captions — revised so that no case implies automatic fixing:**

| Selector | Caption | Footer tag (dashed) |
|---|---|---|
| …the wrong people click | Clicks are recorded, but few are people you'd want to hear from. We review the searches and change what we manage. | *(none — within the core service)* |
| …the page doesn't answer them | People arrive and leave. Clicks are recorded; enquiries aren't. We tell you what we find and what we'd change. | **Changing the page: agreed separately** |
| …a recorded action isn't a genuine enquiry | Google Ads counts a conversion. On inspection, it was spam, a mistake or someone with no real interest. We tell you what we find and what needs fixing. | **Tracking repairs: agreed separately** |

**Closing line (qualified):**
> So we look past the report to the enquiries your business receives, as far as your tracking allows. Not every enquiry can be traced to an ad.

**Logic and F-2 alignment.**
1. *Cases 1* sit entirely inside the **core service** (searches, ads, budget), so no "separately" tag appears.
2. *Cases 2 and 3* sit in **oversight** (finding and explaining) and then in **separately agreed implementation** (changing the page, repairing tracking). The dashed footer tag appears **only** when the visitor selects those cases, so the figure itself teaches the boundary.
3. The figure never shows a fix being made. It shows a **finding and a recommendation**, then a dashed "agreed separately" node.
4. *Default (healthy) state:* all five nodes lit, no dashed node.
5. *Selected state:* the path breaks at the relevant step; steps after it dim; step 4 reads **Fewer genuine enquiries**; the dashed tag (cases 2 and 3) appears.
6. No numbers, rates or benchmarks anywhere.

**Interaction and accessibility.** Draw-in once on first view (≈ 1.2 s); selecting a case swaps state with a short transition. Native buttons in a `role="group"`; `aria-pressed`; one `aria-live="polite"` caption (caption and footer tag read together); "Show the healthy path" reset. *Mobile:* vertical path, rails as two labelled columns, selectors as a segmented control, dashed tag below the caption. *Reduced motion:* fully drawn at load; instant state changes. *No JS:* three stacked mini-paths, each with its caption and (cases 2 and 3) its dashed tag. *Text equivalent:* "A path with five steps from budget to learning. Google Ads can record clicks and, where set up, conversions. Your business confirms which are genuine enquiries. Where we find a problem with a page or tracking, we explain it and recommend a fix; the fix itself is agreed separately." Not the SEO trail component or geometry.

**Technical impact:** one new client component (primary graphic).
