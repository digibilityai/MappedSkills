Source: app/(pages)/how-it-works/page.tsx and components/cplus/how-it-works/*. Read-only: I changed no source, made no commits and deployed nothing. G1 has not started.

Summary. The page is accurate on the process (Discovery call → Audit → Proposal → Approval → Execution), technically sound and accessible in the automated checks. It is much longer than the redesigned pages: 2,012 words against 545 to 760, and 13.5 viewports on desktop and 20.9 on mobile. About 40% of the words sit in one section (measuring and reviewing). Most of that is attribution limits written in analytics language. There is no call to action between the hero and the close, and there are no links to service pages.

Two statements need correcting. Both need an owner decision:

  1. It promises a monthly review call to every client. DEC-057 withdrew that for Lead Generation.
  2. It implies that MappedSkills checks, qualifies and reports every client's enquiries. DEC-057 says Lead Generation enquiries go straight to the client and are not routinely received or reviewed.

Recommended G1 direction: one five-stage journey using the shared sequencer, keep the priority sort, a short monthly review block, and a condensed but visible "what we can't measure" section. That section stays because four released pages link here promising it. Target 750 to 950 words.

7. Inbound-link constraint (important for G1)

Four released pages link to /how-it-works with labels that promise attribution and measurement limits. Their wording can't change without reopening those pages:

  • /conversion-optimization: "The method, including what cannot be attributed"
  • /google-ads: "How we measure, including what can't be traced"
  • /about: "The method, published · Including what cannot be measured" and "Read the method"
  • /faq: "the method is published"
  • /lead-generation ("See how we work") and /contact ("See how the work runs") are neutral.

So G1 must keep a clearly headed "what we can and can't measure or trace" section. It can be shorter and plainer, but it must not be removed. A closed native disclosure was approved as an exception for /ai-seo (DEC-054). The owner deliberately restored these limits as always-visible here (OD-03, "never collapsed"), so a disclosure here needs its own decision.

8. Opportunities for simpler visual storytelling

  • One five-stage journey. Merge §2 and §3 into Discovery call → Audit → Findings and plan → Proposal → Your approval, using the shared sequencer: plays once, step 1 visible, each number selectable, reduced-motion and no-JS show everything. The working sheet fills one row per step. That removes the repeated discovery call and three of the four sheet variants.
  • A three-step hero. "We talk → We plan → We do the work and review it monthly", the same pattern as the LG and CO heroes, with the existing working sheet as the visual.
  • Interactive prioritisation (keep and sharpen). The sort is the page's most distinctive element and it works. Options: (a) keep it as is; (b) let each card be selected to show its "why" before sorting, which adds a click target but no new library; (c) avoid a scoring or quiz mechanic, because the caption already says this is not a scoring system.
  • The monthly review as Starting point → What changed → What's still uncertain. Three steps instead of four tiles, a ledger and a callout. This echoes the approved Improve → Measure → Review.
  • Limits as one compact list. Merge "not promised" (6), "gaps" (6), "measurement gap" and "unknowns" (4) into about five plain-language items under one heading. Move the dated self-evidence items (keyword tool, own analytics, AI round) to /faq or /ai-seo.
  • "What happens next" in the close. Reuse the CO-D09 pattern with DEC-051 facts only.
