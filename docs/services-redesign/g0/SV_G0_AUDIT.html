/services: G0 audit and recommendation

Read-only review of the live page (https://mappedskills.com/services, HTTP 200) and the origin/main source (components/cplus/services/). Nothing implemented, committed or deployed. Measured at 1440×900, 820×1180 and 390×844 in Chromium (playwright-core). Brand basis: MappedSkills Brand DNA v1.0 (18-page PDF) and the released C+ pages, with /how-it-works (DEC-058) as the nearest visual reference.

Recommendation in one paragraph. Keep the page's best idea, which is starting from the visitor's problem, and fix the rest. The live page lists the six services three times (hero plate, router, journey), uses two competing labelling systems, and spends 1,685 px (desktop) or 2,221 px (mobile) on 14 bordered boxes. The proposed five-section direction is sound, with one change: merge the problem selector and the six service cards into one section. As two sections they would repeat the same six links again. Tell the whole page as one visual story, "the route": a single drawn path from search to enquiry to report, which the hero shows, the selector lights up where it breaks, and the journey plays once.

2. Main weaknesses

Visual and UI

  • False affordance in the hero. The plate's eight chips (SEO, Google Ads …) are styled like buttons and are not clickable. On mobile the plate adds about 600 px before the visitor reaches anything they can use.
  • A card grid, not a composition. Section 2 is 14 identically bordered boxes with uneven heights. The Brand DNA (p.11) asks for "asymmetry, editorial offsets and meaningful spatial contrast over repetitive card grids".
  • The same list three times. The hero plate, the router and the journey each restate the services or stages, each with a different visual language.
  • Section 4 reads as fine print. Two side-by-side H2s in small type. The content (scope and exclusions) is useful; the treatment buries it.
  • The journey's active text looks faded on ink in the capture ("See where enquiries come from…"). Contrast to be measured in G1.

UX and interaction

  • Two taxonomies at once. Each problem row carries a tag (Reach, Enquiries, Tracking, Overview) and a stage (Capture, Convert, Measure, Connect). "Connect" is a fourth stage that the Brand DNA does not have.
  • A dead-end row. "Your marketing runs in separate pieces…" links nowhere; its only content is "The next section explains how the pieces fit together."
  • Journey timing is out of line with the released pages: STEP_MS = 650 in Journey.tsx, against 1.5 s on /ai-seo, /conversion-optimization and /how-it-works (2 s on /lead-generation).
  • The journey is four steps (discovered, visit, enquiry, measure), which blurs the Capture → Convert → Measure system it is meant to explain.

Copy

  • The H1 and the section 3 H2 say the same thing ("works together"), and neither names a customer problem. "Better results start when…" is generic agency language.
  • Several service lines lead with a caveat rather than what the visitor gets: Paid social "An optional source…", AI search "…nobody can guarantee it". The honesty belongs on the page, but not as each card's first impression.
  • One long dash in the rendered copy ("tracking—within the agreed scope"), against the owner's no-long-dash rule applied on other pages.

Conversion

  • No secondary "Book a call" route in the body, unlike /how-it-works and /lead-generation.
  • No "What happens next" in the close (present on /conversion-optimization and /how-it-works).
  • The useful prompt "Not sure which?" sits in a dashed box at the bottom of the longest section and sends visitors to /how-it-works, not to /contact.

5. Recommended page structure

1 Hero (cream). Kicker "Our services". H1 "Get found. Get enquiries. See what's working." One short lede. Contact CTA + Book a call. Right: the route drawing, still (Search → Your page → Enquiry → Report), labelled "Illustration".
2 "What's not working?" (paper). Three native buttons: "Not enough people find us" · "People visit, but few get in touch" · "We can't tell what's working". Below, six cards grouped Capture (4) · Convert (2), plus a Measure line linking to /how-it-works. Choosing a problem highlights its cards and its point on a mini route. A fourth option, "Not sure", points to the contact CTA.
3 "One enquiry, start to finish" (ink). The route plays once at 1.5 s per stage: Capture (someone searches and finds you) → Convert (they visit and send an enquiry) → Measure (it shows up in your monthly report, with its source where tracking allows). Example business, labelled "Illustrative example".
4 "What we do, and don't" (cream strip). Start with what you need · add later if needed · six exclusions as one line of chips · link to pricing.
5 Close (tangerine). Two-colour heading, "What happens next" row, Contact primary, Book a call secondary.

Estimate (WORKING HYPOTHESIS until a G1 mockup is measured): roughly 300 to 350 visible words against 563 today, and a shorter page at every width, mainly from removing the hero plate and the 14 boxes.

6. One visual storytelling concept: "the route"

One drawn path, used three times, so the page explains itself without paragraphs. It is the Brand DNA's own idea ("show the path from demand to action") and visually distinct from the step cards on /how-it-works.

  • Calm, event, calm. One play per visit, no loop, no sticky or pinned scrolling, step 1 visible from the start.
  • Tangerine marks the event only: the selected break point, the enquiry station, the CTAs.
  • Honest geometry. No numbers, bars or dashboard shapes; "Illustration" and "Illustrative example" labels.
  • Responsive by design. Horizontal at ≥1081 px, vertical from 761 to 1080 px and below (Brand DNA breakpoints), not a shrunk desktop.
  • Motion. About 150 ms for hover, press and selection; 1.5 s between journey stages; reduced motion and no-JavaScript show the completed route and all cards.
